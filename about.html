About this site

This site previously hosted "Sheldon's Theory of Unified Resistance" (STUR), a self-published theory-of-everything project: a manuscript, dozens of markdown derivation documents, over a hundred interactive HTML pages, and several dozen Python verification scripts, developed across many iterations up to a "v7.0" revision.

What happened

The manuscript and every downstream document in the repository were put through an explicit peer-review pass, run in stages:

  1. The core manuscript was reviewed first. Its central unification claim rests on a "geometric dual tensor" that is invoked throughout the action and field equations but is never actually defined anywhere in the text — it is listed as an open question even in the chapter titled "proof." The claimed four independent derivations of each force law (geometric, modular-operator, entropic, variational) are asserted by symbolic analogy, not carried out. The one place a concrete construction is attempted (unifying the four force sectors via a tensor product of operator algebras) provably makes the sectors mathematically decoupled — the opposite of unification.
  2. The rest of the repository (64 markdown documents, 123 interactive pages, 33 Python scripts) was then reviewed in parallel batches against the same standard: is the math internally consistent and well-typed, is its validity independent of the undefined core object, and are its numerical "predictions" actually derived rather than reverse-fit to known experimental values.

What that review found

The overwhelming pattern, consistent across every batch: quantitative "predictions" (particle masses, mixing angles, the cosmological constant, dark matter density, superconducting transition temperatures, and more) depended on chains of 2–5 multiplicative "correction factors" tuned after the fact to match the known experimental answer. A striking number of the documents contained their own prior self-audit notes admitting exactly this — phrases like "calibrated, not derived," "a target value, not a derived result," or "chosen specifically because it reproduces the known answer" appear throughout the repository in the documents' own words. Applications of the framework to biology, geology, materials science, artificial intelligence, and consciousness were speculative extensions with no independent validation.

What survived

Four pieces of content did not depend on the undefined core object and did not involve free parameters tuned to a known answer:

ItemWhy it survived
Maxwell's equations Standard U(1) gauge theory, no free parameters
Domain wall energy bound Standard cosmology (Zel'dovich–Kobzarev–Okun 1974)
Zeta-regularization on a Z₃ orbifold Standard QFT technique, cross-checked two ways, no tunable inputs
Dimensional-constant argument Standard Buckingham-π dimensional analysis

Each has been rewritten to remove framework-specific branding and terminology, since none of their validity ever depended on it — they are standard physics and mathematics that happened to be written down inside this repository.

A note on honesty in the deleted material

It's worth saying plainly: a meaningful fraction of the removed content was itself unusually self-critical for material of this kind. Several Python scripts correctly derived that specific "correction factors" used elsewhere in the framework should equal 1 (i.e. no correction at all), directly contradicting the fitted values other scripts kept using. Several pages honestly reported large discrepancies between predictions and observation rather than concealing them. That intellectual honesty doesn't change the outcome — a script that correctly shows a theory's prediction fails is not itself "resistance physics that holds up" — but it's a genuinely different failure mode than the majority of the material, which asserted agreement that wasn't there.
