RUNG 2  The strong force: chiral symmetry breaking (MODEL: linear sigma model)
==============================================================================
   In plain words: when a symmetry of the strong force breaks, light particles called
   pions appear. Exchanging pions between protons and neutrons is what holds atomic nuclei
   together. Some checks here use a simplified stand-in for the strong force, and they are
   marked [model].

   [PASS] [math] [model]  massless quarks: the vacuum breaks O(4) -> O(3), leaving exactly 3 Goldstone pions (6 - 3)
   [PASS] [math] [model]  quark masses: m_pi^2 = H/<sigma>, f_pi = <sigma>, so m_pi^2 f_pi^2 = H f_pi (the GMOR form)
   GMOR: m_pi = sqrt((m_u + m_d)|<qbar q>|)/f_pi = 127.6 MeV; measured 135.0 MeV (-5.5%, higher-order chiral terms)
   [PASS] [data]          GMOR with measured quark masses, f_pi and the lattice condensate: m_pi = 127.6 MeV (within 8% of 135)
          got 127.6 +- 3.6 MeV (inputs, 2.9%) vs 135.0 MeV. Tolerance 8%: GMOR is leading
          order with f_pi standing in for the chiral-limit F; the next check does it at
          O(p^4)
   O(p^4) chiral perturbation theory, FLAG inputs: m_pi = 132.6 +- 3.8 MeV
   [PASS] [data]          the pion mass at O(p^4) in chiral perturbation theory: 132.6 MeV, within 2 sigma of 135.0
