P4  Small splits: the minimum heating of matter (FOLLOWS)
==============================================================================
   Every nucleus at its own floor (local coupling, g = 1; nuclei as uniform spheres):
     diamond : 0.51 W/kg of internal heating
     silica  : 0.31 W/kg of internal heating
   Smearing at the entanglement cutoff, R0 = a = 5.0e-36 m, changes nothing: the nuclei are
   already ~1e-15 m across (diamond 0.51 W/kg). The point-nucleus formula
   hbar G / (4 sqrt(pi) R0^3) of src/stur_thermo_gravity.py Q3 holds only for R0 >> 1 fm;
   its 1e60 W/kg at R0 = a is an artefact of point nuclei. The real figure is ~0.5 W/kg.
   Earth's internal heat flow is 7.9e-12 W/kg (LIT recalled): local continuous CQ
   gravity is excluded by a factor 4e+10. It needs a smearing R0; the radiation bound
   already demands R0 >= 0.54 Angstrom (consistent with Oppenheim et al.'s own remark that
   continuous models with noise everywhere are ruled out).

   Centre-of-mass heating floor, in phonons/s (trap 50 kHz, ASSUMPTION):

    0.00e+00    1.021e+02                      1.021e+02

==============================================================================
P5  Any classical-quantum gravity, any kernel, continuous or discrete (STUR)
==============================================================================
   The trade-off (their eq. 29) is an operator inequality, D0 >= S_J^-1 / 4, for every CQ model
   with Newtonian back-reaction on average, including jump models whose noise sits only where
   mass is. Cauchy-Schwarz in the S_J inner product then gives, with no kernel assumed:
        Gamma(split) * S_a  >=  (1/8) |<Delta m, w>|^2  =  (1/8) (F_LR / M)^2
   F_LR is the Newtonian pull of one branch's mass on the other's. Checks: random matrices,
   min ratio lhs/rhs = 150.593 (>= 1); saturating case = 1.000000.
   Uniform spheres, F_LR / (G M^2/R^2):  split/R = 0.25, 0.5, 1, 1.5, 2, 5
     closed form: 0.2150, 0.3613, 0.4688, 0.3926, 0.2500, 0.0400
     Fourier    : 0.2150, 0.3613, 0.4688, 0.3926, 0.2500, 0.0400
   The pull is largest at split = R (0.46875 G M^2/R^2): the best test is a SMALL split.
   Design-A diamond, Gamma <= 0.12 /s:
     split         bound (m^2/s^4)   CQ acceleration noise needed (one-sided, m s^-2/rtHz)
     R = 1.0 um    2.677e-26         6.68e-13   (5.1e+04 x design A's requirement)
     2R            7.615e-27         3.56e-13   (2.7e+04 x design A's requirement)
     721 um        4.636e-37         2.78e-18   (2.1e-01 x design A's requirement)
   At 721 um the kernel-free bound is 5e-12 of the translation-invariant one (P3);
   at split = R it is 0.40 of P3 at the same split. So one diamond held coherent across about its radius,
   with its acceleration noise measured, tests every Markovian CQ gravity, discrete ones included.
   Diamond density, split = R, Gamma <= 0.1 /s:
     mass (kg)    R (um)    acceleration noise CQ gravity must produce (one-sided)
        1e-17     0.088    6.39e-14 m s^-2/rtHz
        1e-15     0.408    2.97e-13 m s^-2/rtHz
        1e-14     0.880    6.39e-13 m s^-2/rtHz
        1e-12     4.083    2.97e-12 m s^-2/rtHz
   For small splits (dx << R) the same bound becomes a heating floor of 2 pi G rho/(3 omega)
   phonons/s, 1.6e-12 at 50 kHz: unmeasurable. The test needs a split of order R.
