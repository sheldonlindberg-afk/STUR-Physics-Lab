1. Beta Functions in STUR

1.1 Definition

The beta function describes how a coupling g evolves with energy scale μ:

\[\beta(g) \equiv \mu \frac{dg}{d\mu} = \frac{dg}{d\ln\mu}\]
(1.1)

1.2 One-Loop Beta Functions

For a non-Abelian gauge theory with gauge group G:

\[\boxed{\beta(g) = -\frac{g^3}{16\pi^2}\left[\frac{11}{3}C_2(G) - \frac{4}{3}\sum_f T(r_f) - \frac{1}{6}\sum_s T(r_s)\right]}\]
(1.2) — One-loop beta function

where:

  • C2(G) = quadratic Casimir of adjoint (N for SU(N))
  • T(rf) = Dynkin index of fermion representation
  • T(rs) = Dynkin index of scalar representation

2. Asymptotic Freedom of QCD

2.1 Derivation from STUR

For SU(3)C in STUR with the derived matter content (3 generations of quarks):

\[b_3 = \frac{11}{3} \cdot 3 - \frac{4}{3} \cdot n_f \cdot \frac{1}{2} = 11 - \frac{2}{3}n_f\]
(2.1)

For nf = 6 (u, d, c, s, t, b):

\[\boxed{b_3 = 11 - \frac{2}{3} \cdot 6 = 11 - 4 = 7 > 0 \quad \Rightarrow \quad \beta_3 = -\frac{7g_3^3}{16\pi^2} < 0}\]
(2.2) — QCD is asymptotically free
Why This Works in STUR

STUR derives exactly 3 generations from MHP + TFP. With ngen = 3:

  • nf = 6 quarks < 16.5 (maximum for AF)
  • β3 < 0 automatically satisfied
  • Asymptotic freedom is a consequence of the generation count derivation

2.2 Physical Interpretation

The running coupling:

\[\boxed{\alpha_s(\mu) = \frac{\alpha_s(M_Z)}{1 + \frac{7\alpha_s(M_Z)}{2\pi}\ln(\mu/M_Z)}}\]
(2.3) — Running strong coupling
  • At low energy: αs → large (confinement)
  • At high energy: αs → 0 (asymptotic freedom)

4. Kaluza-Klein Threshold Corrections

4.1 Above MKK

Above the KK scale, the tower of KK modes contributes to the running:

\[\beta_i^{\rm 5D} = \beta_i^{\rm 4D} + \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \delta\beta_i^{(n)}\]
(4.1)

The sum over KK modes modifies the running:

\[\boxed{\alpha_i^{-1}(\mu) = \alpha_i^{-1}(M_{\rm KK}) - \frac{b_i^{\rm 5D}}{2\pi}\left[\mu L_X - 1\right] \quad (\mu > M_{\rm KK})}\]
(4.2) — Power-law running in 5D

The running becomes power-law rather than logarithmic above MKK.

4.2 Holonomy Regulation

In STUR, the holonomy weight regulates the UV:

\[\alpha_i(\mu) \to \alpha_i^* \quad \text{as } \mu \to \infty\]
(4.3)

where αi* is the MHP-determined fixed point value.

Result: No Landau Pole

The holonomy regulation prevents α1 from growing without bound. U(1) remains perturbative to all scales.
