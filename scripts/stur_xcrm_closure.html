2.5 Derivation of V(R) from Orbifold Topology

First Principles Statement

The double-well potential \(\Pot{V(R)} = \frac{\Pot{\lambda}}{4}(\Diff{R}^2 - \Pot{v}^2)^2\) is not assumed — it is uniquely determined by the \(\XCRM{S^1/\mathbb{Z}_2}\) orbifold topology. This section proves that the manifold structure forces this functional form.

2.5.1 The Z₂ Parity Assignment

On the orbifold \(\XCRM{\Mcal^5 = M^4 \times S^1/\mathbb{Z}_2}\), the identification \(\XCRM{X \sim -X}\) creates fixed points at \(\XCRM{X = 0}\) and \(\XCRM{X = L_X}\). Fields must transform definitely under this \(\XCRM{\mathbb{Z}_2}\):

\[\Diff{R}(x, -\XCRM{X}) = \Pot{\eta_R} \, \Diff{R}(x, \XCRM{X}), \qquad \Pot{\eta_R} = \pm 1\]
(2.10) — Z₂ parity transformation
Physical Requirement: ηᵣ = −1 (Z₂-odd)

The resistance scalar \(\Diff{R}\) must be \(\XCRM{\mathbb{Z}_2}\)-odd for two essential reasons:

  1. Chiral fermion localization: Yukawa couplings \(\Quant{y}\Diff{R}\Matt{\bar\Psi\Psi}\) require \(\Diff{R}\) to change sign across the orbifold to generate chiral zero modes with opposite chiralities at the two fixed points.
  2. Domain wall physics: The resistance field must interpolate between distinct vacuum states to create the brane structure of the theory.

2.5.2 Boundary Conditions at Fixed Points

The \(\XCRM{\mathbb{Z}_2}\)-odd assignment has immediate consequences at the orbifold fixed points:

\[ \begin{aligned} \text{At } \XCRM{X} = 0: &\quad \Diff{R}(x, 0) = \Diff{R}(x, -0) = -\Diff{R}(x, 0) \;\;\Rightarrow\;\; \boxed{\Diff{R}(x, 0) = 0} \\[0.5em] \text{At } \XCRM{X} = \XCRM{L_X}: &\quad \Diff{R}(x, \XCRM{L_X}) = -\Diff{R}(x, -\XCRM{L_X}) = -\Diff{R}(x, \XCRM{L_X}) \;\;\Rightarrow\;\; \boxed{\Diff{R}(x, \XCRM{L_X}) = 0} \end{aligned} \]
(2.11) — Orbifold boundary conditions
Topological Constraint

The scalar field is forced to vanish at both orbifold boundaries. This is not a choice — it is a mathematical consequence of the \(\XCRM{\mathbb{Z}_2}\)-odd parity on \(\XCRM{S^1/\mathbb{Z}_2}\).

2.5.3 Action Symmetry Constraints

The action \(\Pot{S}[\Diff{R}]\) must be invariant under the orbifold identification. Since the integration measure and metric are \(\XCRM{\mathbb{Z}_2}\)-even, the potential term must satisfy:

\[\int_{\XCRM{\Mcal^5}} d^5x\, \sqrt{-\Tor{g}}\, \Pot{V}(\Diff{R}) = \int_{\XCRM{\Mcal^5}} d^5x\, \sqrt{-\Tor{g}}\, \Pot{V}(-\Diff{R}) \quad\Rightarrow\quad \boxed{\Pot{V}(\Diff{R}) = \Pot{V}(-\Diff{R})}\]
(2.12) — Action invariance requires even potential

This eliminates all odd powers of \(\Diff{R}\) from the potential. The most general form is:

\[\Pot{V}(\Diff{R}) = a_0 + a_2 \Diff{R}^2 + a_4 \Diff{R}^4 + a_6 \Diff{R}^6 + \cdots\]
(2.13)

2.5.4 Five-Dimensional Renormalizability

Power Counting in 5D

In five dimensions, the scalar field has mass dimension \([\Diff{R}] = M^{3/2}\). The Lagrangian density has dimension \([M^5]\). This constrains the allowed potential terms:

  • \([\Diff{R}^2] = M^3\) — marginal (renormalizable)
  • \([\Diff{R}^4] = M^6 \Rightarrow [\Pot{\lambda} \Diff{R}^4] = M^5\) requires \([\Pot{\lambda}] = M^{-1}\) — marginal
  • \([\Diff{R}^6] = M^9\) — irrelevant (non-renormalizable)

For a UV-complete theory, we truncate at the marginal operators:

\[\Pot{V}(\Diff{R}) = \frac{m^2}{2} \Diff{R}^2 + \frac{\Pot{\lambda}}{4} \Diff{R}^4 + \text{const}\]
(2.14) — Most general renormalizable even potential

2.5.5 Topological Stability: The Kink Requirement

The boundary conditions (2.11) require \(\Diff{R} = 0\) at both fixed points. For the theory to have non-trivial dynamics in the bulk, the potential must allow \(\Diff{R} \neq 0\) in the interior.

Theorem 2.1 — Degenerate Minima are Forced

For a stable kink profile interpolating between the orbifold boundaries while passing through a non-zero bulk value, the potential \(\Pot{V}(\Diff{R})\) must have degenerate minima at \(\Diff{R} = \pm \Pot{v} \neq 0\).

Proof: If \(\Pot{V}(\Diff{R})\) had a unique minimum at \(\Diff{R} = 0\), the boundary conditions \(\Diff{R}(0) = \Diff{R}(\XCRM{L_X}) = 0\) would be satisfied by the trivial solution \(\Diff{R} \equiv 0\) everywhere. Non-trivial physics requires the field to "roll" through \(\Diff{R} \neq 0\) in the bulk.

For degenerate minima at \(\Diff{R} = \pm \Pot{v}\), applying \(\Pot{V'}(\Diff{R}) = m^2 \Diff{R} + \Pot{\lambda} \Diff{R}^3 = 0\):

\[m^2 < 0 \quad\Rightarrow\quad m^2 \equiv -\Pot{\lambda} \Pot{v}^2, \quad \Pot{v}^2 = -\frac{m^2}{\Pot{\lambda}}\]
(2.15) — Symmetry breaking condition

2.5.6 The Unique Potential Form

Substituting (2.15) into (2.14) and dropping the irrelevant constant:

\[\boxed{\color{#f472b6}{V(R)} = \frac{\color{#fbbf24}{\lambda}}{4}\left(\color{#4ade80}{R}^2 - \color{#fbbf24}{v}^2\right)^2}\]
(2.16) — Double-well potential from orbifold topology
Theorem 2.2 — Uniqueness of V(R)

The double-well potential (2.16) is the unique renormalizable potential compatible with:

  1. \(\XCRM{\mathbb{Z}_2}\) orbifold symmetry (even in \(\Diff{R}\))
  2. Five-dimensional power counting (polynomial degree ≤ 4)
  3. Topological stability (degenerate non-zero minima)
  4. Orbifold boundary conditions (\(\Diff{R} = 0\) at fixed points)

The manifold forces this form — it is not a phenomenological assumption.

2.5.7 The Kink Solution

The static field equation \(\XCRM{\partial_X}^2 \Diff{R} = \Pot{V'}(\Diff{R}) = \Pot{\lambda} \Diff{R}(\Diff{R}^2 - \Pot{v}^2)\) admits the kink solution:

\[\color{#4ade80}{R_0(X)} = v \tanh\!\left(\frac{X - X_c}{\color{#fbbf24}{\xi}}\right), \qquad \color{#fbbf24}{\xi} = \frac{1}{v}\sqrt{\frac{2}{\lambda}}\]
(2.17) — Kink profile

where \(\XCRM{\xi}\) is the domain wall width and \(\XCRM{X_c}\) is the kink center (typically \(\XCRM{X_c} = \XCRM{L_X}/2\)).

Physical Interpretation

The kink (2.17) is a domain wall interpolating between the two degenerate vacua \(\Diff{R} = \pm \Pot{v}\). Fermions localize at the domain wall center (where \(\Diff{R} = 0\)), acquiring mass through the gradient \(\XCRM{\partial_X} \Diff{R_0}\). This is the geometric origin of chiral localization.

Derivation Chain Summary

\[\boxed{\color{#22d3ee}{\ell_{\rm coh}} = \frac{\sqrt{2}\, \color{#fbbf24}{L_X}}{\color{#4ade80}{\sigma_R}}}\]
XCRM Parameter Closure
