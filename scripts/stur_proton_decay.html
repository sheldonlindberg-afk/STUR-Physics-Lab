\[\boxed{P_{\rm KK}[\phi_n] = (-1)^n \cdot \omega^n \quad \text{where } \omega = e^{2\pi i/3}}\]

Why KK-Parity Forbids Dangerous Operators Advanced

On the Z3 helix S1/Z3, fields satisfy twisted boundary conditions:

\[\phi(x, X + L_X) = \omega \cdot \phi(x, X)\]

The KK expansion becomes:

\[\phi(x, X) = \sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty} \phi_n(x) \exp\left(i \frac{(3n+1) \cdot 2\pi X}{3L_X}\right)\]

Conservation law: At every vertex, KK numbers (mod 3) must sum to zero.

Result: Single KK-mode exchange is forbidden for B-violating processes where initial and final states are both SM particles (n = 0 sector).
