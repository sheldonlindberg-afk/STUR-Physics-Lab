1. Why Proton Decay Matters

1.1 The Challenge for 5D Theories

In many extra-dimensional theories, Kaluza-Klein modes can mediate proton decay through dimension-6 operators:

\[\mathcal{O}_6 = \frac{1}{M_{\rm KK}^2} (qqq\ell)\]
(1.1) — Generic B-violating operator

This would give a proton lifetime:

\[\tau_p \sim \frac{M_{\rm KK}^4}{m_p^5} \sim 10^{30}\, \text{years} \quad \text{for } M_{\rm KK} \sim 1\, \text{TeV}\]
(1.2) — Naive estimate (RULED OUT)

The experimental bound τp > 1034 years would require MKK > 1016 GeV, which would make STUR untestable.

1.2 STUR's Resolution

KK-Parity Forbids Proton Decay

The Z2 Z₃ helix symmetry in STUR generates a KK-parity PKK = (-1)n, where n is the KK level. This parity is exactly conserved, forbidding single KK-mode exchange contributions to proton decay.

2. KK-Parity from Orbifold Geometry

On the Z₃ helix S1/Z2, fields have definite parity under X → -X:

\[\phi(x, -X) = \pm \phi(x, X)\]

\[\phi^{(+)}(x, X) = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \phi_n(x) \cos\left(\frac{n\pi X}{L_X}\right), \quad \phi^{(-)}(x, X) = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \tilde{\phi}_n(x) \sin\left(\frac{n\pi X}{L_X}\right)\]

3. Why Proton Decay Is Forbidden

3.1 KK-Parity Analysis of Proton Decay

Consider the proton decay process p → e+ + π0:

  • Initial state: p (contains u, u, d quarks) → PKK = (+1)(+1)(+1) = +1
  • Final state: e+ + π0 → PKK = (+1)(+1) = +1

KK-parity is conserved in the external states. But the intermediate state must also conserve KK-parity.

3.2 Single KK-Mode Exchange

For proton decay via single KK-mode exchange:

\[\text{Amplitude} \propto \frac{g^2}{M_{\rm KK}^2} \langle e^+ \pi^0 | \phi^{(1)} | p \rangle\]
(3.1)

But this violates KK-parity:

\[\underbrace{(+1)}_{\rm initial} \to \underbrace{(-1)}_{\phi^{(1)}} \to \underbrace{(+1)}_{\rm final} \quad \text{FORBIDDEN!}\]
(3.2)
Result: No Single KK-Mode Contribution

KK-parity conservation forbids proton decay through single KK-mode exchange. The leading contribution comes from two KK-mode exchange.

3.3 Two KK-Mode Exchange (Allowed but Suppressed)

The leading allowed process involves two KK-modes:

\[\boxed{\Gamma(p \to e^+ \pi^0) \propto \frac{g^4}{M_{\rm KK}^4} \cdot (\text{phase space}) \cdot (\text{matrix elements})}\]
(3.3) — Two-KK exchange rate

This gives dimension-8 operators (not dimension-6), which are suppressed by an additional factor of MKK2.

(PD.5.1) — Dimension-8 B-violating operator

The loop factor 1/(16π²) arises from the two-propagator diagram.

The proton decay amplitude requires:

\[\langle e^+ \pi^0 | (uud)_{\rm proton} \rangle = \alpha_H \cdot f_\pi \cdot m_p^2\]

\[\alpha_H = 0.0112 \pm 0.0012\;\text{GeV}^3, \quad f_\pi = 130.4\;\text{MeV}\]

The partial width for p → e+π0 is:

\[\boxed{\Gamma(p \to e^+ \pi^0) = \frac{m_p}{32\pi}\left(1 - \frac{m_\pi^2}{m_p^2}\right)^2 \left|\mathcal{M}\right|^2}\]
(PD.5.3) — Decay width formula

where the matrix element is:

\[\left|\mathcal{M}\right|^2 = \frac{g^8}{(16\pi^2)^2 M_{\rm KK}^8} \cdot \alpha_H^2 \cdot v^4\]
