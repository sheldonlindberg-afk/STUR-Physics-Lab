1. The Ultraviolet Problem in QFT

1.1 Standard QFT Divergences

In conventional quantum field theory, loop integrals diverge at high momenta. The divergences are classified by their degree of severity:

\[\Quant{\text{Quadratic:}} \quad \int^\Lambda \frac{d^4k}{(2\pi)^4} \frac{1}{k^2 - m^2} \sim \Lambda^2\]
(1.1a) — Quadratic divergence (scalar mass corrections)
\[\Quant{\text{Quartic:}} \quad \int^\Lambda \frac{d^4k}{(2\pi)^4} \frac{k^2}{(k^2 - m^2)^2} \sim \Lambda^4\]
(1.1b) — Quartic divergence (vacuum energy)
\[\Quant{\text{Logarithmic:}} \quad \int^\Lambda \frac{d^4k}{(2\pi)^4} \frac{1}{(k^2 - m^2)^2} \sim \ln\Lambda\]
(1.1c) — Logarithmic divergence (charge renormalization)

These divergences create severe theoretical problems:

  • Hierarchy problem: Quadratic divergences destabilize the Higgs mass, requiring fine-tuning to 1 part in 1032
  • Cosmological constant problem: Quartic divergences predict \(\rho_\Lambda \sim M_{\rm Pl}^4\), off by 120 orders of magnitude
  • Regularization dependence: Physical predictions require arbitrary cutoff schemes
  • UV completion: New physics at high energies (strings, asymptotic safety, etc.) must be postulated
