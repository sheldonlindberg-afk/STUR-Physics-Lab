Core Concepts

STUR — Sheldon's Theory of Unified Resistance

A unified physics framework proposing that fundamental physics emerges from a single resistance field \(\XCRM{R(x^\mu, X)}\) defined on a 5D orbifold manifold \(M_4 \times S^1/\mathbb{Z}_2\). The theory derives gravity (via TEGR), gauge forces, matter, and cosmology from XCRM foundation (χR∂ₓR → Master Action → DHP → MHP; all derived from path integral).

See: Core Theory

XCRM — X-Coupled Resistance Manifold

The central mechanism of STUR. The resistance field \(\XCRM{R}\) couples to an internal coordinate \(\XCRM{X}\) via the term \(\XCRM{\chi R \partial_X R}\) in the master action. This coupling:

  • Generates the resistance holonomy observable
  • Closes the parameter space (only \(\XCRM{L_X}\) is free)
  • Produces the Gaussian visibility prediction

See: XCRM Closure

TEGR — Teleparallel Equivalent of General Relativity

A reformulation of General Relativity using torsion instead of curvature. In TEGR, the fundamental object is the vierbein (frame field) \(\Tor{e^a_\mu}\), and gravity is encoded in the torsion tensor rather than the Riemann curvature. TEGR and GR are dynamically equivalent but conceptually different.

STUR claim: TEGR emerges as the equilibrium limit of XCRM dynamics when \(\Diff{R \to R_{\rm bg}}\).

See: Gravity Emergence

Plain English:

An orbifold is like a circle that's been folded in half, creating a line segment with two special boundary points (shown in gold). These boundaries are called "fixed points" because they don't move when you fold. The "Z₂" means the fold identifies opposite points: if you walk one way around the original circle, you end up at the same spot as walking the other way.

Why This Matters for Physics:
  • The boundary conditions at X = 0 and X = L_X force the potential V(R) to have a specific double-well shape
  • Matter (fermions) gets trapped at the boundaries—this is where we live in 4D
  • The winding number around this shape determines how many particle generations exist (answer: 3)

Size: L_X ~ 0.1-10 μm (about the size of a bacterium to a human cell—tiny, but not Planck-scale tiny)

The fundamental dynamical variable in STUR. A real scalar field \(\XCRM{R(x^\mu, X)}\) defined on the 5D orbifold, representing "resistance to change" in the fabric of spacetime. The R-field:

What is "Visibility"? (Novice Explanation)

STUR's main prediction involves visibility decay in interferometers. Here's what that means, explained from scratch.

What is an Interferometer?

An interferometer splits a beam (of light or atoms) into two paths, sends them different ways, then recombines them. When the beams meet again, they interfere—creating a pattern of bright and dark stripes called fringes.

The pattern depends on the path difference (ΔL)—how much longer one path is than the other. Change ΔL, and the fringes shift.

What is "Visibility"?

Visibility measures how clear the fringes are.

  • V = 100%: Perfect fringes—bright stripes are maximally bright, dark stripes are completely dark
  • V = 50%: Faded fringes—you can still see the pattern, but it's less distinct
  • V = 0%: No fringes—just uniform gray, pattern completely washed out

What STUR Predicts

Standard physics says visibility should stay near 100% (assuming a good apparatus). STUR predicts visibility decreases as path difference increases—and in a very specific way:

V(ΔL) = V₀ · e−ΔL²/ℓ²
  • Gaussian — the decay is smooth and bell-curve-shaped (ΔL² in the exponent)
  • No oscillations — just steady decay, no wiggles
  • No time dependence — same pattern whether you wait 1 second or 1 hour
  • Coherence length ℓ — the scale where visibility drops to ~37% (estimated 0.3–30 m)

Everyday scale: The coherence length (0.3–30 m) is about the length of a car to a small building. This is measurable with current technology!
