1. Central Postulate

1.1 Statement

\[\boxed{\text{Observable interaction is a calibrated boundary flux of resistance on }\mathcal{M}^5.}\]

1.2 Mathematical Formulation

Let \(\Sigma\) be a measurement boundary with unit normal \(n^A\). The observable associated with resistance component \(R\) is:

\[\widehat{\mathcal{O}} := \frac{n^A \nabla_A R \big|_\Sigma}{\left(n^A \nabla_A R\right)_{\rm ref}}\]
(1.1)

The reference flux \((n^A \nabla_A R)_{\rm ref}\) is computed from the governing equations under specified reference conditions—not measured post-hoc.

1.3 Physical Content

The postulate asserts that:

  • All interactions manifest as flux across boundaries
  • The dynamical quantity is resistance—generalized impedance to state change
  • Observables are dimensionless ratios, eliminating absolute calibration dependence

4. Scalar Sector Dynamics

4.1 Free Energy Functional

On a spatial hypersurface \(\Sigma_t\) with induced metric \(\gamma_{ij}\):

Step 1: Vary \(F[R + \epsilon\eta]\) with test function \(\eta\) satisfying \(\eta|_{\partial\Sigma} = 0\)
Step 2: Integrate by parts: \[\int D(\nabla_i R)(\nabla^i\eta)\, d^3x = -\int D(\nabla_i\nabla^i R)\,\eta\, d^3x + \text{(boundary terms)}\]
Step 3: Extract coefficient of \(\eta\):
\[\frac{\delta F}{\delta R} = -D\nabla^2 R + V'(R) - J\]

\[\partial_t R = -M \frac{\delta F}{\delta R}\]

\[\partial_t R = MD\nabla^2 R - MV'(R) + MJ\]

The free energy monotonically decreases for \(M \geq 0\), with equality only at equilibrium.

11. Interferometric Prediction

11.1 Physical Configuration

Consider a Mach-Zehnder interferometer with path lengths \(L_1\) and \(L_2 = L_1 + \Delta L\). A quantum particle (photon, atom, neutron) with de Broglie wavelength \(\lambda\) traverses both paths coherently.

\[\Delta\phi_{\rm QM} = \frac{2\pi\Delta L}{\lambda}\]

11.3 Resistance-Modified Phase

Step 1: Coherence Evolution

In the Law of Resistance, the particle's coherence state evolves along its trajectory. In the co-moving frame, equation (4.5) reduces to:

\[\frac{dR}{dt} + \Gamma_X(R - R_{\rm bg}) = 0\]
(11.2)

with solution:

\[R(t) = R_{\rm bg} + (R_0 - R_{\rm bg})e^{-\Gamma_X t}\]
(11.3)

Step 2: Phase-Resistance Coupling

The quantum phase acquires a resistance-dependent contribution:

\[\phi_R = \alpha \int_0^\tau R(t)\, dt\]
(11.4)
Origin of Phase-Resistance Coupling

From the matter action (3.5), the coupling \(yR\bar{\Psi}\Psi\) gives an effective mass \(m_{\rm eff} = yR\). The WKB phase integral is:

\[\phi = \int p\, dx = \int \sqrt{E^2 - m_{\rm eff}^2}\, dx\]

Expanding for \(yR \ll E\): \[\phi \approx \int \left(p_0 - \frac{y^2R^2}{2p_0}\right) dx\]

The leading resistance correction is: \[\Delta\phi \propto \frac{y^2}{p_0} \int R\, dt\]

Thus \(\alpha \sim y^2/p_0\), with specific values depending on particle species.

Step 3: Phase Difference Evaluation

For paths with transit times \(\tau_1 = L_1/v\) and \(\tau_2 = L_2/v\), the resistance-induced phase difference is:

\[\Delta\phi_R = \alpha \int_{\tau_1}^{\tau_2} R(t)\, dt\]

In the weak-relaxation limit \(\Gamma_X\tau \ll 1\):

\[\Delta\phi_R \approx \alpha R_0 \cdot \frac{\Delta L}{v}\]
(11.5)

Step 4: Resistance Phase Scale

Define the characteristic length:

\[\ell_R := \frac{2\pi v}{\alpha R_0}\]
(11.6 — Definition)

The resistance phase becomes:

\[\Delta\phi_R = \frac{2\pi\Delta L}{\ell_R}\]
(11.7 — STUR Phase Shift)

Case A: Deterministic \(\ell_R\)

12. Experimental Constraints

12.1 Optical Interferometry

Gravitational Wave Detectors

LIGO/Virgo/KAGRA maintain visibility \(V > 0.99\) at \(\lambda \sim 1\,\mu\)m with arm lengths \(L \sim 4\) km. The effective path difference probed is \(\Delta L \sim\) pm (strain sensitivity \(\sim 10^{-23}\)).

\[\text{Constraint: } \ell_R > 10^{12}\,\text{m}\]

High-Finesse Optical Cavities

Fabry-Pérot cavities achieve finesse \(\mathcal{F} > 10^6\), implying \(V > 0.9999\) over path differences \(\Delta L \sim 1\) m at \(\lambda = 633\) nm.

\[\text{Constraint: } \frac{\sigma_\ell}{\ell_R^2} < 10^{-6}\,\text{m}^{-1}\]

12.2 Matter-Wave Interferometry

Atom Interferometers

Cold atom interferometers (Cs, Rb) achieve path separations \(\Delta L \sim 10\) cm at de Broglie wavelength \(\lambda \sim 10^{-11}\) m with visibility \(V > 0.9\).

\[\text{Constraint: } \ell_R > 1\,\text{m} \quad \text{(atomic scale)}\]

Neutron Interferometers

Silicon perfect-crystal interferometers at ILL Grenoble achieve \(\Delta L \sim 10\) cm at \(\lambda \sim 10^{-10}\) m with \(V \sim 0.6 - 0.9\).

The reduced visibility is attributed to crystal imperfections and vibrations, not fundamental physics. Constraints are inconclusive.
