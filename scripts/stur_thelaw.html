Introduce two tetrads \((e^a{}_\mu, \tilde{e}^a{}_\mu)\) with metrics \(g_{\mu\nu}\) and \(f_{\mu\nu}\). The ghost-free interaction depends on \(S^\mu{}_\nu := (\sqrt{g^{-1}f})^\mu{}_\nu\) via:

\[ \mathcal{L}_{\text{int}} = m^2 \sum_{n=0}^{4} \beta_n e_n(S) \]

Linearization and Eigenstates

Diagonalize to mass eigenmodes:

\[ \bar{h}^{(0)}_{\mu\nu} := \cos\vartheta\, h_{\mu\nu} + \sin\vartheta\, \ell_{\mu\nu}, \qquad \bar{h}^{(m)}_{\mu\nu} := -\sin\vartheta\, h_{\mu\nu} + \cos\vartheta\, \ell_{\mu\nu} \]

Field Equations

\[ \boxed{ \begin{aligned} \Box\Phi_0 &= 4\pi G\cos\vartheta\,\rho, \quad \Box\mathbf{A}_0 = \frac{4\pi G}{c^2}\cos\vartheta\,\mathbf{J} \\ (\nabla^2 - \mu^2)\Phi_m &= 4\pi G\sin\vartheta\,\rho, \quad (\nabla^2 - \mu^2)\mathbf{A}_m = \frac{4\pi G}{c^2}\sin\vartheta\,\mathbf{J} \end{aligned} } \]

Observables and Screening

Static, spherically symmetric source \(M\) gives:

\[ \Phi_g(r) = -\frac{GM}{r}\cos\vartheta - \frac{GM}{r}\sin\vartheta\, e^{-\mu r} \]

The massive admixture yields Yukawa screening at \(r \gtrsim \mu^{-1}\).

HGEM with Mass

\[ \boxed{ k_m^2 = \frac{\omega^2}{c^2} - \mu^2 \quad \Rightarrow \quad \text{propagating if } \omega > \mu c, \text{ evanescent otherwise} } \]

B.9 Numerical HGEM: Constrained Helmholtz Formulation

Frequency-Domain PDEs

Time-harmonic fields \(e^{-i\omega t}\) with \(k = \omega/c\):

\[ (\nabla^2 + k^2)\Phi_g = 4\pi G\rho_\omega, \qquad (\nabla^2 + k^2)\mathbf{A}_g = \frac{4\pi G}{c^2}\mathbf{J}_\omega \]

with Lorenz constraint \(\nabla \cdot \mathbf{A}_g + i\frac{\omega}{c^2}\Phi_g = 0\).

Discretization Recipe

  1. Mesh \(\Omega\). Use \(H^1\) Lagrange elements for \(\Phi, \lambda\), Nédélec \(H(\text{curl})\) elements for \(\mathbf{A}\).
  2. Choose boundary partitions \(\Gamma_s, \Gamma_v\); apply Sommerfeld/Silver–Müller.
  3. Assemble the mixed system or penalty system. Scale rows to keep units balanced.
  4. Solve the complex linear system. Recover \(\mathbf{E}_g = -\nabla\Phi\), \(\mathbf{B}_g = \nabla \times \mathbf{A}\).

B.12 Variational Boundary Term and Well-Posedness

Surface Term from the TEGR Variation

A boundary contribution remains when \(\delta e^a{}_\mu \neq 0\) on \(\partial M\).

Boundary Counterterm for Well-Posed Problem

To fix \(e^a{}_\mu\) on \(\partial M\), cancel the surface variation by adding the teleparallel boundary term:

\[ S_{\partial M}[e] = -\frac{1}{\kappa}\oint_{\partial M} d^3\Sigma\, e\, T^\mu n_\mu \]

Index clarification: Here \(T^\mu = T^{\nu\mu}{}_\nu\) is the torsion trace vector (contraction of torsion tensor), and \(n_\mu\) is the unit normal covector to \(\partial M\). The product \(T^\mu n_\mu\) is a scalar (full index contraction).

Then:

\[ \boxed{ S^{\text{well-posed}}_{\text{TEGR}} = \frac{1}{2\kappa}\int_M d^4x\, e\, T - \frac{1}{\kappa}\oint_{\partial M} d^3\Sigma\, e\, T^\mu n_\mu } \]

Comparison to GHY

The Einstein–Hilbert action attains a well-posed Dirichlet problem by the Gibbons–Hawking–York term \(\frac{1}{\kappa}\oint\sqrt{|h|}\,K\). The above is the teleparallel analogue.
