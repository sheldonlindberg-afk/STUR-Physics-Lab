Core Field Equation

The Law of Resistance – one equation for motion, structure, and life.

STUR models the universe with a single resistance field $R(x,t,X)$ defined on a 5D manifold. Everything else – diffusion, drift, memory, curvature, probability, emergence – falls out of how $R$ evolves in space, time, and the hidden feedback dimension $X$.

STUR evolution law
$begin:math:display$ \\frac\{\\partial R\}\{\\partial t\} \= \\nabla \\cdot \(D \\nabla R\) \- \\nabla R \\cdot \\Omega \+ \\chi \\frac\{\\partial R\}\{\\partial X\} \+ S $end:math:display$
This is a conservation law for resistance: local changes in $R$ come from diffusion, directed flow, hidden-dimension feedback, and external sources.
Plain language summary
Every system pushes through a landscape of resistance $R$. Gradients in that landscape determine what moves, what forms, and what survives: matter, fields, code, markets, cells, and minds all follow the same rule: they flow down resistance gradients while feedback in $X$ writes memory and coherence.
Term legend
R(x,t,X): resistance field D: diffusion tensor Ω: drift / flow field χ: X-coupling strength S: sources / sinks

The hidden dimension $X$ carries feedback, adaptation, and internal state. When you drop $X$, the universe looks probabilistic. When you keep it, it becomes deterministic.

1 · Deriving the Law of Resistance

From conservation law → STUR 5D evolution equation

Start with the only assumption you really need: there is a scalar resistance field $R(x,t,X)$ defined over physical space $x$, time $t$, and a hidden feedback coordinate $X$.

Local resistance balance
$begin:math:display$ \\frac\{\\partial R\}\{\\partial t\} \+ \\nabla \\cdot J\_R \= S\. $end:math:display$

Here $J_R$ is the resistance flux and $S$ collects all sources and sinks. To turn this balance into a working PDE, you specify how $J_R$ is built from $R$.

Step 1 – Diffusion down gradients

Systems smooth out sharp spikes in resistance. This gives $begin:math:display$ J\_\{\\text\{diff\}\} \= \-D \\nabla R $end:math:display$ and therefore

$begin:math:display$ \\frac\{\\partial R\}\{\\partial t\} \= \\nabla \\cdot \(D \\nabla R\) \+ \\dots $end:math:display$

Step 2 – Drift along a flow field

Add directed transport: $begin:math:display$ J\_\{\\text\{flow\}\} \= R \\Omega $end:math:display$ which yields the drift term

$begin:math:display$ \-\\nabla R \\cdot \\Omega\. $end:math:display$

Step 3 – Hidden-dimension feedback

Promote coherence/history into a coordinate $X$:

X-coupled resistance manifold
$begin:math:display$ \\frac\{\\partial R\}\{\\partial X\} \= \\frac\{R \- R\_\{bg\}\}\{L\_X\}\. $end:math:display$

This feeds back into time evolution through $\chi \partial R/\partial X$.

Step 4 – Collect everything

Combine all pieces:

$begin:math:display$ \\frac\{\\partial R\}\{\\partial t\} \= \\nabla \\cdot \(D \\nabla R\) \- \\nabla R \\cdot \\Omega \+ \\chi \\frac\{\\partial R\}\{\\partial X\} \+ S\. $end:math:display$

2 · How to read the equation

One law, many domains

The Law of Resistance becomes mechanics, thermodynamics, information flow, evolution, or learning depending on how you choose $R$, $D$, $\Omega$, $\chi$, and $S$.

Diffusion Flow X-feedback Sources

In plasmas, $R$ tracks resistive load; in cells, $R$ is metabolic resistance and $X$ is regulatory memory; in markets, $R$ is allocation resistance.

Hidden dimension as deterministic completion

4D spacetime is $(x,t)$ where time is the fourth coordinate. STUR adds a fifth: $X$. Ignoring $X$ produces apparent randomness. Keeping it makes the system deterministic.

3 · Proof of concept: resistance smoothing in 1D

A minimal numerical experiment inside the page

Switch off flow, X-feedback, and sources to view the pure diffusion limit:

1D diffusion limit
$begin:math:display$ \\frac\{\\partial R\}\{\\partial t\} \= D \\frac\{\\partial\^2 R\}\{\\partial x\^2\}\. $end:math:display$

Below is the minimal 1D JS demo. 

import numpy as np
import matplotlib.pyplot as plt

# Grid and physical parameters
N = 128          # number of spatial points
D = 0.2          # diffusion coefficient
dx = 1.0         # spatial step
dt = 0.1         # time step (explicit Euler)
steps = 400      # number of time steps

# Spatial grid
x = np.arange(N) * dx

# Initial resistance field R(x, 0):
# sharp peak in the center (high resistance region)
R = np.zeros(N)
center = x.mean()
R[np.abs(x - center)  <  3 * dx] = 1.0

initial_R = R.copy()

# Choose some steps to snapshot the profile
snap_indices = [0, 5, 20, 100, steps - 1]
snapshots = []

# Explicit finite-difference time stepping for
# ∂R/∂t = D ∂²R/∂x²
for n in range(steps):
    # Compute discrete Laplacian with periodic neighbors via roll
    lap = (np.roll(R, -1) - 2.0 * R + np.roll(R, 1)) / (dx * dx)

    # New field after one step
    newR = R.copy()
    newR[1:-1] = R[1:-1] + dt * D * lap[1:-1]

    # Simple boundary conditions (Neumann-like: zero gradient)
    newR[0] = newR[1]
    newR[-1] = newR[-2]

    R = newR

    if n in snap_indices:
        snapshots.append(R.copy())

final_R = R.copy()

# Build a small summary "JSON-like" dictionary of the run
summary = {
    "N": N,
    "D": D,
    "dx": dx,
    "dt": dt,
    "steps": steps,
    "initial_peak": float(initial_R.max()),
    "final_peak": float(final_R.max()),
    "initial_sum": float(initial_R.sum()),
    "final_sum": float(final_R.sum())
}

print("STUR 1D diffusion demo summary:")
for key, value in summary.items():
    if isinstance(value, float):
        print(f"{key}: {value:.6f}")
    else:
        print(f"{key}: {value}")

print("\nInitial R (rounded):")
print(np.round(initial_R, 3))

print("\nFinal R (rounded):")
print(np.round(final_R, 3))

# Plot initial vs final
plt.figure(figsize=(6, 4))
plt.plot(x, initial_R, label="initial R(x, 0)")
plt.plot(x, final_R, label=f"final R(x, t={steps * dt})")
plt.xlabel("x")
plt.ylabel("R")
plt.title("STUR 1D diffusion: initial vs final")
plt.legend()
plt.tight_layout()
plt.show()

# Plot snapshots over time
plt.figure(figsize=(6, 4))
for snap, idx in zip(snapshots, snap_indices):
    plt.plot(x, snap, label=f"step {idx}")
plt.xlabel("x")
plt.ylabel("R")
plt.title("STUR 1D diffusion snapshots")
plt.legend()
plt.tight_layout()
plt.show()
