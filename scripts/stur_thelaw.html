0. Conceptual Origin

The Law of Resistance is a formalized and falsifiable expression of Sheldon's Theory of Unified Resistance (STUR), originally introduced as a resistance-based unification framework and iteratively refined through successive versions.

The present Law consolidates and rigorously constrains the framework introduced in Lindberg (2025), extending it with explicit gauge structure, teleparallel correspondence, and hard falsification criteria.

1. Formal Law

\[\boxed{\text{Observable interaction is a calibrated boundary flux of resistance on }\mathcal{M}^5.}\]

Resistance is the dynamical quantity whose gradients, fluxes, and boundary projections define measured forces, phase evolution, and geometry.

2. Manifold and Field Content

Extended manifold: \(\mathcal{M}^5 = M^4 \times X\), with \(X\) a compact coordinate carrying coherence structure.

The coordinate \(X\) is not assumed to be a conventional spacetime dimension. It functions as an internal coherence/memory coordinate whose physical interpretation depends on the sector under consideration (scalar memory flow, vector gauge redundancy, or internal degeneracy). Whether \(X\) is fundamental or emergent is left open at the level of this Law.

\[\mathcal{R}_{AB}(x^\mu, X), \qquad A,B\in\{0,1,2,3,5\}\]

Decomposition (off-shell):

  • \(\mathcal{R}_{\mu\nu}\): symmetric 4D tensor (10 components)
  • \(\mathcal{R}_{\mu5}\): 4D vector (4 components)
  • \(\mathcal{R}_{55}\): scalar (1 component)
A single scalar field cannot supply the degrees of freedom required for GR + SM. The tensor structure above is the minimal correction (15 off-shell components); on-shell counting requires explicit constraint analysis.

A2 — Dynamics

The parameter \(t\) in the scalar evolution equation is physical laboratory time. The free energy \(F\) is evaluated on a fixed-time spatial hypersurface \(\Sigma_t\), ensuring dimensional and variational consistency.

A3 — Gauge Symmetries

A5 — Falsifiability

4. Scalar-Sector Derivation

4.1 Free Energy Functional

\[F[R] = \int_{\Sigma_t} d^3x\, \sqrt{\gamma}\left(\frac{D}{2}\nabla_i R\nabla^i R + V(R) - JR\right)\]

Here \(\gamma_{ij}\) is the induced metric on the spatial slice \(\Sigma_t\), and \(\nabla_i\) is the spatial covariant derivative.

4.2 Gradient Flow

For dissipative evolution with mobility \(M > 0\):

\[\partial_t R = -M\frac{\delta F}{\delta R}\]

4.3 Functional Derivative

Varying \(F\) and integrating by parts:

\[\frac{\delta F}{\delta R} = -D\nabla_i\nabla^i R + V'(R) - J\]

4.4 Evolution Equation

\[\boxed{\partial_t R = MD\nabla_i\nabla^i R - MV'(R) + MJ}\]
This is a gradient flow with guaranteed non-negative dissipation for convex \(V\) and \(M > 0\).

This evolution equation is a dissipative effective law (Onsager gradient flow), not a conservative Euler–Lagrange field equation.

4.5 Transport Extension

With advection velocity \(u^i\) and \(X\)-transport speed \(v_X\):

\[\partial_t R + u^i\nabla_i R + v_X\partial_X R = MD\nabla_i\nabla^i R - MV'(R) + MJ\]

6. Non-Abelian Gauge Structure

Resistance Degeneracy

The tensorial resistance field admits locally degenerate internal channels:

\[\mathcal{R}_{AB}(x^\mu,X) \;\longrightarrow\; \mathcal{R}_{AB}^{\;\;I}(x^\mu,X), \qquad I=1,\dots,N\]

The index \(I\) labels indistinguishable resistance modes. Observables are invariant under local rotations in this internal space.

Local Basis Transformations

A change of local basis in the degenerate resistance space is represented by \(U(x) \in \mathrm{GL}(N)\):

\[\mathcal{R}^{I}_{AB}(x) \;\to\; U^{I}{}_{J}(x)\,\mathcal{R}^{J}_{AB}(x)\]

Emergent Gauge Connection

Transport of resistance between neighboring spacetime points requires a connection:

\[\mathcal{D}_\mu \mathcal{R}_{AB}^{\;\;I} = \partial_\mu \mathcal{R}_{AB}^{\;\;I} + g\,\mathcal{A}_{\mu}^{\;\;I}{}_{J}\, \mathcal{R}_{AB}^{\;\;J}\]

Requiring covariance under local basis transformations fixes the gauge transformation law:

\[\mathcal{A}_\mu \;\to\; U\mathcal{A}_\mu U^{-1} - \frac{1}{g}(\partial_\mu U)U^{-1}\]

Non-Abelian Curvature

The curvature is defined by parallel-transport non-commutativity:

\[\boxed{\mathcal{F}_{\mu\nu} = \partial_\mu \mathcal{A}_\nu - \partial_\nu \mathcal{A}_\mu + g\,[\mathcal{A}_\mu,\mathcal{A}_\nu]}\]

The commutator term is unavoidable when resistance channels mix.

Gauge-Field Dynamics

The minimal gauge-invariant action for the connection is:

\[S_{\rm YM} = -\frac{1}{4}\int d^4x\,\sqrt{-g}\,\mathrm{Tr}\!\left(\mathcal{F}_{\mu\nu}\mathcal{F}^{\mu\nu}\right)\]

Variation with respect to \(\mathcal{A}_\mu\) yields the Yang–Mills equations:

\[\mathcal{D}_\mu \mathcal{F}^{\mu\nu} = \mathcal{J}^\nu\]

The current \(\mathcal{J}^\nu\) represents effective resistance currents sourced by matter degrees of freedom. Its microscopic derivation and representation content are not specified at the level of this Law.

Gauge Group

The internal symmetry group is a compact Lie group \(G\) acting on the degenerate resistance channels. Common minimal choices include \(U(1)\), \(SU(2)\), and \(SU(3)\), corresponding to one-, two-, and three-channel degeneracies.

Non-abelian gauge structure emerges from resistance degeneracy and parallel transport, not as an independent postulate. The specific choice of gauge group and coupling constants remains an empirical input.
