X-Coupled Resistance Manifold (STUR)

Authors: Sheldon Lindberg · James Lockwood

This manuscript presents the unified geometric foundation of Sheldon’s Theory of Unified Resistance (STUR), formulated as a five-dimensional resistance field theory with chronomagnetic boundary closure.

The framework unifies spacetime curvature, torsion, coherence, entropy, and modular flow into a single scalar resistance field. It is constructed to be explicitly falsifiable in both laboratory and astrophysical systems.

1. Foundational Postulate

STUR begins with a single ontological statement:

\[ \textbf{All physical interaction is resistance.} \]

Resistance is defined operationally as the substrate’s opposition to deformation, transport, phase evolution, or information flow. Particles, forces, and fields are not fundamental—they are projections of resistance dynamics.

The universal resistance field is a scalar

\[ R = R(x^\mu, X), \]

defined on an extended manifold

\[ \mathcal{M}^5 = M^4 \times X. \]

The internal coordinate \(X\) is not spatial. It is a coherence and memory coordinate, required to encode hysteresis, phase history, entropy displacement, and holographic reconstruction.

\[ ds^2 = g_{\mu\nu}(x)\,dx^\mu dx^\nu + dX^2. \]
