14. Teleparallel Gravity (TEGR) Correspondence

The geometric sector of STUR admits a well-defined correspondence with the Teleparallel Equivalent of General Relativity (TEGR), in which gravity is encoded entirely through torsion rather than curvature. This section makes the correspondence explicit and fixes notation.

\[ T^{\rho}{}_{\mu\nu} = \Gamma^{\rho}{}_{\nu\mu} - \Gamma^{\rho}{}_{\mu\nu}, \qquad S_{\rho}{}^{\mu\nu} = \frac{1}{2} \left( K^{\mu\nu}{}_{\rho} + \delta^{\mu}_{\rho} T^{\alpha\nu}{}_{\alpha} - \delta^{\nu}_{\rho} T^{\alpha\mu}{}_{\alpha} \right) \]

\[ T = S_{\rho}{}^{\mu\nu} T^{\rho}{}_{\mu\nu}. \]

The TEGR action is

\[ \mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{TEGR}} = \frac{1}{2\kappa} \int d^4x \, e \, T \]

which is related to the Einstein–Hilbert action by the exact identity

\[ R(g) = -T + B, \qquad B = 2 e^{-1} \partial_{\mu}(e T^{\mu}), \]

where \(B\) is a total divergence. Thus TEGR and GR are dynamically equivalent up to a boundary term [1].

\[ T^{\rho}{}_{\mu\nu} \;\longleftrightarrow\; \partial_{[\mu} R \, \delta_{\nu]}^{\rho}, \qquad T \;\longleftrightarrow\; (\nabla R)^2. \]

The correspondence holds in a restricted TEGR-like sector, not as a complete dynamical equivalence.

  • \(S \rightarrow 0\) (no sources)

The classical gravitational sector of STUR is therefore TEGR-like in structure within this restricted sector, with curvature replaced by resistance-induced torsion.

Boundary Interpretation

Unlike standard TEGR, STUR treats the boundary as constitutive. The TEGR boundary term \(B\) is promoted to a physical holoscreen action, providing a geometric motivation for phase-gated corrections and chronomagnetic modulation. This is a boundary-term analogy, not a claim of full TEGR reduction.
