Core Field Equation

The Law of Resistance – one equation for motion, structure, and life.

STUR models the universe with a single resistance field $R(x,t,X)$ defined on a 5D manifold. Everything else – diffusion, drift, memory, curvature, probability, emergence – falls out of how $R$ evolves in space, time, and the hidden feedback dimension $X$.

STUR evolution law
\[ \frac{\partial R}{\partial t} = \nabla \cdot \bigl(D \nabla R\bigr) - \nabla R \cdot \Omega + \chi \frac{\partial R}{\partial X} + S \]
This is a conservation law for resistance: local changes in $R$ come from diffusion, directed flow, hidden-dimension feedback, and external sources.
Plain language summary
Every system pushes through a landscape of resistance $R$. Gradients in that landscape determine what moves, what forms, and what survives: matter, fields, code, markets, cells, and minds all follow the same rule: they flow down resistance gradients while feedback in $X$ writes memory and coherence.
Term legend
R(x,t,X): resistance field D: diffusion tensor Ω: drift / flow field χ: X-coupling strength S: sources / sinks

The hidden dimension $X$ carries feedback, adaptation, and internal state. When you drop $X$, the universe looks probabilistic. When you keep it, it becomes deterministic.

1 · Deriving the Law of Resistance

From conservation law → STUR 5D evolution equation

Start with the only assumption you really need: there is a scalar resistance field $R(x,t,X)$ defined over physical space $x$, time $t$, and a hidden feedback coordinate $X$. Locally, $R$ can move around, be transported, diffuse, be created, or be destroyed – but it must obey a conservation-style balance:

Local resistance balance
\[ \frac{\partial R}{\partial t} + \nabla \cdot J_R = S. \]

Here $J_R$ is the resistance flux (how resistance “flows” through space), and $S$ collects sources and sinks (injected energy, dissipation, reactions, learning events). To turn this into a working evolution equation, you specify how $J_R$ depends on $R$ and its environment.

Step 1 – Diffusion down gradients

Systems smooth out sharp spikes in resistance: high-resistance regions tend to relax toward their surroundings. This is captured by a diffusive flux \[ J_{\text{diff}} = - D \nabla R, \] where $D$ is a (possibly anisotropic) diffusion tensor. Plugging this into the balance law gives

\[ \frac{\partial R}{\partial t} = \nabla \cdot (D \nabla R) + \dots \]

This term alone will take a jagged resistance landscape and smooth it over time.

Step 2 – Drift along a flow field

Now add directed transport: convection, currents, flows, algorithms, behavior. Suppose resistance is also carried by a vector field $\Omega(x,t)$ (a velocity or “organizational flow”). The corresponding flux is \[ J_{\text{flow}} = R\,\Omega, \] which contributes a term

\[ -\nabla R \cdot \Omega \]

after you rewrite $\nabla\cdot(R\Omega)$ and absorb the remaining pieces into $S$. This is the “follow the slope” part: systems drift along gradients of $R$ shaped by $\Omega$.

Step 3 – Hidden-dimension feedback

The key STUR move is promoting “environmental history” and coherence into an explicit coordinate $X$. You postulate that the resistance field also changes as you move along $X$:

X-coupled resistance manifold
\[ \frac{\partial R}{\partial X} = \frac{R - R_{bg}}{L_X}, \]

where $R_{bg}$ is a background resistance level and $L_X$ is a characteristic feedback length. This says: as you move along $X$, you accumulate deviations from the background. To let this hidden feedback shape real dynamics, you couple it back into time evolution:

\[ \text{feedback term} = \chi \frac{\partial R}{\partial X}, \]

with $\chi$ controlling how strongly stored coherence in $X$ affects the visible 4D world.

Step 4 – Collect everything

Combine diffusion, directed flow, hidden-dimension feedback, and sources into a single evolution law:

STUR evolution law
\[ \frac{\partial R}{\partial t} = \nabla \cdot \bigl(D \nabla R\bigr) - \nabla R \cdot \Omega + \chi \frac{\partial R}{\partial X} + S. \]

This is the Law of Resistance. Every system you model in STUR is just a different choice of $D, \Omega, \chi,$ and $S$, plus boundary and initial conditions for $R$.

2 · How to read the equation

One law, many domains

The Law of Resistance is not a single-purpose physics equation; it is a template that becomes mechanics, thermodynamics, information theory, learning, and evolution depending on what you call “resistance” and how you choose the coefficients.

Diffusion: smooth sharp gradients Flow: drift along ordered motion X-feedback: memory & coherence Sources: driving & dissipation

In a plasma, $R$ can track effective resistive load on charge carriers; $D$ encodes microscopic collisions; $\Omega$ carries bulk flows; $S$ describes injection of power. In a living cell, $R$ is metabolic resistance; $X$ carries internal regulatory state; $\chi \partial R/\partial X$ is the feedback loop that makes adaptation and robustness possible.

In a market, $R$ is the resistance to price moves or resource allocation; diffusion captures random exploration, $\Omega$ is informed flow, and $X$ stores institutional memory, protocols, and algorithmic responses.

The point is not that these systems “look similar” by analogy. In STUR they are literally different parameterizations of the same field theory for $R(x,t,X)$.

Hidden dimension as deterministic completion

The 4D spacetime we observe is $(x,t)$, with time as the fourth coordinate. STUR adds one more coordinate $X$ that carries the missing internal information.
When you project down and ignore $X$, the effective dynamics look probabilistic. When you keep $X$, the noise becomes deterministic feedback on the resistance manifold.

3 · Proof of concept: resistance smoothing in 1D

A minimal numerical experiment inside the page

To keep the demo small enough to run in your browser, we switch off flow, X-feedback, and sources, and look at the pure diffusion limit in one spatial dimension:

1D diffusion limit of the Law of Resistance
\[ \frac{\partial R}{\partial t} = D \frac{\partial^2 R}{\partial x^2} \quad\text{(set } \Omega = 0,\ \chi = 0,\ S = 0\text{).} \]

We start with a sharp spike of resistance in the middle of a 1D line and evolve it forward in time. The full STUR equation would add drift, feedback in $X$, and sources; here we just let diffusion do its work and watch the spike smooth out, exactly as the theory predicts. 

// Minimal 1D resistance diffusion (JS, run in browser)
const N = 64;           // grid points
const D = 0.2;          // diffusion coefficient
const dx = 1.0;
const dt = 0.1;         // stable for this D, dx
const steps = 200;      // time steps

let R = new Array(N).fill(0);

// Sharp resistance spike in the center
for (let i = 0; i < N; i++) {
    const dist = Math.abs(i - N/2);
    R[i] = dist < 3 ? 1.0 : 0.0;
}

const initialR = R.slice();

// Explicit finite-difference update
for (let n = 0; n < steps; n++) {
    const newR = R.slice();
    for (let i = 1; i < N - 1; i++) {
        const lap = (R[i+1] - 2*R[i] + R[i-1]) / (dx*dx);
        newR[i] = R[i] + dt * D * lap;
    }
    R = newR;
}

const finalR = R.slice();
Proof-of-concept core

This is a stripped-down instance of the Law of Resistance: it keeps only the $\nabla \cdot (D \nabla R)$ term, but the logic is identical. A concentrated packet of resistance cannot stay infinitely sharp; the PDE forces gradients to relax, spreading the spike into a smooth bump.
