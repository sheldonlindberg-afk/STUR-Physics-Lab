2. Deriving the Schrodinger Equation — Standard Path-Integral Route

2.1 Path Integral to Wave Function

The standard path integral relation (Feynman, 1948) gives the propagator:

\[K(x_f, t_f; x_i, t_i) = \int_{x(t_i)=x_i}^{x(t_f)=x_f} \mathcal{D}[x(t)]\, e^{iS[x]/\hbar}\]
(2.1)

The wave function evolves as:

\[\psi(x_f, t_f) = \int dx_i\, K(x_f, t_f; x_i, t_i)\, \psi(x_i, t_i)\]
(2.2)

2.2 Infinitesimal Time Step

For infinitesimal δt = ε:

\[K(x', t+\varepsilon; x, t) = \sqrt{\frac{m}{2\pi i\hbar\varepsilon}}\, \exp\left[\frac{i}{\hbar}\left(\frac{m(x'-x)^2}{2\varepsilon} - V(x)\varepsilon\right)\right]\]
(2.3)

2.3 Deriving the Differential Equation

Expanding ψ(x', t+ε) to first order in ε and second order in (x'-x):

\[\psi + \varepsilon\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial t} = \int dx'\, K(x,x';t,\varepsilon) \left[\psi + (x'-x)\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial x} + \frac{(x'-x)^2}{2}\frac{\partial^2\psi}{\partial x^2}\right]\]
(2.4)

The Gaussian integrals give:

  • ∫(x'-x) = 0 (odd function)
  • ∫(x'-x)² = iℏε/m

2.4 The Schrodinger Equation Emerges

Collecting terms of order ε:

\[\boxed{i\hbar\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial t} = -\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\frac{\partial^2\psi}{\partial x^2} + V(x)\psi}\]
(2.5) — Schrodinger equation (DERIVED)
Key Result — Standard Path-Integral Construction

Equations (2.1)–(2.5) above are the standard Feynman path-integral derivation of the Schrodinger equation (1948) for a generic action S[x] = ∫(½mẋ² − V)dt; it holds regardless of what S[x] is, and nowhere in this derivation does SSTUR, R, or Φ appear. 3. Canonical Commutation Relations

3.1 Classical Poisson Brackets

In classical mechanics, position and momentum satisfy:

\[\{x^i, p_j\}_{\rm PB} = \delta^i_j\]

\[\boxed{\{A, B\}_{\rm PB} \;\longrightarrow\; \frac{1}{i\hbar}[\hat{A}, \hat{B}]}\]

Applying (3.2) to (3.1):

\[\boxed{[\hat{x}^i, \hat{p}_j] = i\hbar\delta^i_j}\]

Alternatively, the commutator can be derived directly from the path integral using:

\[\langle x_f | [\hat{x}, \hat{p}] | x_i \rangle = \lim_{\varepsilon \to 0} \left[\langle x_f | \hat{x}\hat{p} | x_i \rangle - \langle x_f | \hat{p}\hat{x} | x_i \rangle\right]\]

\[\langle \hat{x}(t+\varepsilon)\hat{p}(t) \rangle - \langle \hat{p}(t)\hat{x}(t+\varepsilon) \rangle = i\hbar\]

4. The Born Rule

4.1 Statement of the Born Rule

The Born rule states that the probability density is:

\[\boxed{P(x) = |\psi(x)|^2}\]
