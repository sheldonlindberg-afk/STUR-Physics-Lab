1. Partial Wave Unitarity

1.1 Unitarity Condition

For elastic scattering, the partial wave amplitude aJ must satisfy:

\[\boxed{|a_J|^2 \leq \text{Im}(a_J) \leq 1/2, \quad \text{or equivalently} \quad |a_J| \leq 1}\]
(U.2) — Unitarity bound

1.2 Partial Wave Decomposition

The scattering amplitude decomposes as:

\[\mathcal{M}(s,t) = 16\pi \sum_J (2J+1) a_J(s) P_J(\cos\theta)\]
(U.3)

1.3 J = 0 Dominance

For longitudinal gauge bosons, the J = 0 partial wave dominates at high energy:

\[a_0 = \frac{1}{32\pi s} \int_{-s}^{0} dt\, \mathcal{M}(s,t)\]
(U.4)

2. WLWL → WLWL Scattering

2.1 Without Higgs (SM Pathology)

Without the Higgs, the amplitude grows with energy:

\[\mathcal{M}(W_L W_L \to W_L W_L)_{\rm no\;H} = \frac{g^2 s}{4M_W^2} \propto s\]
(U.5)

The J = 0 partial wave:

\[a_0^{\rm no\;H} = \frac{g^2 s}{64\pi M_W^2} \quad \Rightarrow \quad |a_0| = 1 \text{ at } \sqrt{s} \approx 1.2\;\text{TeV}\]
(U.6)

2.2 With Higgs (SM Solution)

The Higgs exchange cancels the growth:

\[\boxed{\mathcal{M}_{\rm total} = \mathcal{M}_{\rm gauge} + \mathcal{M}_H = O(g^2) \quad (\text{no } s \text{ growth})}\]
(U.7) — Higgs unitarization

2.3 STUR Enhancement

In STUR, additional KK Higgs modes contribute at high energy:

\[\mathcal{M}^{\rm STUR} = \mathcal{M}^{H^{(0)}} + \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \mathcal{M}^{H^{(n)}} = \mathcal{M}^{\rm SM} + O\left(\frac{s}{M_{\rm KK}^2}\right)\]
(U.8)

The KK corrections are small for √s < MKK.

3. KK Resonance Unitarity

3.1 Resonance Contribution

Each KK resonance contributes a Breit-Wigner:

\[a_J^{(n)}(s) = \frac{g_n^2}{s - m_n^2 + i m_n \Gamma_n}\]
(U.9)

3.2 Unitarity at Resonance

At s = mn², the imaginary part dominates:

\[a_J^{(n)}(m_n^2) = \frac{g_n^2}{i m_n \Gamma_n} = -i\frac{g_n^2}{m_n \Gamma_n}\]
(U.10)

Unitarity requires:

\[\boxed{|a_J^{(n)}| = \frac{g_n^2}{m_n \Gamma_n} \leq 1 \quad \Rightarrow \quad \Gamma_n \geq g_n^2/m_n}\]
(U.11) — Resonance unitarity condition

3.3 STUR KK Widths

In STUR, KK particles have decay widths:

\[\Gamma_{W^{(n)}} \approx \frac{g^2 n}{48\pi L_X} \approx \frac{g^2 M_{KK}}{48\pi} \approx 40\;\text{GeV}\]
(U.12)

With g² ≈ 0.42 and MKK ≈ 2 TeV:

\[|a_J^{(1)}| \approx \frac{0.42}{2000 \times 40} \times (2000)^2 = \frac{0.42 \times 2000}{40} \approx 21 \times 10^{-3} \ll 1\]
KK Resonances Are Narrow and Unitary

All KK resonances satisfy |aJ| << 1. The widths are large enough to ensure unitarity but small enough to be observable as distinct resonances.

4. High-Energy Behavior: KK Sum Rules

4.1 Naive KK Sum

Summing over KK modes naively:

\[\mathcal{M}^{\rm naive} = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{g^2}{s - m_n^2} \xrightarrow{s \to \infty} \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{g^2}{-m_n^2} = -g^2 L_X^2 \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{n^2}\]
(U.13)

4.2 Convergent Sum

The sum converges to:

\[\boxed{\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{n^2} = \frac{\pi^2}{6} \quad \Rightarrow \quad \mathcal{M}^{\rm KK}(s \to \infty) = -\frac{g^2 L_X^2 \pi^2}{6}}\]
(U.14) — Convergent KK sum

4.3 Amplitude Bounded

At very high energies (√s >> MKK):

\[a_0(s \to \infty) = \frac{1}{32\pi s} \times g^2 L_X^2 \frac{\pi^2}{6} \times s = \frac{g^2 L_X^2 \pi}{192} \approx 0.02\]
(U.15)
★ Asymptotic Safety

The partial wave amplitude remains bounded |a0| ≈ 0.02 << 1 even at √s → ∞! This is a consequence of the KK tower summing to a finite constant, not growing with s.

5.1 Modified Propagator

\[G(p) = \frac{1}{p^2 + m^2 + \Lambda_\Omega^2 \sin^2(p_X L_X)}\]

\[\boxed{\sigma_{\rm tot}(s) \leq \frac{\pi}{m_\pi^2} \ln^2\left(\frac{s}{s_0}\right) \quad (\text{satisfied in STUR})}\]
(U.17) — Froissart bound respected
