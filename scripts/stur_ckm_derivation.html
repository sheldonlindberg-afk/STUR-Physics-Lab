1. Gauge Eigenstates vs Mass Eigenstates

1.1 Gauge Interactions at Fixed Points

In STUR, the SU(2)L gauge bosons are localized at the orbifold fixed points (the 4D branes). Weak interactions couple fermions at these specific locations:

\[\boxed{\Matt{\mathcal{L}}_{\rm weak} = g_2 \, \XCRM{W_\mu^a} \, \Matt{\bar{\psi}_L^{(g)}}(x, X=0) \, \gamma^\mu T^a \, \Matt{\psi_L^{(g)}}(x, X=0)}\]
(1.1) — Weak coupling at fixed point

The gauge eigenstates \(\psi_L^{(g)}\) are the fields that couple to W bosons with diagonal coupling. These are the "flavor" states: (u, c, t) and (d, s, b) for quarks; (e, μ, τ) and (νe, νμ, ντ) for leptons.

1.2 Mass Eigenstates from Yukawa Overlaps

As derived in the Yukawa page, the 4D mass matrix comes from overlap integrals:

\[(\Matt{M})_{ij} = v \cdot \tilde{y}_{ij} \int_0^{\XCRM{L_X}} \Matt{\psi_i^{(g)}}^*(X) \, \Matt{H}(X) \, \Matt{\psi_j^{(g)}}(X) \, dX\]
(1.2) — Mass matrix from overlaps

This mass matrix is not diagonal in the gauge basis because different gauge eigenstates have different localization profiles.

1.3 Diagonalization Defines Mass Eigenstates

We diagonalize the mass matrix with unitary transformations:

\[\boxed{U_f^\dagger \, \Matt{M}_f \, V_f = \Matt{M}_f^{\rm diag} = {\rm diag}(m_1, m_2, m_3)}\]
(1.3) — Mass matrix diagonalization

The mass eigenstates are then:

\[\Matt{\psi_L^{(m)}} = U_f^\dagger \, \Matt{\psi_L^{(g)}}, \qquad \Matt{\psi_R^{(m)}} = V_f^\dagger \, \Matt{\psi_R^{(g)}}\]
(1.4) — Basis transformation

2. The CKM Matrix from Geometry

2.1 Charged Current in Mass Basis

The W boson couples up-type and down-type quarks in the gauge basis:

\[\Matt{\mathcal{L}}_{CC} = \frac{g_2}{\sqrt{2}} \, W_\mu^+ \, \Matt{\bar{u}_L^{(g)}} \gamma^\mu \Matt{d_L^{(g)}} + {\rm h.c.}\]
(2.1) — Charged current (gauge basis)

Rewriting in the mass basis using \(\psi_L^{(g)} = U \psi_L^{(m)}\):

\[\boxed{\Matt{\mathcal{L}}_{CC} = \frac{g_2}{\sqrt{2}} \, W_\mu^+ \, \Matt{\bar{u}_L^{(m)}} \gamma^\mu \, \XCRM{V_{\rm CKM}} \, \Matt{d_L^{(m)}} + {\rm h.c.}}\]
(2.2) — CKM appears in mass basis

where the CKM matrix is:

\[\boxed{\XCRM{V_{\rm CKM}} = U_u^\dagger \, U_d}\]
(2.3) — CKM from unitary rotations

2.2 Localization Determines \(U_u\) and \(U_d\)

The key insight: \(U_u\) and \(U_d\) are determined by the localization profiles of up-type and down-type quarks. Since MHP places different generations at different \(X_i^*\), and up-type and down-type quarks have different localization parameters, \(U_u \neq U_d\).

\[(U_f)_{ij} \sim \int_0^{L_X} \psi_i^{\rm gauge}(X)^* \, \psi_j^{\rm mass}(X) \, dX\]
(2.4) — Rotation matrix from overlaps

2.3 Small Mixing from Hierarchical Localization

The off-diagonal elements of \(V_{\rm CKM}\) are small because:

  • Generations are well-separated in the extra dimension
  • Wavefunction overlaps between different generations are exponentially suppressed
  • This is the same mechanism that produces mass hierarchies
\[\boxed{|(V_{\rm CKM})_{ij}| \sim \exp\!\left(-\frac{|X_i^{(u)} - X_j^{(d)}|^2}{2\sigma^2}\right) \quad (i \neq j)}\]
(2.5) — Mixing suppression from geometry

3. The PMNS Matrix from Geometry

3.1 Lepton Mixing

By the same mechanism, the PMNS matrix arises from charged lepton and neutrino localization:

\[\boxed{\XCRM{U_{\rm PMNS}} = U_\ell^\dagger \, U_\nu}\]
(3.1) — PMNS from unitary rotations

3.2 Why Lepton Mixing is Large

Unlike quarks, neutrino mixing angles are large: \(\theta_{12} \approx 34°\), \(\theta_{23} \approx 45°\), \(\theta_{13} \approx 8.5°\). In STUR, this arises because:

Neutrinos: Bulk Propagation

Right-handed neutrinos propagate in the bulk, leading to broader localization profiles and larger overlaps

Charged Leptons: Brane-Localized

Charged leptons are more strongly localized, similar to quarks

Mismatch Creates Large Mixing

The different localization behaviors of \(\nu\) and \(\ell\) give \(U_\ell \neq U_\nu\) with large misalignment

\[\sigma_\nu^{\rm bulk} \gg \sigma_\ell^{\rm brane} \quad \Rightarrow \quad |(U_{\rm PMNS})_{ij}| \sim O(1)\]
(3.2) — Large mixing from bulk/brane asymmetry
Quark vs Lepton Mixing

Quarks: Both up and down types brane-localized → small mixing
Leptons: Charged leptons brane-localized, neutrinos bulk → large mixing
