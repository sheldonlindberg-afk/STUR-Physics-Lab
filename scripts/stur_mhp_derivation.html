1. The MHP Cost Functional

Theorem 1.1 — MHP as Saddle Point Condition

The Minimal Holonomy Principle is not an independent axiom—it is the saddle point condition for the holonomy-weighted path integral.

1.1 Setup: Holonomy on the Z&sub3; Helix

On the Z&sub3; helix, the gauge field holonomy around S¹ is:

\[W = \mathcal{P}\exp\left(i \oint_{S^1} A_X\, dX\right) \in G\]
(1.1) — Wilson loop (holonomy)

For a Lie group G with Cartan subalgebra, parametrize W by:

\[W = \exp(2\pi i \, \mathbf{h} \cdot \mathbf{H})\]
(1.2) — Holonomy in Cartan gauge

where h is a vector in the Cartan subalgebra and H are the Cartan generators.

1.2 The MHP Cost Functional

Complete Derivation of SMHP Advanced

Step 1: The Faddeev-Popov determinant from gauge-fixing produces the Vandermonde structure:

\[\Delta_{\text{FP}} = \prod_{\alpha > 0} |1 - e^{i\alpha \cdot \mathbf{h}}|^2 = \prod_{\alpha > 0} 4\sin^2\left(\frac{\pi\alpha \cdot \mathbf{h}}{2}\right)\]
(1.3) — Vandermonde determinant

Step 2: Exponentiate as an effective action:

\[\Quant{\Omega}[\mathbf{h}] = -\log|\Delta_{\text{FP}}| = -\sum_{\alpha > 0} \log|2\sin(\pi\alpha \cdot \mathbf{h})|\]
(1.4) — Holonomy cost functional

Step 3: Write in gauge-invariant form using Wilson loop traces:

The MHP Cost Functional
\[\boxed{\XCRM{S_{\text{MHP}}} = \int d^4x \left[ |\text{Tr}(\Quant{W})|^2 + \lambda|\Quant{W} - \Quant{W}^\dagger|^2 \right]}\]
(1.5) — Full MHP Action

where:

  • \(\Quant{W} = \mathcal{P}\exp(i\oint A_X\, dX)\) is the Wilson loop
  • \(\lambda\) is determined by the gauge group structure
  • The first term penalizes large trace (holonomy magnitude)
  • The second term enforces unitarity (W close to unitary)
Physical Interpretation of Each Term Intermediate
|Tr(W)|² Term

This term measures how "large" the holonomy is. When W = 1 (identity), Tr(W) = N for SU(N). Smaller traces indicate holonomy eigenvalues spread around the unit circle. MHP favors spread-out eigenvalues because they give smaller |Tr(W)|².

|W - W†|² Term

This term vanishes when W is Hermitian (W = W†) and is maximized when W is anti-Hermitian. For unitary matrices, this constrains the eigenvalue phases. Combined with the trace term, it selects holonomy configurations that minimize the total cost.

1.3 Equivalence to Root-Sum Form

The gauge-invariant form is equivalent to the root-based formula:

\[\boxed{\Quant{\Omega}[\mathbf{h}] = -\sum_{\alpha > 0} \ln|2\sin(\pi\alpha \cdot \mathbf{h})|}\]
(1.6) — MHP in root form

Physical interpretation:

  • Large |sin(πα·h)| → small cost → gauge bosons remain light
  • Small |sin(πα·h)| → large cost (divergent) → forbidden by MHP
  • |sin(πα·h)| = 0 → infinite cost → exactly zero holonomy in α direction

1.4 Z&sub3; Constraint on Holonomy

On the Z&sub3; helix, the holonomy must satisfy the periodicity condition:

\[\boxed{\Quant{W}^3 = \mathbb{1} \quad \text{(after 3 circuits)}}\]
(1.7) — Z&sub3; holonomy constraint

This restricts h to values where:

\[3\mathbf{h} \cdot \alpha \in \mathbb{Z} \quad \text{for all roots } \alpha\]
(1.8) — Quantization condition

For SU(N), this gives hi = ki/3 where ki ∈ {0, 1, 2}.
