Standard 5D Kaluza-Klein theory presents gravity, gauge fields, and scalar moduli as separate ingredients that emerge after dimensional reduction. The "resistance tensor" \(\Qsf_{AB}\) provides an alternative organizational scheme:

Important Clarification

STUR is mathematically equivalent to standard 5D Kaluza-Klein + teleparallel gravity in the gravitational sector. The resistance formalism is a reorganization of variables, not new physics. Its value lies in: (1) conceptual unity, (2) natural accommodation of scalar-matter coupling, and (3) a framework for the interferometric prediction.

Terminology Note: "Resistance" vs. Torsion/Contortion

The term "resistance" in STUR is metaphorical, not a claim of novel geometric content. In the teleparallel formulation (TEGR), gravity is encoded in the contortion tensor \(K^a{}_{bc}\), which relates the Weitzenböck (teleparallel) and Levi-Civita connections. The "resistance tensor" \(\mathsf{Q}_{AB}\) is a 5D metric whose 4D projection yields frame fields \(e^a{}_\mu\), from which contortion is derived. The effective parameter \(K_{\rm eff}\) (§12–13) is phenomenologically linked to an RMS contortion scale but is operationally defined from visibility statistics, not extracted from microscopic torsion components.

Formal vs Effective Variables in STUR

This document operates at two distinct levels that must not be conflated:

  • Formal level: The resistance tensor \(\mathsf{Q}_{AB}\) is a bookkeeping device mathematically equivalent to standard KK variables. Any claim about "substrate stiffness" or "geometric resistance" at this level is organizational language, not ontological assertion.
  • Effective level: The parameter \(K_{\rm eff}\) (§12–13) is an experimentally inferred RMS phase-gradient magnitude. It is defined operationally from visibility decay, not geometrically from microscopic torsion. The identification \(\sigma_R \equiv K_{\rm eff}\) is a phenomenological ansatz, not a derived result.

The falsifiable prediction (Gaussian visibility decay) depends only on the effective level. The formal level provides conceptual framing but does not constitute independent physical content.

Appendix B: Schwinger-Keldysh Derivation

This appendix sketches the derivation of the effective Langevin equation (8.2) from the microscopic theory (8.1) using the Schwinger-Keldysh (closed-time-path) formalism.

B.1 Setup

The generating functional on the closed time contour \(\mathcal{C}\) is:

\[Z[J] = \int \mathcal{D}\varphi\, \mathcal{D}\chi\, \exp\!\left(i \int_\mathcal{C} \dd^4 x\, \left[\mathcal{L}_{\rm scalar}^{\rm micro} + J \cdot \varphi\right]\right)\]
(B.1)

B.2 Integrating Out \(\chi\)

The \(\chi\) path integral is Gaussian. Completing the square:

\[\int \mathcal{D}\chi\, \exp\!\left(i \int_\mathcal{C} \left[-\frac{1}{2}\chi(\Box + m_\chi^2)\chi - \lambda R \chi^2\right]\right) = \exp\!\left(-\frac{i}{2}\text{Tr}\ln(G_\chi^{-1}[R])\right)\]
(B.2)

where \(G_\chi^{-1}[R] = \Box + m_\chi^2 + 2\lambda R\). Expanding to second order in \(\lambda R\):

\[S_{\rm eff}[\varphi] = S_{\rm free}[\varphi] - i\lambda^2 \int_\mathcal{C} \dd^4 x\, \dd^4 x'\, \varphi(x) G_\chi(x,x')^2 \varphi(x') + \mathcal{O}(\lambda^3)\]
(B.3)

B.3 Influence Functional

Decomposing \(\varphi = \varphi_+ - \varphi_-\) (forward/backward branches) and taking the Keldysh rotation \(\varphi_{\rm cl} = (\varphi_+ + \varphi_-)/2\), \(\varphi_{\rm q} = \varphi_+ - \varphi_-\):

\[S_{\rm IF}[\varphi_{\rm cl}, \varphi_{\rm q}] = \int \dd^4 x\, \varphi_{\rm q}\left[-\gamma \dot{\varphi}_{\rm cl} + D\nabla^2 \varphi_{\rm cl}\right] + i\int \dd^4 x\, \varphi_{\rm q}^2 \cdot \gamma T_{\rm eff}\]
(B.4)

where \(\gamma \propto \lambda^2 \cdot \text{Im}(G_\chi^2)\) and the fluctuation-dissipation theorem relates the noise kernel to \(\gamma T_{\rm eff}\).

B.4 Langevin Equation

The equation of motion for \(\varphi_{\rm cl}\) in the presence of the stochastic \(\varphi_{\rm q}\) source is:

\[\dot{\varphi} + \Gamma(\varphi - \varphi_{\rm eq}) = D\nabla^2 \varphi + \xi, \quad \langle\xi(x)\xi(x')\rangle = 2\Gamma T_{\rm eff}\, \delta^{(4)}(x - x')\]
(B.5)

The covariant generalization replaces \(\dot{\varphi}\) with \(u^\mu \partial_\mu \varphi\) where \(u^\mu\) is the bath 4-velocity. See Calzetta & Hu (2008), Chapters 6–8 for the full derivation.
