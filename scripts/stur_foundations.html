Abstract

We present STUR (Sheldon's Theory of Unified Resistance), a five-dimensional framework built on a symmetric tensor field \(\Rcal_{AB}\) on the orbifold \(\Mcal^5 = M^4 \times S^1/\mathbb{Z}_2\). This document has two objectives:

  1. Framework: Demonstrate that teleparallel gravity (TEGR ≡ GR), Yang-Mills gauge structure, and chiral fermion localization can be organized within a unified geometric language.
  2. Experimental signature: Derive a model-independent interferometric visibility prediction from generic scalar-matter Yukawa coupling: \[V(\Delta L) = V_0 \exp\!\left(-(\Delta L / \ell_{\rm coh})^2\right)\] where the coherence length \(\ell_{\rm coh}\) is treated as a phenomenological parameter to be constrained by experiment.
Critical Clarification (v8.1)

The coherence length \(\ell_{\rm coh}\) is not predicted from first principles in this document. A naive estimate from TeV-scale Kaluza-Klein vacuum fluctuations yields \(\ell_{\rm coh} \sim 10^{-59}\) m—roughly 60 orders of magnitude below observable scales. The visibility formula is therefore presented as a model-independent test of any scalar-matter coupling framework, with \(\ell_{\rm coh}\) as a free parameter. Observable effects (\(\ell_{\rm coh} \sim 10\text{–}10^3\) m) would require additional light scalar sectors or non-vacuum fluctuation mechanisms not derived here.

1. Motivation and Axioms

1.1 What the Resistance Formalism Provides

Standard 5D Kaluza-Klein theory presents gravity, gauge fields, and scalar moduli as separate ingredients that emerge after dimensional reduction. The "resistance tensor" \(\Rcal_{AB}\) provides an alternative organizational scheme:

  • Unified bookkeeping: A single symmetric tensor contains gravitational (10), mixed (4), and scalar (1) components. The decomposition follows from the 4+1 split.
  • Natural teleparallel formulation: Frame fields arise as the "square root" of \(\Rcal_{\mu\nu}\), making TEGR primary.
  • Gauge structure from degeneracy: When multiple configurations yield identical physics, the equivalence class defines a gauge group.
Important Clarification

STUR is mathematically equivalent to standard 5D Kaluza-Klein + teleparallel gravity in the gravitational sector. The resistance formalism is a reorganization of variables, not new physics. Its value lies in: (1) conceptual unity, (2) natural accommodation of scalar-matter coupling, and (3) a framework for the interferometric prediction.

6.2 Effective Langevin Equation

Theorem 6.1 — Effective Dynamics

Integrating out \(\chi\) via the Schwinger-Keldysh formalism (Appendix B) yields, in the Markov limit:

\[\boxed{u^\mu \partial_\mu R + \Gamma(R - R_{\rm eq}) = D\Delta R + \xi}\]
(6.2)

where \(\Gamma\) is the relaxation rate, \(D\) the diffusion coefficient, \(\Delta\) the spatial Laplacian in the bath frame (defined by \(u^\mu\)), and \(\xi\) satisfies the fluctuation-dissipation relation \(\langle\xi(x)\xi(x')\rangle = 2\Gamma T_{\rm eff}\, \delta^{(4)}(x-x')\).

Frame Dependence

The effective equation is derived covariantly; the 4-velocity \(u^\mu\) enters through the bath state definition. In the laboratory, \(u^\mu \approx (1, 0, 0, 0)\) for a stationary apparatus.

9. Interferometric Signature

9.1 Phase Modification from Yukawa Coupling

The matter action (3.5) contains \(yR\bar{\Psi}\Psi\), which modifies the effective mass: \(m_{\rm eff} = m_0 + yR\). We work in the non-relativistic atom-interferometry regime (\(p_0 \simeq mv\), where \(v \ll c\)). In a WKB approximation, the phase accumulated along a path is:

\[\phi = \int_{\rm path} p\, \dd\ell = \int_{\rm path} \sqrt{E^2 - m_{\rm eff}^2}\, \dd\ell \approx \int_{\rm path} \left(p_0 - \frac{m_0 y R}{p_0}\right) \dd\ell\]
(9.1)

For two paths with lengths \(L_1\) and \(L_2\), the phase difference includes a contribution \(\Delta\phi_R \propto y\bar{R}\, \Delta L\) where \(\bar{R}\) is the spatially averaged scalar and \(\Delta L = L_1 - L_2\).

9.2 Visibility Under Shot-to-Shot Fluctuations

If \(R\) fluctuates between experimental shots with variance \(\sigma_R^2\), the visibility (fringe contrast) is the expectation of \(\cos(\Delta\phi)\):

\[V = \langle \cos(\Delta\phi) \rangle = \exp\!\left(-\frac{\langle(\Delta\phi_R)^2\rangle}{2}\right) = \exp\!\left(-\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{y\sigma_R}{p_0}\right)^2 (\Delta L)^2\right)\]
(9.2)

Defining the coherence length:

\[\boxed{\ell_{\rm coh} := \frac{p_0}{y\sigma_R} \quad \Rightarrow \quad V(\Delta L) = V_0 \exp\!\left(-\frac{(\Delta L)^2}{\ell_{\rm coh}^2}\right)}\]
(9.3) — STUR Visibility Prediction

10. Coherence Length Estimates

10.1 Naive KK Vacuum Fluctuations

For heavy KK modes (\(m_\chi \gg T_{\rm lab}\)), the dominant fluctuations are quantum vacuum:

\[\langle\chi^2\rangle \approx \frac{1}{2m_\chi L^3} \quad \Rightarrow \quad \sigma_R^2 \sim \frac{\lambda^2 \langle\chi^2\rangle^2}{m_\chi^4} \sim \frac{\lambda^2}{4 m_\chi^6 L^6}\]
(10.1)

10.2 Numerical Estimate

Order-of-magnitude calculation (see Appendix C for details)

Parameters: \(m_\chi = 5\) TeV \(\approx 2.5 \times 10^{16}\) m\(^{-1}\) (in natural units), \(L = 10\) m (interferometer size), \(p_0/m \approx v/c \approx 3 \times 10^{-9}\) (atom velocity), \(y \sim \lambda \sim 1\).
Scalar fluctuation: \[\sigma_R \sim \frac{\lambda}{2 m_\chi^3 L^3} \sim \frac{1}{2 \cdot (2.5 \times 10^{16})^3 \cdot 10^3} \sim 3 \times 10^{-53}\ \text{m}^{-1}\]
Coherence length: \[\ell_{\rm coh} = \frac{p_0}{y\sigma_R} \sim \frac{5 \times 10^{6}}{1 \cdot 3 \times 10^{-53}} \sim 10^{59}\ \text{m}^{-1} \to \ell_{\rm coh} \sim 10^{-59}\ \text{m}\]
Conclusion: The naive estimate yields \(\ell_{\rm coh} \sim 10^{-59}\) m—roughly 60 orders of magnitude below observable scales.
Critical Assessment

Observable effects (\(\ell_{\rm coh} \sim 10\)–\(10^3\) m) are NOT predicted by the naive STUR mechanism with TeV-scale KK modes. The discrepancy is roughly 60 orders of magnitude. For the interferometric signature to be detectable, additional mechanisms are required:

  • Light scalar sector: A scalar with \(m_\phi \sim 10^{-3}\) eV (cf. ultralight dark matter)
  • Cosmological background: Residual oscillations from early-universe dynamics
  • Non-equilibrium sources: Topological defects, domain-wall nucleation

10.3 Model-Independent Parametrization

Given the above, we treat \(\ell_{\rm coh}\) as a phenomenological parameter to be constrained experimentally:

\[\text{Visibility law: } V(\Delta L) = V_0 \exp\!\left(-(\Delta L / \ell_{\rm coh})^2\right), \quad \ell_{\rm coh} \in [10, 10^4]\ \text{m (search range)}\]
(10.2)

This reframes STUR's contribution: the theory predicts the functional form (Gaussian in \(\Delta L^2\)) but not the magnitude (\(\ell_{\rm coh}\)). Experiments constrain or exclude specific \(\ell_{\rm coh}\) values.

Appendix B: Schwinger-Keldysh Derivation

This appendix sketches the derivation of the effective Langevin equation (6.2) from the microscopic theory (6.1) using the Schwinger-Keldysh (closed-time-path) formalism.

B.1 Setup

The generating functional on the closed time contour \(\mathcal{C}\) is:

\[Z[J] = \int \mathcal{D}R\, \mathcal{D}\chi\, \exp\!\left(i \int_\mathcal{C} \dd^4 x\, \left[\Lcal_{\rm scalar}^{\rm micro} + J \cdot R\right]\right)\]
(B.1)

B.2 Integrating Out \(\chi\)

The \(\chi\) path integral is Gaussian. Completing the square:

\[\int \mathcal{D}\chi\, \exp\!\left(i \int_\mathcal{C} \left[-\frac{1}{2}\chi(\Box + m_\chi^2)\chi - \lambda R \chi^2\right]\right) = \exp\!\left(-\frac{i}{2}\Tr\ln(G_\chi^{-1}[R])\right)\]
(B.2)

where \(G_\chi^{-1}[R] = \Box + m_\chi^2 + 2\lambda R\). Expanding to second order in \(\lambda R\):

\[S_{\rm eff}[R] = S_{\rm free}[R] - i\lambda^2 \int_\mathcal{C} \dd^4 x\, \dd^4 x'\, R(x) G_\chi(x,x')^2 R(x') + \mathcal{O}(\lambda^3)\]
(B.3)

B.3 Influence Functional

Decomposing \(R = R_+ - R_-\) (forward/backward branches) and taking the Keldysh rotation \(R_{\rm cl} = (R_+ + R_-)/2\), \(R_{\rm q} = R_+ - R_-\):

\[S_{\rm IF}[R_{\rm cl}, R_{\rm q}] = \int \dd^4 x\, R_{\rm q}\left[-\gamma \dot{R}_{\rm cl} + D\nabla^2 R_{\rm cl}\right] + i\int \dd^4 x\, R_{\rm q}^2 \cdot \gamma T_{\rm eff}\]
(B.4)

where \(\gamma \propto \lambda^2 \cdot \text{Im}(G_\chi^2)\) and the fluctuation-dissipation theorem relates the noise kernel to \(\gamma T_{\rm eff}\).

B.4 Langevin Equation

The equation of motion for \(R_{\rm cl}\) in the presence of the stochastic \(R_{\rm q}\) source is:

\[\dot{R} + \Gamma(R - R_{\rm eq}) = D\nabla^2 R + \xi, \quad \langle\xi(x)\xi(x')\rangle = 2\Gamma T_{\rm eff}\, \delta^{(4)}(x - x')\]
(B.5)

The covariant generalization replaces \(\dot{R}\) with \(u^\mu \partial_\mu R\) where \(u^\mu\) is the bath 4-velocity. See Calzetta & Hu (2008), Chapters 6–8 for the full derivation.

Appendix C: Dimensional Analysis

This appendix provides the detailed calculation of the coherence length estimate in Section 10.2.

C.1 Units and Conversions

In natural units (\(\hbar = c = 1\)):

  • Mass \(m\) has dimension [length]\(^{-1}\)
  • 1 TeV \(= 10^{12}\) eV \(\approx 5.07 \times 10^{15}\) m\(^{-1}\)
  • Atom velocity \(v \approx 1\) m/s \(\Rightarrow v/c \approx 3.3 \times 10^{-9}\)

C.2 Scalar Fluctuation Variance

From Eq. (10.1), the scalar variance due to vacuum fluctuations of \(\chi\):

\[\sigma_R^2 \sim \frac{\lambda^2}{4 m_\chi^6 L^6}\]
(C.1)

With \(m_\chi = 5\) TeV \(\approx 2.5 \times 10^{16}\) m\(^{-1}\) and \(L = 10\) m:

\[\sigma_R \sim \frac{1}{2 m_\chi^3 L^3} = \frac{1}{2 \cdot (2.5 \times 10^{16})^3 \cdot 10^3} \approx \frac{1}{3 \times 10^{52}} \approx 3 \times 10^{-53}\ \text{m}^{-1}\]
(C.2)

C.3 Coherence Length

From Eq. (9.3), with \(p_0 = m v \approx m \cdot 3 \times 10^{-9}\) (where \(m \sim\) GeV for atoms):

\[p_0 \sim 10^9\ \text{eV} \cdot 3 \times 10^{-9} = 1\ \text{eV} \approx 5 \times 10^{6}\ \text{m}^{-1}\]
(C.3)

Therefore:

\[\ell_{\rm coh} = \frac{p_0}{y \sigma_R} \sim \frac{5 \times 10^{6}}{1 \cdot 3 \times 10^{-53}} \sim 2 \times 10^{59}\ \text{m}^{-1}\]
(C.4)

This gives \(\ell_{\rm coh}^{-1} \sim 2 \times 10^{59}\) m\(^{-1}\), meaning \(\ell_{\rm coh} \sim 5 \times 10^{-60}\) m—still catastrophically below observable scales.

Conclusion

The naive KK vacuum fluctuation mechanism yields \(\ell_{\rm coh} \sim 10^{-59}\) m—roughly 60 orders of magnitude below observable scales. Any observable effect requires fundamentally different scalar physics (light scalars, non-equilibrium sources, cosmological backgrounds).
