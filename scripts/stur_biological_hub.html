Why Life Works (The Physics Behind Biology)

Life runs on chemistry, and chemistry runs on physics. The Standard Model gives us atoms that can form bonds. Carbon atoms can make four bonds, letting them build complex molecules. Hydrogen bonds let proteins fold. Electron transfer lets cells make ATP. None of this is magic - it all follows from the same physics that makes stars shine.

ATP: Life's Battery

ATP synthase is a tiny molecular motor that spins to make ATP. It works because of electromagnetic forces between charged particles. The Standard Model explains why these forces have the exact strength they do.

Why Carbon is Special

Carbon can form 4 bonds and create long chains and rings. This flexibility comes from its electron structure, which ultimately traces back to quantum mechanics and the electromagnetic force.

Water: The Solvent of Life

Water's unusual properties (liquid at room temperature, expands when freezing) come from hydrogen bonding. This bonding depends on the precise charges of hydrogen and oxygen atoms - determined by fundamental physics.

Energy from Food

When you eat, your body breaks chemical bonds and captures energy. This energy transfer follows thermodynamics, which emerges from the statistical behavior of particles governed by the Standard Model.
