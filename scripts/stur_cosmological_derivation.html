3. The Residual Cosmological Constant

3.1 Why Cancellation Isn't Perfect

The self-tuning mechanism has a finite response time. The R-field equation of motion is:

\[\ddot{\Diff{R}} + 3H\dot{\Diff{R}} + \frac{\partial V}{\partial \Diff{R}} = 0\]
(3.1) — R-field dynamics

The Hubble friction term \(3H\dot{R}\) limits how fast R can adjust.

3.2 Cosmological Timescale Sets Λ

The residual vacuum energy is set by the competition between:

  • R-field relaxation rate: \(\Gamma_R \sim m_R\)
  • Hubble expansion rate: \(H\)

At late times (\(H \to H_0\)), the residual is:

\[\boxed{\Lambda_{\rm eff} \sim H_0^2 M_{\rm Pl}^2 \sim (10^{-3} \text{ eV})^4}\]
(3.2) — Residual cosmological constant

Observed: \(\Lambda_{\rm obs} \approx (2.3 \times 10^{-3} \text{ eV})^4\) ✓

Why This Scale?

The cosmological constant is small because it's set by the current age of the universe. \(\Lambda \sim H_0^2 M_{\rm Pl}^2\) where \(H_0^{-1} \sim 10^{10}\) years is the Hubble time. This explains the "coincidence problem" — we observe \(\Lambda \sim \rho_{\rm matter}\) today because both scale with \(H_0\).
