4. Gravitational Sector: Teleparallel Equivalence

4.1 The Torsion Scalar

In the teleparallel formulation of gravity, the fundamental geometric object is the torsion tensor, defined in terms of the vierbein (frame field) \(\Tor{e^a_M}\):

\[\color{#60a5fa}{T^P{}_{MN}} = e_a^P(\partial_M e^a_N - \partial_N e^a_M)\]
(4.0) — Torsion tensor

The gravitational action is built from the torsion scalar, a specific contraction of the torsion tensor:

\[\boxed{\color{#60a5fa}{\Tcal} = \frac{1}{4}\color{#60a5fa}{T^{PMN}T_{PMN}} + \frac{1}{2}\color{#60a5fa}{T^{PMN}T_{NPM}} - \color{#60a5fa}{T^P{}_{PM}T^{QM}{}_Q}}\]
(4.1) — Torsion scalar
Teleparallel vs Riemannian Geometry

In standard General Relativity, gravity is described by spacetime curvature (the Riemann tensor). In teleparallel gravity, the same physics is described by torsion instead. The Weitzenböck connection \(\Tor{\hat{\Gamma}^P_{MN} = e_a^P \partial_M e^a_N}\) has torsion but no curvature — the opposite of the Levi-Civita connection.

4.2 TEGR-GR Equivalence Theorem

Theorem 4.1 — Teleparallel Equivalence

The Teleparallel Equivalent of General Relativity (TEGR) and the Einstein-Hilbert formulation differ only by a total derivative:

\[\boxed{\color{#fb923c}{e}\, \color{#60a5fa}{\mathcal{R}_{\rm Ricci}} = -\color{#fb923c}{e}\, \color{#60a5fa}{\Tcal} + 2\partial_M(\color{#fb923c}{e} \color{#60a5fa}{T^M})}\]
(4.2) — TEGR-GR Equivalence

Here \(\Tor{e = \det(e^a_M)}\) is the vierbein determinant, \(\Tor{\mathcal{R}_{\rm Ricci}}\) is the Ricci scalar, \(\Tor{\Tcal}\) is the torsion scalar, and \(\Tor{T^M = T^P{}_{PM}}\) is the torsion vector.

Physical Implications

For matched boundary conditions (where the total derivative integrates to zero), the two formulations yield:

  • Identical field equations — the Einstein equations
  • Identical predictions — all gravitational phenomena (perihelion precession, light bending, gravitational waves)
  • Different conceptual interpretation — geometry (curvature) vs dynamics (torsion)
