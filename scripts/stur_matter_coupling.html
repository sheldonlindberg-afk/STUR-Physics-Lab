6. Matter Sector: XCRM Coupling

6.1 The Matter Action

The matter sector of STUR couples fermions to the resistance field through Yukawa interactions:

\[\boxed{S_{\rm matter} = \int_{\Mcal^5} \dd^5 x\, e\, \color{#22d3ee}{\bar{\Psi}\left(i\Gamma^M D_M - yR\right)\Psi}}\]
(6.1) — Matter action with Yukawa coupling

Here \(\Matt{\Psi}\) is a five-dimensional spinor, \(\Matt{\Gamma^M}\) are the 5D Dirac matrices, \(\Diff{D_M}\) is the covariant derivative, and \(\Quant{y}\) is the Yukawa coupling constant.

The Yukawa Coupling

The term \(\Quant{y}\Diff{R}\Matt{\bar{\Psi}\Psi}\) couples fermions to the resistance field. This coupling:

  • Modifies the effective fermion mass: \(\Matt{m_{\rm eff}} = \Matt{m_0} + \Quant{y}\Diff{R}\)
  • Generates phase shifts in interferometry through mass variation
  • Enables chiral fermion localization on domain walls
  • Creates exponential Yukawa hierarchies from X-localization overlap

7. Fermion Localization

7.1 Zero-Mode Profile

In the presence of a kink solution \(\Diff{R_0}(\XCRM{X})\) that interpolates between vacuum values \(\pm \Pot{v}\), five-dimensional fermions develop localized zero modes. The profile is determined by the Dirac equation with the position-dependent mass \(\Matt{m}(\XCRM{X}) = \Quant{y}\Diff{R_0}(\XCRM{X})\).

Theorem 7.1 — Chiral Localization

The fermionic zero modes have the profile:

\[\boxed{\color{#22d3ee}{f_{0,\pm}(X)} = \mathcal{N}_\pm \exp\!\left(\pm \color{#fbbf24}{y} \int_0^X \color{#4ade80}{R(X')}\, \dd X'\right)}\]
(7.1) — Zero-mode profile

Zero modes localize exponentially near domain walls where \(\Diff{R}(\XCRM{X_*}) = 0\). The orbifold \(\XCRM{\mathbb{Z}_2}\) projection ensures chirality by removing the opposite-chirality zero mode.

7.2 Physical Mechanism

Left-Handed Modes \(\Matt{f_{0,-}}(\XCRM{X})\) localizes where \(\Diff{R}\) transitions from positive to negative values. The profile decays exponentially away from the domain wall.
Right-Handed Modes \(\Matt{f_{0,+}}(\XCRM{X})\) localizes at the opposite boundary. The orbifold projection removes one chirality at each boundary, ensuring 4D chirality.
Domain Wall Physics

This is standard domain-wall fermion physics (Kaplan 1992). STUR inherits this result from its 5D orbifold structure. The kink solution \(\Diff{R_0}(\XCRM{X}) = \Pot{v}\tanh((\XCRM{X}-\XCRM{X_c})/\Diff{\xi})\) naturally provides the sign-changing mass profile required for chiral localization.

8. Mass Hierarchies

8.1 Effective Yukawa from Overlap

The four-dimensional effective Yukawa coupling between localized fermions is determined by the overlap of their wavefunctions in the extra dimension:

\[\boxed{\color{#22d3ee}{y_{\rm eff}^{(ij)}} \propto \exp\!\left(-\frac{(\color{#fbbf24}{X_L^{(i)}} - \color{#fbbf24}{X_R^{(j)}})^2}{2\sigma^2}\right)}\]
(8.1) — Effective Yukawa from X-localization overlap

where \(\XCRM{X_L^{(i)}}\) and \(\XCRM{X_R^{(j)}}\) are the localization positions of the left-handed fermion \(\Matt{i}\) and right-handed fermion \(\Matt{j}\), and \(\Diff{\sigma}\) characterizes the wavefunction width.

8.2 Exponential Suppression

Hierarchy Generation

The exponential suppression can accommodate large mass hierarchies with \(\mathcal{O}(1)\) five-dimensional Yukawa couplings. Small changes in localization positions produce exponentially different effective couplings.

\[\frac{\Matt{m_e}}{\Matt{m_\tau}} \sim \exp\!\left(-\frac{\XCRM{\Delta X}^2}{2\Diff{\sigma}^2}\right) \sim 10^{-4}\]

The observed hierarchy \(\Matt{m_e} \ll \Matt{m_\mu} \ll \Matt{m_\tau}\) corresponds to different localization profiles in \(\XCRM{X}\), not to fine-tuned 5D Yukawa couplings.

9. Phase Modification from Yukawa Coupling

9.1 WKB Phase Accumulation

The Yukawa coupling \(\Quant{y}\Diff{R}\Matt{\bar{\Psi}\Psi}\) modifies the effective mass: \(\Matt{m_{\rm eff}} = \Matt{m_0} + \Quant{y}\Diff{R}\). In the non-relativistic atom interferometry regime (\(\Matt{p_0} \simeq \Matt{m}\Matt{v}\), where \(\Matt{v} \ll c\)), the WKB phase accumulated along a path is:

\[\phi = \color{#a78bfa}{\int_{\rm path}} p\, \dd\ell \approx \color{#a78bfa}{\int_{\rm path}} \left(p_0 - \frac{m_0 \color{#22d3ee}{y} \color{#fbbf24}{R}}{p_0}\right) \dd\ell\]
(9.1) — WKB phase with Yukawa correction

9.2 Resistance Holonomy

For a two-arm interferometer, the measured phase shift is the difference of line integrals, expressible as a closed-loop integral:

\[\boxed{\color{#a78bfa}{\Phi_R} \equiv \Delta\phi_R = \color{#a78bfa}{\oint_{\mathcal{C}}} \color{#fbbf24}{\mathsf{J}_a(x)}\, dx^{a}}\]
(9.2) — Resistance holonomy

Here \(\XCRM{\mathsf{J}_a}\) is the effective phase-gradient 1-form induced by the Yukawa coupling. The holonomy \(\Quant{\Phi_R}\) is gauge-invariant by construction — it is the interferometer output.

Physical Interpretation

The resistance holonomy measures non-integrability of phase transport induced by the resistance substrate. It is a Wilson-loop analogue for the Yukawa-induced phase-gradient 1-form. Unlike the geometric path difference \(\Matt{\Delta L}\), the holonomy \(\Quant{\Phi_R}\) can be nonzero even when the total path length difference vanishes.

9.3 Visibility Under Shot-to-Shot Fluctuations

If \(\Diff{R}\) fluctuates between experimental shots with variance \(\Diff{\sigma_R^2}\), the visibility (fringe contrast) is the expectation of \(\cos(\Delta\phi)\):

\[\color{#f472b6}{V} = \langle \cos(\Delta\phi) \rangle = \exp\!\left(-\frac{\langle(\Delta\phi_R)^2\rangle}{2}\right)\]
(9.3)

For Gaussian-distributed phases (many independent fluctuations, central limit theorem):

\[\boxed{ \color{#f472b6}{V} = V_0 \exp\!\left(-\frac{\langle \color{#a78bfa}{\Phi_R}^2 \rangle}{2}\right) \;\;\xrightarrow{\;\text{linear path}\;}\;\; \color{#f472b6}{V(\Delta L)} = V_0 \exp\!\left(-\frac{(\color{#fb923c}{\Delta L})^2}{\color{#4ade80}{\ell_{\rm coh}}^2}\right) }\]
(9.4) — STUR Visibility Prediction
Regime Interpretation

Long-baseline experiments probe the linear-path limit (right side), where visibility decays as a Gaussian in path difference squared. Tabletop loops probe the holonomy \(\Quant{\Phi_R}\) directly (left side), where area enhancement may be significant.
