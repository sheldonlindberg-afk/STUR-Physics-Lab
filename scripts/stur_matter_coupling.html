6. Matter Sector: XCRM Coupling

6.1 The Matter Action

The matter sector of STUR couples fermions to the resistance field through Yukawa interactions:

\[\boxed{S_{\rm matter} = \int_{\Mcal^5} \dd^5 x\, e\, \color{#22d3ee}{\bar{\Psi}\left(i\Gamma^M D_M - yR\right)\Psi}}\]
(6.1) — Matter action with Yukawa coupling

Here \(\Psi\) is a five-dimensional spinor, \(\Gamma^M\) are the 5D Dirac matrices, \(D_M\) is the covariant derivative, and \(y\) is the Yukawa coupling constant.

The Yukawa Coupling

The term \(yR\bar{\Psi}\Psi\) couples fermions to the resistance field. This coupling:

  • Modifies the effective fermion mass: \(m_{\rm eff} = m_0 + yR\)
  • Generates phase shifts in interferometry through mass variation
  • Enables chiral fermion localization on domain walls
  • Creates exponential Yukawa hierarchies from X-localization overlap

7. Fermion Localization

7.1 Zero-Mode Profile

In the presence of a kink solution \(R_0(X)\) that interpolates between vacuum values \(\pm v\), five-dimensional fermions develop localized zero modes. The profile is determined by the Dirac equation with the position-dependent mass \(m(X) = yR_0(X)\).

Theorem 7.1 — Chiral Localization

The fermionic zero modes have the profile:

\[\boxed{\color{#22d3ee}{f_{0,\pm}(X)} = \mathcal{N}_\pm \exp\!\left(\pm \color{#fbbf24}{y} \int_0^X \color{#4ade80}{R(X')}\, \dd X'\right)}\]
(7.1) — Zero-mode profile

Zero modes localize exponentially near domain walls where \(R(X_*) = 0\). The orbifold \(\mathbb{Z}_2\) projection ensures chirality by removing the opposite-chirality zero mode.

7.2 Physical Mechanism

Left-Handed Modes \(f_{0,-}(X)\) localizes where \(R\) transitions from positive to negative values. The profile decays exponentially away from the domain wall.
Right-Handed Modes \(f_{0,+}(X)\) localizes at the opposite boundary. The orbifold projection removes one chirality at each boundary, ensuring 4D chirality.
Domain Wall Physics

This is standard domain-wall fermion physics (Kaplan 1992). STUR inherits this result from its 5D orbifold structure. The kink solution \(R_0(X) = v\tanh((X-X_c)/\xi)\) naturally provides the sign-changing mass profile required for chiral localization.

8. Mass Hierarchies

8.1 Effective Yukawa from Overlap

The four-dimensional effective Yukawa coupling between localized fermions is determined by the overlap of their wavefunctions in the extra dimension:

\[\boxed{\color{#22d3ee}{y_{\rm eff}^{(ij)}} \propto \exp\!\left(-\frac{(\color{#fbbf24}{X_L^{(i)}} - \color{#fbbf24}{X_R^{(j)}})^2}{2\sigma^2}\right)}\]
(8.1) — Effective Yukawa from X-localization overlap

where \(X_L^{(i)}\) and \(X_R^{(j)}\) are the localization positions of the left-handed fermion \(i\) and right-handed fermion \(j\), and \(\sigma\) characterizes the wavefunction width.

8.2 Exponential Suppression

Hierarchy Generation

The exponential suppression can accommodate large mass hierarchies with \(\mathcal{O}(1)\) five-dimensional Yukawa couplings. Small changes in localization positions produce exponentially different effective couplings.

\[\frac{m_e}{m_\tau} \sim \exp\!\left(-\frac{\Delta X^2}{2\sigma^2}\right) \sim 10^{-4}\]

The observed hierarchy \(m_e \ll m_\mu \ll m_\tau\) corresponds to different localization profiles in X, not to fine-tuned 5D Yukawa couplings.

9. Phase Modification from Yukawa Coupling

9.1 WKB Phase Accumulation

The Yukawa coupling \(yR\bar{\Psi}\Psi\) modifies the effective mass: \(m_{\rm eff} = m_0 + yR\). In the non-relativistic atom interferometry regime (\(p_0 \simeq mv\), where \(v \ll c\)), the WKB phase accumulated along a path is:

\[\phi = \color{#a78bfa}{\int_{\rm path}} p\, \dd\ell \approx \color{#a78bfa}{\int_{\rm path}} \left(p_0 - \frac{m_0 \color{#22d3ee}{y} \color{#fbbf24}{R}}{p_0}\right) \dd\ell\]
(9.1) — WKB phase with Yukawa correction

9.2 Resistance Holonomy

For a two-arm interferometer, the measured phase shift is the difference of line integrals, expressible as a closed-loop integral:

\[\boxed{\color{#a78bfa}{\Phi_R} \equiv \Delta\phi_R = \color{#a78bfa}{\oint_{\mathcal{C}}} \color{#fbbf24}{\mathsf{J}_a(x)}\, dx^{a}}\]
(9.2) — Resistance holonomy

Here \(\mathsf{J}_a\) is the effective phase-gradient 1-form induced by the Yukawa coupling. The holonomy \(\Phi_R\) is gauge-invariant by construction — it is the interferometer output.

Physical Interpretation

The resistance holonomy measures non-integrability of phase transport induced by the resistance substrate. It is a Wilson-loop analogue for the Yukawa-induced phase-gradient 1-form. Unlike the geometric path difference \(\Delta L\), the holonomy \(\Phi_R\) can be nonzero even when the total path length difference vanishes.

9.3 Visibility Under Shot-to-Shot Fluctuations

If \(R\) fluctuates between experimental shots with variance \(\sigma_R^2\), the visibility (fringe contrast) is the expectation of \(\cos(\Delta\phi)\):

\[\color{#f472b6}{V} = \langle \cos(\Delta\phi) \rangle = \exp\!\left(-\frac{\langle(\Delta\phi_R)^2\rangle}{2}\right)\]
(9.3)

For Gaussian-distributed phases (many independent fluctuations, central limit theorem):

\[\boxed{ \color{#f472b6}{V} = V_0 \exp\!\left(-\frac{\langle \color{#a78bfa}{\Phi_R}^2 \rangle}{2}\right) \;\;\xrightarrow{\;\text{linear path}\;}\;\; \color{#f472b6}{V(\Delta L)} = V_0 \exp\!\left(-\frac{(\color{#fb923c}{\Delta L})^2}{\color{#4ade80}{\ell_{\rm coh}}^2}\right) }\]
(9.4) — STUR Visibility Prediction
Regime Interpretation

Long-baseline experiments probe the linear-path limit (right side), where visibility decays as a Gaussian in path difference squared. Tabletop loops probe the holonomy \(\Phi_R\) directly (left side), where area enhancement may be significant.
