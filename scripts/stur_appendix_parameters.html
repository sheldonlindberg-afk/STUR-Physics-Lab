A.1 The XCRM Coupling Constant \(\chi\)

Definition

The XCRM coupling \(\XCRM{\chi}\) appears in the master action as the coefficient of the X-derivative term. For the doublet \(\Diff{\mathbf{R}} = (R_1, R_2)\):

\[\boxed{\XCRM{S_{\rm XCRM}} = \int_{\Mcal^5} \dd^5x\, \sqrt{-g}\, \XCRM{\chi}\, |\Diff{\mathbf{R}}|^2\, \XCRM{\partial_X \phi}}\]
(A.1) — XCRM action term (doublet form)

Equivalently: \(\XCRM{\chi}(R_1 \partial_X R_2 - R_2 \partial_X R_1)\). The doublet structure ensures the torsion coupling remains real.

Physical Origin

The XCRM term arises from the non-trivial topology of the Z₃ helix \(\XCRM{Z₃ helix}\). When a scalar field couples to itself via the internal derivative, the coupling strength \(\XCRM{\chi}\) is determined by the requirement that the effective 4D theory has the correct normalization.

Dimensional Analysis

\[[\XCRM{\chi} |\Diff{\mathbf{R}}|^2 \XCRM{\partial_X \phi}] = M^5 \;\;\Rightarrow\;\; [\XCRM{\chi}] \cdot [\Diff{R}]^2 \cdot [1/L] = M^5\] \[[\XCRM{\chi}] \cdot M^{3} \cdot M^{-1} = M^5 \;\;\Rightarrow\;\; \boxed{[\XCRM{\chi}] = M^3}\]
(A.2) — \(\chi\) has mass dimension 3 (doublet form)

Determination from \(L_X\)

Once the compactification scale \(\XCRM{L_X}\) is fixed experimentally, dimensional analysis determines:

\[\boxed{\XCRM{\chi} = \frac{c_\chi}{\XCRM{L_X}^3}}\]
(A.3) — \(\chi\) from \(L_X\) (doublet form)

where \(c_\chi\) is a dimensionless \(\mathcal{O}(1)\) coefficient determined by the Z₃ helix geometry. For the minimal Z₃ helix with standard normalization, \(c_\chi = 2\pi^2\).

A.2 The Torsion Coupling Constant \(\alpha\)

Definition

The torsion coupling \(\Tor{\alpha}\) appears in the gravitational sector of the master action:

\[\boxed{\Tor{S_{\rm torsion}} = \int_{\Mcal^5} \dd^5x\, \sqrt{-g}\, \Tor{\alpha}\, \Diff{R}\, \Tor{\Tcal}}\]
(A.4) — Torsion action term

Physical Origin

The torsion coupling arises from the requirement that the resistance field couples to the teleparallel torsion scalar \(\Tor{\Tcal}\). This is the unique diffeomorphism-invariant scalar that can be constructed from the vierbein without introducing curvature.

Dimensional Analysis

\[[\Tor{\alpha} \Diff{R} \Tor{\Tcal}] = M^5, \quad [\Diff{R}] = M^{3/2}, \quad [\Tor{\Tcal}] = M^2\] \[\Rightarrow\;\; \boxed{[\Tor{\alpha}] = M^{1/2}}\]
(A.5) — \(\alpha\) has mass dimension 1/2

Relationship to Newton's Constant

In the equilibrium limit \(\Diff{R} \to \Diff{R_{\rm bg}}\), the gravitational sector reduces to TEGR with:

\[\Tor{\alpha} \Diff{R_{\rm bg}} \Tor{\Tcal} = \frac{1}{16\pi G} \Tor{\Tcal} \;\;\Rightarrow\;\; \boxed{\Tor{\alpha} = \frac{1}{16\pi G \Diff{R_{\rm bg}}}}\]
(A.6) — \(\alpha\) from G and \(R_{\rm bg}\)
Non-Circularity Note

This is not circular. The background resistance \(\Diff{R_{\rm bg}}\) is determined by the vacuum expectation value of the potential minimum: \(\Diff{R_{\rm bg}} = v\) where \(\Pot{V'(v)} = 0\). The vacuum value \(v\) is set by the self-coupling \(\Pot{\lambda}\) via \(v^2 = |m^2|/\Pot{\lambda}\), and \(\Pot{\lambda}\) is determined by \(\XCRM{L_X}\) through dimensional analysis: \(\Pot{\lambda} = c_\lambda/\XCRM{L_X}\).

A.3 The Visibility Normalization \(V_0\)

Definition

The visibility normalization \(\Matt{V_0}\) appears in the STUR prediction:

\[\boxed{\Matt{V(\Delta L)} = \Matt{V_0} \exp\!\left(-\frac{\Delta L^2}{\Matt{\ell_{\rm coh}}^2}\right)}\]
(A.7) — STUR visibility prediction

Physical Origin

\(\Matt{V_0}\) represents the intrinsic visibility of the interferometer in the absence of XCRM phase fluctuations. It accounts for all non-STUR sources of visibility reduction: imperfect beam splitters, path length fluctuations, vibrations, etc.

Operational Definition

\[\boxed{\Matt{V_0} \equiv \lim_{\Delta L \to 0} \Matt{V(\Delta L)} = \Matt{V_{\rm apparatus}}}\]
(A.8) — \(V_0\) is the zero-separation visibility

Measurement Protocol: Determine \(\Matt{V_0}\) by extrapolating visibility measurements to \(\Delta L = 0\). Alternatively, measure visibility with a control experiment where STUR effects are absent (e.g., at separations \(\Delta L \ll \Matt{\ell_{\rm coh}}\)).

Typical Values: For well-aligned interferometers, \(\Matt{V_0} \in [0.8, 0.99]\). Perfect visibility corresponds to \(\Matt{V_0} = 1\).

A.4 The RMS Resistance Fluctuation \(\sigma_R\)

XCRM Closure: \(\sigma_R\) is Derived

The fluctuation amplitude \(\Diff{\sigma_R}\) has a single, consistent definition derived from XCRM equilibrium. This is not a free parameter — it is uniquely determined by the compactification scale \(\XCRM{L_X}\).

Definition

\[\boxed{\Diff{\sigma_R}^2 \equiv \langle (\Diff{R} - \Diff{R_{\rm bg}})^2 \rangle = \frac{k_B \Matt{T_{\rm eff}}}{m_{\rm eff}^2}}\]
(A.9) — \(\sigma_R\) from thermal equilibrium

Physical Origin

The resistance field fluctuates about its equilibrium value \(\Diff{R_{\rm bg}}\) due to thermal/quantum excitation of Kaluza-Klein modes. The variance is determined by the equipartition theorem applied to the XCRM-induced effective mass.

After integrating over the internal dimension, the XCRM term generates an effective mass:

\[m_{\rm eff}^2 = \frac{\XCRM{\chi}^2}{\XCRM{L_X}^2} = \frac{c_\chi^2}{\XCRM{L_X}^6}\]
(A.10) — Effective mass from XCRM

The Effective Temperature

The effective temperature \(\Matt{T_{\rm eff}}\) is set by the occupation of the lowest Kaluza-Klein mode:

\[k_B \Matt{T_{\rm eff}} = \hbar c \cdot \frac{\pi}{\XCRM{L_X}} \cdot n_{\rm KK}\]
(A.11) — Effective temperature from KK occupation

where \(n_{\rm KK}\) is the mean occupation number of KK modes (typically \(n_{\rm KK} \sim 1\) at low temperature).

Dimensionless Form

For phenomenological applications, we define the dimensionless fluctuation ratio:

\[\boxed{\tilde{\sigma}_R \equiv \frac{\Diff{\sigma_R}}{\Diff{R_{\rm bg}}} = \sqrt{\frac{k_B \Matt{T_{\rm eff}}}{m_{\rm eff}^2 \Diff{R_{\rm bg}}^2}}}\]
