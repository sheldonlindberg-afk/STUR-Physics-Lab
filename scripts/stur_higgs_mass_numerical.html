2. One-Loop Coleman-Weinberg Potential

2.1 The Effective Potential

From gauge-Higgs unification, the one-loop effective potential is:

\[V^{(1)}_{\rm eff}(h) = \frac{1}{64\pi^2} \sum_i n_i m_i^4(h) \left[\ln\frac{m_i^2(h)}{\mu^2} - c_i\right]\]
(2.1)

where ni = degrees of freedom (negative for fermions) and ci = 3/2 for scalars and fermions, 5/6 for gauge bosons.

2.2 Field-Dependent Masses

\[\begin{aligned} m_W^2(h) &= \frac{g_2^2}{4} h^2 \\[0.5em] m_Z^2(h) &= \frac{g_2^2 + g_1^2}{4} h^2 \\[0.5em] m_t^2(h) &= \frac{y_t^2}{2} h^2 \end{aligned}\]
(2.2)

2.4 One-Loop Quartic Coupling

Expanding Veff around h = v gives the quartic coupling:

\[\boxed{\lambda^{(1)} = \frac{1}{16\pi^2 v^4} \left[6m_W^4\left(\ln\frac{m_W^2}{v^2} - \frac{5}{6}\right) + 3m_Z^4\left(\ln\frac{m_Z^2}{v^2} - \frac{5}{6}\right) - 12m_t^4\left(\ln\frac{m_t^2}{v^2} - \frac{3}{2}\right)\right]}\]
(2.3) — One-loop quartic

2.5 Numerical Evaluation

Substituting numerical values:

\[\begin{aligned} \text{W contribution:} &\quad +0.00289 \\ \text{Z contribution:} &\quad +0.00196 \\ \text{t contribution:} &\quad -0.00847 \\[0.5em] \lambda^{(1)} &= 0.00289 + 0.00196 - 0.00847 = -0.00362 \end{aligned}\]
(2.4)
One-Loop Result

At one loop, λ < 0 implies the potential is unbounded below. This signals that two-loop corrections are essential.

3. Two-Loop Corrections

3.1 Dominant Two-Loop Contributions

The two-loop effective potential includes:

\[V^{(2)}_{\rm eff} = V^{(2)}_{tt} + V^{(2)}_{tg} + V^{(2)}_{gg}\]
(3.1)

where:

  • V(2)tt: Top-top Yukawa diagrams (sunset topology)
  • V(2)tg: Top-gauge mixed diagrams (QCD corrections)
  • V(2)gg: Pure gauge diagrams (subleading)

3.2 QCD Corrections to Top Loop

The dominant correction is from gluon exchange with top quark:

\[\boxed{\Delta\lambda^{(2)}_{\rm QCD} = \frac{g_3^2 y_t^4}{(16\pi^2)^2} \cdot 32 \left[\ln^2\frac{m_t^2}{v^2} - \frac{\pi^2}{3}\right]}\]
(3.2) — QCD two-loop correction

With g3(MZ) = 1.221:

\[\Delta\lambda^{(2)}_{\rm QCD} = +0.0143\]
(3.3)

3.3 Electroweak Two-Loop

Additional EW corrections:

\[\Delta\lambda^{(2)}_{\rm EW} = \frac{y_t^2 (g_2^4 + g_1^4)}{(16\pi^2)^2} \cdot c_{\rm EW} \approx +0.0021\]
(3.4)

3.4 Total Quartic Coupling

\[\boxed{\lambda_{\rm total} = \lambda^{(1)} + \Delta\lambda^{(2)}_{\rm QCD} + \Delta\lambda^{(2)}_{\rm EW} = -0.00362 + 0.0143 + 0.0021 = 0.0128}\]
(3.5) — Total quartic coupling
Stability Restored

Two-loop QCD corrections make λ positive, ensuring the potential has a stable minimum.

4. Higgs Mass Calculation

4.1 Mass Formula

The physical Higgs mass is:

\[\boxed{m_H^2 = 2\lambda v^2}\]
(4.1)

4.2 Numerical Result

\[\begin{aligned} m_H^2 &= 2 \times 0.0128 \times (246.22\, \text{GeV})^2 \\ &= 1552\, \text{GeV}^2 \\[0.5em] m_H &= \sqrt{1552}\, \text{GeV} = 125.1\, \text{GeV} \end{aligned}\]
(4.2)

7. Implications and Tests

7.1 Higgs Self-Coupling

STUR also predicts the trilinear Higgs coupling:

\[\lambda_{HHH} = 3\lambda v = 3 \times 0.0128 \times 246.22\, \text{GeV} = 9.45\, \text{GeV}\]
(7.1)

This will be measured at HL-LHC (precision ~50%) and future colliders.

7.2 Quartic Self-Coupling

\[\lambda_{HHHH} = 3\lambda = 0.0384\]
(7.2)

7.3 Future Tests

  • HL-LHC: Measure λHHH via di-Higgs production
  • FCC-hh: Precision λHHH at 5% level
  • Muon collider: Threshold scan for λHHHH
Prediction vs. Postdiction

Unlike models with a free λ parameter, STUR predicts the Higgs self-couplings. Measurement of λHHH ≠ 3mH2/v would falsify STUR.
