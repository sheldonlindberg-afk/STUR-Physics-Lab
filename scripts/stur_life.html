1. STUR Manifold and Origin-of-Life Geometry

The STUR framework treats the universe as a unified resistance manifold R(x,t,X) evolving on a five-dimensional space, where the extra coordinate X carries hidden coherence, memory, and constructive information. In this model we focus on a single hydrothermal micro-pore and track how a prebiotic chemical mixture spontaneously assembles into a stable proto-cell-like structure.

On a 4D slice (3D space plus time), the effective resistance field follows a STUR-style evolution law

∂R/∂t = ∇·(D∇R) − ∇R · Ω + χ ∂R/∂X + S

The hidden dimension X encodes the “missing determinism”:

∂R/∂X = (R − R_bg) / L_X where R_bg(t) = ⟨R(x,t)⟩ is the spatial mean at time t.

In this concrete hydrothermal pore model, we construct R from the chemical and energetic fields:

R(x,t) = w_A A(x,t) + w_B B(x,t) + w_C C(x,t) + w_E E(x,t) A: high-energy precursor B: autocatalytic proto-metabolite C: waste / low-free-energy products E: local free-energy density (J/m³, scaled)

The X-dimension couples back into chemistry through an exponential feedback on the autocatalytic channel:

f_X(x,t) = exp( χ_X [ R(x,t) − R_bg(t) ] ) effective rate: A + B → 2B is multiplied by f_X.

When the local resistance R rises above its background value, f_X increases and amplifies local autocatalysis. When R falls below R_bg, f_X decreases, suppressing growth. This curvature-sensitive feedback turns the resistance manifold into a selection mechanism: spatial regions that dissipate energy efficiently and maintain coherent structure are automatically reinforced.

In the STUR acronym used here,

S = Spatial resistance (R gradients in 3D) T = Temporal resistance (stability of patterns in time) U = Universal constructor (feedback loops that copy structure) R = Robustness (persistence under noise and flow)

The 5D manifold R(x,t,X) implements this STUR loop explicitly: spatial patterns of resistance are converted into temporal persistence through X-feedback, and structures that successfully harness the energy flux of the pore become robust attractors — proto-cells.

2. Hydrothermal Pore Physics and Non-Equilibrium Drivers

The simulation includes several realistic non-equilibrium mechanisms:

  • Vertical temperature gradient (≈ 10 K/µm) across a 10 µm pore, driving Arrhenius kinetics and thermophoresis.
  • Thermophoresis (Soret effect), species-specific: B (proto-metabolite) is strongly thermophobic and accumulates in colder regions.
  • Recirculating convection modeled as a toroidal vertical flow v_z ∝ sin(2πz/L) with small stochastic perturbations.
  • Mineral surface catalysis via a smooth exponential boost near walls, mimicking FeS/NiS mineral surfaces.
  • Open-system feeding of precursor A and free energy E to maintain an entropy-balanced non-equilibrium steady state.
  • Energy field E(x,t) with local consumption by reactions and cooling back toward an ambient value.

Together, these mechanisms create a physically rich environment where gradients, flows, and STUR curvature conspire to focus B into compact, long-lived structures that behave like abiotic proto-cells.

4. Usage Notes and Publication Hooks

For a quick visual check, keep nt_run = 8000. For publication-grade runs, increase nt_run to 50000–100000, enable snapshot_stride and set a snapshot_prefix to generate 3D time series suitable for Paraview/VTK rendering and movies.

The key control parameter is chi_X. As you sweep across chi_X, you will see:

  • chi_X = 0: no STUR feedback, diffuse growth, no stable localization.
  • chi_X ~ 2–3: weak clustering, transient spots.
  • chi_X ~ 3.5–4.5: sharp transition to persistent proto-cell-like structures.
  • chi_X ≥ 7: runaway regimes where STUR feedback must be clamped.

This makes the model ideal for a phase-diagram style study: emergent order and metabolism-like structures as a function of STUR curvature feedback strength.
