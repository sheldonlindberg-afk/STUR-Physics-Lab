1. STUR Field Equation → Hydrothermal Vent Model

STUR is Sheldon's Theory of Unified Resistance. The fundamental object is a unified resistance field R(x,t,X) defined on a 5D manifold: three spatial coordinates, physical time t, and an internal coordinate X that carries the “missing” deterministic information (coherence, memory, feedback) that gets projected away in standard 4D physics.

R = R(x,t,X) unified resistance field ∂R/∂t = ∇·(D∇R) − ∇R · Ω + χ ∂R/∂X + S STUR evolution law ∂R/∂X = (R − R_bg) / L_X X-coupled resistance manifold G_ab = ∂_a R ∂_b R + g_ab V(R) curvature from resistance gradients

In this specific lab we focus on a single alkaline hydrothermal micro-pore. We ignore bulk fluid vorticity Ω and group the detailed source terms into a reaction–diffusion description for a small set of chemical fields: CO₂, H₂, an acetyl-thioester analogue Ac, and an ATP-like phosphate potential. The pore provides: a vertical temperature gradient, a proton (pH) gradient, redox energy via H₂/CO₂, and catalytic FeS/NiS walls.

The resistance field is constructed from these chemical fields:

R(x,t) = w_CO2 · [CO2](x,t) + w_H2 · [H2](x,t) + w_Ac · [Ac](x,t) + w_ATP · [ATP](x,t) + w_Fe · [Fe²⁺](x,t) R_bg(t) = ⟨R(x,t)⟩_volume ∂R/∂X ≈ (R − R_bg)/L_X f_X(x,t) = exp[ χ_X · (R(x,t) − R_bg(t)) ] (bounded numerically)

The X-dimension is not simulated explicitly as a new axis. Instead, the discrete map ∂R/∂X ≈ (R−R_bg)/L_X is folded into the reaction rates through f_X, which is how STUR shows up in the PDEs:

∂[Ac]/∂t = D_Ac ∇²[Ac] − v_th(z) ∂[Ac]/∂z + r_form([CO2],[H2],[Ac],T,pH) · f_X − λ_Ac [Ac] ∂[CO2]/∂t = D_CO2 ∇²[CO2] − r_form ∂[H2]/∂t = D_H2 ∇²[H2] − r_form ∂[ATP]/∂t = D_ATP ∇²[ATP] + y_ATP · r_form − λ_ATP [ATP]

with the catalytic formation term

r_form = surface(x,y) · k_cat(T) · f_pH(pH) · f_X(R−R_bg) · [CO2] · [H2] · ([Ac] + ε) k_cat(T) = k_base · exp[ −E_a/R_g (1/T − 1/T_ref) ] f_pH = exp[ −α (pH − pH_opt)² ] surface ≈ 1 + O(10–40×) near FeS/NiS-like walls v_th(z) = − D_Ac S_T · ∂T/∂z / T (thermophoresis, Soret effect)

The script below implements these equations on a 32³ grid embedding a cylindrical micro-pore with a 12 µm height/diameter, 80–120 °C vertical temperature gradient, and a pH gradient from ≈ 9.8 (alkaline interior) to ≈ 6 (external ocean side). STUR enters only through the curvature-sensitive feedback factor f_X, which elevates regions with large resistance curvature into stable, self-sustaining proto-cell-like attractors.

To keep Jupyter and JupyterLite stable, the script uses: bounded STUR feedback (arg = clip(χ (R−R̄), −8, 8)), a capped catalytic rate, a reduced time step, and upper bounds on CO₂, H₂, Ac, and ATP. This preserves the qualitative STUR bifurcation while eliminating NaNs and numerical blow-ups.

The JSON file stur_vent_fast.json now includes a dedicated "life_signature" block. It records: the growth ratio of ⟨Ac⟩ and ⟨ATP⟩ over time, the peak and final resistance variance, the spatial variance of the final Ac field, and the fraction of grid cells where Ac exceeds three times the spatial mean. In STUR language, that non-uniform, curvature-selected cluster fraction is the “emergent proto-cell” signature.

4. Interpretation and life_signature JSON

As you sweep χX from 0 upward, the bifurcation plot shows the transition from a near-uniform mixer to a curvature-selected regime with enhanced resistance variance. The example χX history tracks how ⟨Ac⟩ and ⟨ATP⟩ grow over time in that regime, and the midplane slice makes the localized structure visible as an acetyl-rich hot spot anchored to the FeS/NiS wall and the ΔT–ΔpH gradients.

The JSON life_signature block turns that picture into numbers: if the final ⟨Ac⟩ and ⟨ATP⟩ are significantly larger than their initial values, the peak resistance variance is high, and the Ac_cluster_fraction_gt3x is non-zero, the system has organized into a localized, curvature-maintained structure rather than smearing into equilibrium. In STUR language, that is the emergent proto-cell attractor of this 5D resistance manifold.
