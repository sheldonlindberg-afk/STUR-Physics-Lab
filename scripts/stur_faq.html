Credibility & Skepticism

"Is this just another crackpot theory?"

Fair question. Here's how to evaluate it:

Signs this is serious:
  • Explicit falsification criteria
  • Quantitative predictions (not just qualitative)
  • Complete first-principles derivations
  • Acknowledges experimental verification needed
  • Uses standard mathematical framework
Red flags to watch for:
  • Claims certainty without evidence
  • Immune to falsification
  • Invokes conspiracy against acceptance
  • Dismisses all criticism
  • No quantitative predictions

Bottom line: STUR makes a specific, testable prediction (Gaussian visibility decay with ℓ ~ 0.3-30 m). If experiments show something else, STUR is wrong. That's how science works. Read the Falsification Protocol to see exactly what would falsify the theory.

"Has this been peer-reviewed?"

Not in the traditional sense. STUR has not been published in a peer-reviewed physics journal.

What exists:

  • Complete derivation chain document with theorem-proof structure
  • Documentation meeting scientific standards for clarity and falsifiability
  • Open-source code and derivations anyone can check

What doesn't exist (yet):

  • Publication in Physical Review, JHEP, or similar
  • Independent verification by other physicists
  • Experimental confirmation of the predictions
Honest assessment:

STUR provides complete first-principles derivations, but until the predictions are tested experimentally, it should be treated as a theoretical framework awaiting verification. The documentation here makes the claims clear enough to evaluate and test.

"Who created this theory?"

STUR was developed by Sheldon Lon Lindberg. See the About page for background information.

The theory's validity doesn't depend on who created it—it depends on whether the predictions match experiments. History is full of examples of outsiders making contributions (Ramanujan, Faraday) and insiders making mistakes. Check the math and the predictions, not the credentials.

Testability

"Can STUR actually be tested?"

Yes, with current or near-future technology.

STUR predicts:

V(ΔL) = V₀ exp(−ΔL²/ℓ²coh)

The coherence length ℓcoh is estimated at 0.3–30 meters. This is within range of:

  • MAGIS-100 — 100m baseline atom interferometer at Fermilab
  • AION — UK-based atom interferometer network
  • Existing Sagnac loops — could see area-enhanced effects

See Numerical Predictions for concrete numbers.

"What would prove STUR wrong?"

Any of these observations would falsify STUR:

Linear decay V ∝ exp(−ΔL/ℓ) instead of exp(−ΔL²/ℓ²)
Oscillations Periodic wiggles in visibility vs. ΔL
Time dependence Visibility changes over time at fixed ΔL
Mass dependence Different particles give different ℓ at same ΔL
Multiple coherence lengths Data requires two or more ℓ values to fit

Full details at Falsification Protocol.

"Why hasn't anyone tested this already?"

The prediction is new, and the required precision is challenging.

Matter-wave interferometers with ~10m+ baselines are relatively recent technology. Most existing experiments weren't designed to look for Gaussian visibility decay—they're optimized for other measurements (gravitational waves, fundamental constants, etc.).

The experiments that could test STUR (MAGIS-100, AION) are currently being built or commissioned.

Key difference: String theory's extra dimensions are too small to test directly. STUR's single extra dimension is large enough (~μm) to have observable effects in interferometers.

"Why geometry? Why should shape determine physics?"

This idea has a long history in physics:

  • General Relativity (1915): Gravity is curvature of spacetime geometry
  • Kaluza-Klein (1921): Electromagnetism from 5D geometry
  • Gauge theories: Forces from local symmetry of internal spaces
  • Topology: Quantized quantities (charges) from non-contractible loops

STUR extends this: the orbifold's topology forces certain configurations. Winding numbers (how many times you wrap around) determine particle generations. Holonomy minimization selects the gauge group.

Technical Questions

"What is the 'resistance field' resisting?"

The name is intuitive shorthand. Technically, \(R(x,X)\) is a scalar field on the 5D orbifold that:

  • Has a double-well potential V(R) constrained by the orbifold boundary conditions
  • Creates domain walls where matter localizes
  • Couples to torsion to produce gravitational effects
  • Fluctuates around equilibrium, causing the predicted visibility decay

Think of it like "resistance to being away from equilibrium"—the field wants to sit in one of the potential minima, and fluctuations around that equilibrium have physical consequences.

"Why 3 axioms instead of 2?"

The three axioms serve distinct roles:

  1. Master Action (Axiom 1): Defines what exists and how it evolves—the basic dynamics
  2. DHP (Axiom 2): Governs time evolution and cosmology—ensures holonomy stays finite
  3. TFP (Axiom 3): Determines flavor structure—winding numbers label generations

Note: MHP (Minimum Holonomy Principle) is derived from the path integral, not an axiom. See MHP Derivation.

"What does the '19 problems' claim mean?"

STUR claims to derive 19 physical results from the 3 axioms:

  • 7 Core: Visibility law, coherence, TEGR, Yang-Mills, MHP, moduli, DHP equations
  • 4 SM-origin: Gauge group, 3 generations, Yukawa hierarchies, CKM/PMNS
  • 4 BSM: UV completion, neutrino masses, CP violation, dark matter
  • 4 Cosmology: Λ, hierarchy problem, inflation, baryogenesis

Each has a derivation page. "Derived" means there's a chain of reasoning from axioms to result— see Technical Appendix for full proofs.
