4. All-Orders Finiteness: Inductive Proof (EFT Level)

4.1 Theorem Statement

Theorem 4.1 — EFT-Level All-Orders UV Finiteness

For any Feynman diagram \(\Gamma\) with \(L\) loops in the EFT regime (E < MKK), the amplitude \(\mathcal{A}_\Gamma\) satisfies:

\[\boxed{|\mathcal{A}_\Gamma| \leq C_\Gamma \cdot \Lambda_\Omega^{-L} < \infty}\]

where \(C_\Gamma\) depends on external momenta and coupling constants but not on any UV cutoff.

4.2 Proof by Induction

Base case (L=1): Proven in Section 3.

Inductive step: Assume all diagrams with ≤ (L-1) loops are finite. Consider an L-loop diagram.

Step 1: Superficial degree of divergence

In 5D, the superficial degree of divergence for a diagram with:

  • \(I\) internal lines
  • \(V\) vertices
  • \(L\) loops

is:

\[D = 5L - 2I_{\rm gauge} - 2I_{\rm scalar} - I_{\rm fermion}\]
(4.1)

Step 2: Holonomy suppression

Each loop involves at least one \(k_X\) integration. With the modified propagator:

\[\int dk_X \frac{1}{k_X^2 + \Lambda_\Omega^2} \sim \frac{1}{\Lambda_\Omega}\]
(4.2)

This reduces the effective degree of divergence by 1 per loop:

\[\boxed{D_{\rm eff} = D - L = 4L - 2I_{\rm gauge} - 2I_{\rm scalar} - I_{\rm fermion}}\]
(4.3) — Effective degree of divergence

Step 3: Topology constraint

From graph theory: \(L = I - V + 1\). For gauge theory vertices with at least 3 legs:

\[3V \leq 2I + E \quad \Rightarrow \quad V \leq \frac{2I + E}{3}\]
(4.4)

where \(E\) is the number of external legs.

Step 4: Combining constraints

Substituting:

\[D_{\rm eff} = 4(I - V + 1) - 2I = 2I - 4V + 4 \leq 2I - \frac{4(2I+E)}{3} + 4 = -\frac{2I}{3} - \frac{4E}{3} + 4\]
(4.5)

For \(E \geq 2\) (physical processes) and \(I \geq 1\):

\[\boxed{D_{\rm eff} \leq 4 - \frac{2}{3} - \frac{8}{3} = \frac{2}{3} < 1}\]
(4.6) — Negative effective divergence

Since \(D_{\rm eff} < 1\), all diagrams are convergent. QED.

5. Gauge Invariance Preservation

5.1 The Concern

The holonomy regulation modifies the \(A_X\) propagator differently from \(A_\mu\). Does this break gauge invariance?

5.2 Resolution: Background Field Method

The holonomy functional is defined in terms of Wilson loops, which are gauge-invariant:

\[W[\gamma] = \text{Tr}\, \mathcal{P}\exp\left(i\oint_\gamma A\right) \quad \text{is gauge invariant}\]
(5.1)

The holonomy weight \(e^{-\lambda_\Omega \Omega[A]}\) is therefore gauge-invariant.

5.3 Ward Identities

The modified propagators satisfy deformed Ward identities:

\[\boxed{k^\mu \Pi_{\mu\nu}(k) = 0, \quad k_X G_{X\mu}(k) = 0}\]
(5.2) — Ward identities preserved

The transversality is preserved because the holonomy regulation respects gauge symmetry.

Result: Gauge Invariance Is Preserved

The holonomy weight, being constructed from gauge-invariant Wilson loops, preserves all Ward identities. The modified propagators are still transverse, and gauge anomalies cancel as in the unregulated theory.
