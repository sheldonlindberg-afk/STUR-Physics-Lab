Executive Summary

Main Result

QCD confinement—the fact that isolated quarks cannot exist—emerges from STUR through two mechanisms: (1) Asymptotic freedom from negative beta function (derived in Running Couplings), and (2) area law for Wilson loops from holonomy dynamics (Axiom 2).

1. The Confinement Problem

1.1 Experimental Fact

No isolated quark has ever been observed. All strongly-interacting particles are either:

  • Mesons: quark-antiquark pairs (qq̄)
  • Baryons: three-quark combinations (qqq)

1.2 The Theoretical Challenge

QCD is well-defined, but proving confinement from first principles is one of the Millennium Prize Problems. STUR provides a mechanism.

1.3 Key Observables

Confinement is characterized by:

  • Linear potential: V(r) ~ σr at large r
  • Wilson loop area law: ⟨W(C)⟩ ~ exp(-σ·Area(C))
  • String tension: σ ≈ (420 MeV)² ≈ 0.18 GeV²

3. Asymptotic Freedom

3.1 Beta Function

From Running Couplings, the QCD beta function is:

\[\boxed{\beta(g_s) = -\frac{g_s^3}{16\pi^2}\left(\frac{11}{3}N_c - \frac{2}{3}n_f\right) = -\frac{7g_s^3}{16\pi^2}}\]
(3.1) — QCD beta function (DERIVED)

For Nc = 3 colors and nf = 6 flavors, b0 = 7 > 0, so β < 0.

3.2 Running Coupling

The strong coupling decreases at high energy:

\[\boxed{\alpha_s(Q) = \frac{\alpha_s(\mu)}{1 + \frac{7\alpha_s(\mu)}{2\pi}\ln(Q/\mu)}}\]
(3.2) — Asymptotic freedom

As Q → ∞, αs → 0: quarks become free at high energy.

3.3 Infrared Growth

Conversely, as Q → ΛQCD ≈ 200 MeV:

\[\alpha_s(Q) \to \infty \quad \text{as } Q \to \Lambda_{\rm QCD}\]
(3.3)

The coupling blows up at low energy, indicating strong binding.

Asymptotic Freedom Implies Infrared Slavery

The same mechanism that makes quarks free at high energy makes them strongly bound at low energy. This is half of confinement.

4. Wilson Loop and Area Law

4.1 Wilson Loop Definition

The Wilson loop is the holonomy around a closed curve C:

\[\boxed{W(C) = \text{Tr}\, \mathcal{P}\exp\left(ig_s \oint_C G_\mu dx^\mu\right)}\]
(4.1) — Wilson loop

4.2 Connection to Holonomy Principle

In STUR, the Dynamical Holonomy Principle (Axiom 2) governs holonomy dynamics:

\[\Omega[W] = \text{Tr}[\mathbf{1} - W] \geq 0\]
(4.2)

MHP minimizes Ω, but in the infrared, fluctuations become strong.

4.3 Area Law Emergence

For a rectangular loop of size R × T (R = spatial, T = temporal):

\[\boxed{\langle W(C) \rangle \sim \exp(-\sigma \cdot R \cdot T) = \exp(-\sigma \cdot \text{Area})}\]
(4.3) — Area law (confinement criterion)

4.4 String Tension

The string tension σ arises from holonomy fluctuations in the strong-coupling regime:

\[\sigma = \lim_{R,T\to\infty} -\frac{1}{RT}\ln\langle W(C) \rangle\]
(4.4)

Lattice QCD gives σ ≈ (420 MeV)² ≈ 0.18 GeV².

4.5 Physical Interpretation

The area law means the energy to separate a quark-antiquark pair grows linearly with distance:

\[\boxed{V(r) = \sigma \cdot r + \text{const.}}\]
(4.5) — Linear confining potential

At r ~ 1 fm, the energy exceeds 2mq, creating new qq̄ pairs instead of free quarks.

\[\boxed{\langle \text{Tr}\, W_{\rm color} \rangle = 0 \quad \text{(confinement)}}\]

\[\langle \text{Tr}\, W_{\rm color} \rangle \neq 0 \quad \text{(deconfined QGP)}\]

This is the quark-gluon plasma phase observed at RHIC and LHC.
