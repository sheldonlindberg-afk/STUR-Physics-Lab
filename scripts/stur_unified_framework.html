1. Overview and Architecture

Scope and Status

This document presents a unified teleparallel framework, not a closed ultraviolet-complete Theory of Everything. TEGR (≡ GR) provides the geometric backbone; HGEM describes the linearized wave sector; STUR supplies a phenomenological, interferometrically falsifiable holonomy observable; Chronomagnetics introduces a rigorously defined log-periodic modulation sector.

No claim is made that Standard Model parameters, coupling constants, or cosmological initial conditions are derived. All predictions presented are sector-local and experimentally testable. The unification is organizational and geometric, not a claim of ultimate microscopic completeness.

After dimensional reduction, STUR constitutes a one-parameter family of effective models, parameterized by the dimensionless ratio \(\sigma_R/L_X\), testable across interferometric platforms.

The teleparallel formulation of gravity replaces curvature with torsion as the geometric carrier of gravitational dynamics. In TEGR (Teleparallel Equivalent of General Relativity), the fundamental variable is the tetrad \(e^a{}_\mu\) rather than the metric \(g_{\mu\nu}\), and gravity is encoded in the contortion tensor \(K^a{}_{bc}\) derived from the Weitzenböck connection.

3. TEGR: Teleparallel Equivalent of GR

3.1 Fundamental Variables

In TEGR, gravity is formulated using tetrad (vierbein) fields \(e^a{}_\mu\) satisfying:

\[g_{\mu\nu} = \eta_{ab}\, e^a{}_\mu\, e^b{}_\nu\]

The Weitzenböck connection \(\Gamma^\lambda_{\mu\nu} = e_a{}^\lambda \partial_\mu e^a{}_\nu\) has zero curvature but nonzero torsion:

\[T^a{}_{\mu\nu} = \partial_\mu e^a{}_\nu - \partial_\nu e^a{}_\mu\]

3.2 TEGR ≡ GR Equivalence

Theorem — TEGR Equivalence

The TEGR action differs from the Einstein-Hilbert action by a total divergence:

\[S_{\rm TEGR} = \frac{1}{16\pi G} \int d^4x\, e\, T = S_{\rm EH} + \text{boundary term}\]

The field equations are identical to Einstein's equations. TEGR is GR reformulated in torsion language.

3.3 Contortion Tensor

\[K^a{}_{bc} = \frac{1}{2}\left(T_{bc}{}^a + T_{cb}{}^a - T^a{}_{bc}\right)\]

This is the geometric object that STUR parameterizes phenomenologically via \(K_{\rm eff}\).

6. Chronomagnetics: Phenomenological Log-Periodic Modulation

6.1 The Geometric Constant

Chronomagnetics introduces a log-periodic modulation of tangential contortion, defined from exact triangle geometry and embedded consistently within the teleparallel (TEGR) formalism.

Chronomagnetics is an optional phenomenological modulation layer. None of the STUR closure, derivations, or falsifiable predictions depend on its inclusion. It does not introduce new field equations and does not claim a microscopic derivation of \(\lambda\).

As established in the Chronomagnetics Mathematical Foundation, the constant \(\lambda = 3722/2705\) is an exactly defined geometric ratio arising from a specific illustrative triangle construction \(\{116, 138, 144\}\). This triangle is not claimed to be unique, but serves as a minimal demonstrator of log-periodic modulation consistent with teleparallel kinematics.

\[\lambda = \frac{3722}{2705} \approx 1.376\]

This sector is mathematically well-defined and dimensionally consistent, but remains phenomenological with respect to microscopic origin. No claim is made that \(\lambda\) is fundamental; it is introduced as an illustrative modulation parameter within the teleparallel framework.
