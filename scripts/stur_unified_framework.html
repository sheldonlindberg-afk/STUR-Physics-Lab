1. Overview and Architecture

Scope and Status

This document presents a unified teleparallel framework, not a closed ultraviolet-complete Theory of Everything. TEGR (≡ GR) provides the geometric backbone; HGEM describes the linearized wave sector; STUR supplies an interferometrically falsifiable holonomy observable derived from XCRM; Chronomagnetics introduces a rigorously defined log-periodic modulation sector.

No claim is made that Standard Model parameters, coupling constants, or cosmological initial conditions are derived. All predictions presented are sector-local and experimentally testable. The unification is organizational and geometric, not a claim of ultimate microscopic completeness.

After dimensional reduction, STUR closes with a single free parameter \(L_X\). All other parameters, including \(\sigma_R\), are derived from XCRM dynamics (§5.3.5).

The teleparallel formulation of gravity replaces curvature with torsion as the geometric carrier of gravitational dynamics. In TEGR (Teleparallel Equivalent of General Relativity), the fundamental variable is the tetrad \(e^a{}_\mu\) rather than the metric \(g_{\mu\nu}\), and gravity is encoded in the contortion tensor \(K^a{}_{bc}\) derived from the Weitzenböck connection.

3. TEGR: Teleparallel Equivalent of GR

3.1 Fundamental Variables

In TEGR, gravity is formulated using tetrad (vierbein) fields \(e^a{}_\mu\) satisfying:

\[g_{\mu\nu} = \eta_{ab}\, e^a{}_\mu\, e^b{}_\nu\]

The Weitzenböck connection \(\Gamma^\lambda_{\mu\nu} = e_a{}^\lambda \partial_\mu e^a{}_\nu\) has zero curvature but nonzero torsion:

\[T^a{}_{\mu\nu} = \partial_\mu e^a{}_\nu - \partial_\nu e^a{}_\mu\]

3.2 TEGR ≡ GR Equivalence

Theorem — TEGR Equivalence

The TEGR action differs from the Einstein-Hilbert action by a total divergence:

\[S_{\rm TEGR} = \frac{1}{16\pi G} \int d^4x\, e\, T = S_{\rm EH} + \text{boundary term}\]

The field equations are identical to Einstein's equations. TEGR is GR reformulated in torsion language.
