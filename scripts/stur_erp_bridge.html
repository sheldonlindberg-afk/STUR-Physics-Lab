Result: Biology does not merely use physics — it is physics. Mitochondrial resistance fields are a direct low-energy manifestation of the STUR R-field doublet on M⁴×S¹.

Step 1 — XCRM Lagrangian (STUR Axiom 2)

The real doublet \(\mathbf{R} = (R_1, R_2) \in \mathbb{R}^2\) admits a unique non-vanishing first-derivative coupling (XCRM closure theorem):

\[ \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{XCRM}} = \chi\!\left(R_1\partial_\mu R_2 - R_2\partial_\mu R_1\right)\partial^\mu\varphi = \chi|\mathbf{R}|^2(\partial_\mu\varphi)^2 \]

The second equality uses polar form \(R_1 = \rho\cos\varphi\), \(R_2 = \rho\sin\varphi\), so the antisymmetric bracket equals \(\rho^2 \partial_\mu\varphi\). This coupling is forbidden for a complex singlet (which would require \(|R|^2 = \mathrm{const}\) at the vacuum) and is unique to the real doublet structure of STUR Axiom 2.

Step 2 — Kaluza-Klein Reduction (STUR Axiom 1)

\[ \Phi_R = \oint_{S^1} A^R_\mu\,dx^\mu = J \cdot \ell \]

where \(J\) is the holonomy current and \(\ell = 2\pi L_X\) is the compact circumference. Integrating \(\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{XCRM}}\) over the compact dimension with holonomy boundary conditions yields the effective 4D Lagrangian:

The exponentially-suppressed Kaluza-Klein tower terms are negligible in the biological regime. The holonomy visibility \(V(\Phi_R)\) appears from the wavefunction overlap after compactification.
