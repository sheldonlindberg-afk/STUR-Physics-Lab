9. The Interferometric Signature

9.1 Resistance Holonomy from XCRM

In teleparallel formulation, the natural observable is the resistance holonomy—the accumulated phase from XCRM coupling along a closed path:

\[\color{#fbbf24}{\Phi_R} = \oint_\gamma \color{#22d3ee}{\mathsf{J}_a}\, dx^a = \oint_\gamma \color{#fbbf24}{y\, \partial_a R}\, dx^a\]
(9.1) — Resistance Holonomy

Here \(\Quant{y}\) is the Yukawa coupling strength and \(\XCRM{\mathsf{J}_a = y\,\partial_a R}\) is the resistance current. The holonomy \(\Quant{\Phi_R}\) determines the phase difference between interferometer arms.

9.2 Visibility from Phase Averaging

When resistance fluctuations are stochastic (Markovian limit), the visibility is determined by averaging over the phase distribution:

\[\color{#f472b6}{V} = \left|\langle e^{i\color{#fbbf24}{\Phi_R}} \rangle\right| = \left|\int P(\Phi_R)\, e^{i\Phi_R}\, d\Phi_R\right|\]
(9.2)

For Gaussian-distributed phases (central limit theorem applies when many uncorrelated fluctuations contribute), this becomes:

\[\boxed{\color{#f472b6}{V} = V_0 \exp\!\left(-\frac{\langle\color{#fbbf24}{\Phi_R}^2\rangle}{2}\right)}\]
(9.3) — Gaussian Visibility Law

9.3 Linear Path Limit

For a linear-path interferometer with arm separation \(\Matt{\Delta L}\), the XCRM holonomy variance scales quadratically:

\[\langle\Quant{\Phi_R}^2\rangle = \left(\frac{\Matt{\Delta L}}{\Matt{\ell_{\rm coh}}}\right)^2\]
(9.4)

Substituting into (9.3) gives the fundamental prediction:

\[\boxed{\color{#f472b6}{V(\Delta L)} = V_0 \exp\!\left(-\frac{(\color{#fb923c}{\Delta L})^2}{(\color{#22d3ee}{\ell_{\rm coh}})^2}\right)}\]
(9.5) — The STUR Visibility Prediction
Key Feature: Gaussian in ΔL²

The visibility decays as a Gaussian function of path difference squared. This is not an approximation—it follows necessarily from Gaussian phase averaging of XCRM holonomy. Any deviation from this form falsifies the theory.

10. Coherence Length: Complete Derivation

10.1 XCRM Phase Fluctuations

The R-field (resistance field) in XCRM induces phase randomization through its coupling to matter. When a quantum particle traverses a path, it accumulates a phase shift from the local R-field gradient:

\[\XCRM{\delta\phi(x)} = \Quant{y} \int_{\rm path} \XCRM{\partial_a R(x')}\, dx'^a\]
(10.1) — Phase from R-field gradient

Here \(\Quant{y}\) is the effective matter-R coupling (Yukawa-type), and \(\XCRM{R(x)}\) is the resistance field. Because \(\XCRM{R}\) undergoes stochastic fluctuations in the XCRM substrate, the accumulated phase \(\XCRM{\delta\phi}\) becomes a random variable. For two interferometer arms separated by \(\Matt{\Delta L}\), the differential phase is:

\[\XCRM{\Delta\Phi} = \Quant{y} \left[\int_{\rm arm\,1} \XCRM{\partial_a R}\,dx^a - \int_{\rm arm\,2} \XCRM{\partial_a R}\,dx^a\right]\]
(10.2) — Differential phase between arms

This is the resistance holonomy \(\XCRM{\PhiR}\) introduced in Section 9. The key insight is that R-field fluctuations cause \(\XCRM{\Delta\Phi}\) to vary stochastically across measurement realizations.

10.2 Path Integral Visibility Formula

In interferometry, the visibility (fringe contrast) measures the coherence between the two arms. When the phase difference fluctuates, visibility is determined by averaging over the phase distribution. From the path integral formalism, the visibility is the magnitude of the phase-averaged interference term:

\[\boxed{\Pot{V} = \left|\langle e^{\ii \XCRM{\Delta\Phi}} \rangle\right| = \left|\int P(\Delta\Phi)\, e^{\ii\Delta\Phi}\, \dd\Delta\Phi\right|}\]
(10.3) — Path Integral Visibility Formula

Here \(\langle\cdot\rangle\) denotes ensemble averaging over R-field configurations, and \(P(\Delta\Phi)\) is the probability distribution of the phase difference. This formula is exact—no approximations have been made.

10.3 Gaussian Statistics from R-field Correlations

The central limit theorem applies when the accumulated phase arises from many uncorrelated R-field fluctuations along the interferometer path. For a path of length \(\Matt{L}\) with correlation length \(\XCRM{\xi_R}\), the number of independent fluctuations is \(N \sim L/\xi_R \gg 1\). By the CLT, \(\XCRM{\Delta\Phi}\) approaches a Gaussian distribution:

\[P(\Delta\Phi) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\langle\XCRM{\Delta\Phi}^2\rangle}} \exp\left(-\frac{(\Delta\Phi)^2}{2\langle\XCRM{\Delta\Phi}^2\rangle}\right)\]
(10.4) — Gaussian phase distribution

The variance \(\langle\XCRM{\Delta\Phi}^2\rangle\) is determined by the two-point correlation function of R-field gradients:

\[\langle\XCRM{\Delta\Phi}^2\rangle = \Quant{y}^2 \iint \langle \XCRM{\partial_a R(x)\, \partial_b R(x')} \rangle\, dx^a\,dx'^b\]
(10.5)

For a Gaussian phase distribution, the visibility integral (10.3) evaluates exactly:

\[\boxed{\Pot{V} = V_0 \exp\!\left(-\frac{\langle\XCRM{\Delta\Phi}^2\rangle}{2}\right)}\]
(10.6) — Gaussian Visibility Law

This is a standard result: for Gaussian-distributed phases, visibility decays as the exponential of half the variance.

10.4 Scaling with Path Separation

For a linear-path interferometer where the two arms are separated by distance \(\Matt{\Delta L}\) in the direction transverse to propagation, the phase variance scales as:

\[\langle\XCRM{\Delta\Phi}^2\rangle = \Quant{y}^2 \left(\frac{\Diff{\sigR}}{\XCRM{v}}\right)^2 \frac{(\Matt{\Delta L})^2}{\XCRM{\Lx}^2}\]
(10.7) — Phase variance scaling

where:

  • \(\Diff{\sigR}\) is the RMS R-field fluctuation amplitude
  • \(\XCRM{v}\) is the R-field VEV (vacuum expectation value)
  • \(\XCRM{\Lx}\) is the compactification scale (internal dimension size)
  • \(\Diff{\sigR}/\XCRM{v}\) is the fractional R-field fluctuation

Defining the coherence length \(\Matt{\lcoh}\) as the scale where \(\langle\Delta\Phi^2\rangle = 2\) (i.e., visibility drops to \(V_0/e\)):

\[\boxed{\Matt{\lcoh} = \frac{\sqrt{2}\,\XCRM{\Lx}}{\Quant{y} \times \Diff{\sigR}/\XCRM{v}}}\]
(10.8) — Coherence Length Formula

This is the master equation for STUR's interferometric prediction. The coherence length depends on three parameters: the internal dimension scale \(\XCRM{\Lx}\), the matter-R coupling \(\Quant{y}\), and the fractional R-field fluctuation \(\Diff{\sigR}/\XCRM{v}\).
