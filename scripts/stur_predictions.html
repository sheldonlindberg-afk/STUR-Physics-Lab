9. The Interferometric Signature

9.1 Resistance Holonomy from XCRM

In teleparallel formulation, the natural observable is the resistance holonomy—the accumulated phase from XCRM coupling along a closed path:

\[\color{#fbbf24}{\Phi_R} = \oint_\gamma \color{#22d3ee}{\mathsf{J}_a}\, dx^a = \oint_\gamma \color{#fbbf24}{y\, \partial_a R}\, dx^a\]
(9.1) — Resistance Holonomy

Here \(y\) is the Yukawa coupling strength and \(\mathsf{J}_a = y\,\partial_a R\) is the resistance current. The holonomy \(\Phi_R\) determines the phase difference between interferometer arms.

9.2 Visibility from Phase Averaging

When resistance fluctuations are stochastic (Markovian limit), the visibility is determined by averaging over the phase distribution:

\[\color{#f472b6}{V} = \left|\langle e^{i\color{#fbbf24}{\Phi_R}} \rangle\right| = \left|\int P(\Phi_R)\, e^{i\Phi_R}\, d\Phi_R\right|\]
(9.2)

For Gaussian-distributed phases (central limit theorem applies when many uncorrelated fluctuations contribute), this becomes:

\[\boxed{\color{#f472b6}{V} = V_0 \exp\!\left(-\frac{\langle\color{#fbbf24}{\Phi_R}^2\rangle}{2}\right)}\]
(9.3) — Gaussian Visibility Law

9.3 Linear Path Limit

For a linear-path interferometer with arm separation \(\Delta L\), the XCRM holonomy variance scales quadratically:

\[\langle\Phi_R^2\rangle = \left(\frac{\Delta L}{\ell_{\rm coh}}\right)^2\]
(9.4)

Substituting into (9.3) gives the fundamental prediction:

\[\boxed{\color{#f472b6}{V(\Delta L)} = V_0 \exp\!\left(-\frac{(\color{#fb923c}{\Delta L})^2}{(\color{#22d3ee}{\ell_{\rm coh}})^2}\right)}\]
(9.5) — The STUR Visibility Prediction
Key Feature: Gaussian in ΔL²

The visibility decays as a Gaussian function of path difference squared. This is not an approximation—it follows necessarily from Gaussian phase averaging of XCRM holonomy. Any deviation from this form falsifies the theory.

10. Coherence Length Estimates

10.1 XCRM Closure Relation

The coherence length is determined by the XCRM closure relation:

\[\color{#22d3ee}{\ell_{\rm coh}} = \frac{\sqrt{2}\, \color{#fbbf24}{L_X}}{\color{#4ade80}{\sigma_R}}\]
(10.1)

where \(L_X\) is the proper size of the internal dimension and \(\sigma_R\) is the RMS resistance fluctuation.

10.2 Teleparallel Substrate Estimate

In the teleparallel interpretation, resistance fluctuations are identified with contortion fluctuations:

\[\sigma_R \sim y\, K_{\rm eff}\]
(10.2)

where \(K_{\rm eff}\) is the effective contortion magnitude (units: m⁻¹) and \(y\) is the Yukawa coupling. This gives:

\[\ell_{\rm coh} \sim \frac{1}{y\, K_{\rm eff}}\]
(10.3)

Loop Geometry and Area Scaling

For closed-loop interferometers, the XCRM holonomy scales with the enclosed area:

\[\Phi_R \propto \int_\Sigma \color{#60a5fa}{\mathbb{T}_{ab}}\, dA^{ab} \propto \color{#fb923c}{A_{\rm loop}}\]
(9.6) — Area Scaling

This is analogous to the Aharonov-Bohm effect but for the resistance (torsion) field rather than the electromagnetic potential. The visibility then becomes:

\[V = V_0 \exp\!\left(-\frac{A_{\rm loop}^2}{A_{\rm coh}^2}\right)\]
(9.7) — Loop Visibility

where \(A_{\rm coh}\) is a characteristic coherence area determined by the substrate properties.
