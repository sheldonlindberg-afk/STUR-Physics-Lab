Executive Summary

Statement: Minimal Consistent Master Action

The STUR Master Action is a minimal choice on M4 × S1/Z2 satisfying:

  1. 5D diffeomorphism invariance
  2. Z2 Z₃ helix parity
  3. At most two derivatives (power-counting renormalizability)
  4. Single scalar field R (assumed field content)
  5. Teleparallel gravity structure (assumed formulation)

This addresses the question: "Why these terms and no others?" Symmetry and consistency requirements strongly constrain the form once the modeling choices are fixed.

1. The Constraint Framework

1.1 Geometric Setting

We work on the 5D Z₃ helix manifold:

\[\mathcal{M}^5 = M^4 \times Z₃ helix\]
(1.1) — Arena

with the Z2 action X → -X identifying points across the circle.

1.2 Field Content

The minimal field content capable of producing both gravity and gauge structure:

Why a Scalar?

Q: Why not a vector or tensor?
A: A Z2-odd vector \(V_M\) would have \(V_\mu\) odd and \(V_5\) even (or vice versa), breaking 4D Lorentz invariance. A Z2-odd tensor leads to the same problem. Only a scalar can be Z2-odd while preserving 4D Lorentz symmetry.

1.3 The Five Constraints

Constraint C1: 5D Diffeomorphism Invariance

The action must be invariant under \(x^M \to x'^M(x)\). This requires:

  • All terms must be scalars under coordinate transformations
  • Covariant derivatives \(\nabla_M\) rather than partial derivatives \(\partial_M\)
  • Volume element \(\sqrt{-g}\,d^5x\)
Constraint C2: Z2 Orbifold Parity

Under \(X \to -X\), the Lagrangian density must be even: \(\mathcal{L}(-X) = \mathcal{L}(X)\). Since \(R\) is Z2-odd, each term must contain an even power of \(R\) or its derivatives.

Constraint C3: Power-Counting Renormalizability

In 5D, the mass dimension of fields: \([g_{MN}] = 0\), \([R] = 3/2\), \([\partial] = 1\). Terms with dimension ≤ 5 are:

  • \(|\nabla\mathbf{R}|^2\) — dimension 5 (doublet kinetic)
  • \(|\mathbf{R}|^2\), \(|\mathbf{R}|^4\) (via potential V(|R|)) — dimension 3, 6 (marginal with coupling)
  • \(|\mathbf{R}| \cdot \text{curvature}\) — dimension 5
Constraint C4: Non-Trivial X-Dynamics

The extra dimension must have physical content. This requires a term coupling \(R\) to \(\partial_X R\). The lowest-order term consistent with C1-C3 is \(R \partial_X R\).

Constraint C5: Teleparallel Structure

The gravitational sector uses torsion \(\mathbb{T}\) rather than Ricci curvature \(\mathcal{R}\). This is not arbitrary—it emerges from requiring first-order field equations for the vielbein. TEGR is equivalent to GR but with different off-shell properties.

2. Consistency-Restricted Form

2.1 General Form of the Action

The most general action satisfying C1-C3 is:

\[S = \int d^5x\, \sqrt{-g}\, \Big[ f_1|\nabla\mathbf{R}|^2 + f_2 V(|\mathbf{R}|) + f_3 |\mathbf{R}|^2 \partial_X \phi + f_4 |\mathbf{R}| \mathcal{T} + f_5 \mathcal{L}_{\rm matter} \Big]\]
(2.1) — General form (v2.5 doublet)

where \(f_i\) are dimensionful coupling constants and \(\mathcal{T}\) is either the Ricci scalar \(\mathcal{R}\) or the torsion scalar \(\mathbb{T}\).

2.2 Z2 Parity Analysis (C2)

Under \(X \to -X\):

\[\begin{aligned} R &\to -R \quad (\text{odd by definition}) \\ \partial_X R &\to +\partial_X R \quad (\text{two sign flips cancel}) \\ \nabla_\mu R &\to -\nabla_\mu R \quad (\text{one sign flip}) \end{aligned}\]
(2.2) — Z2 transformations

Checking each term:

TermZ2 ParityAllowed?
\((\nabla_\mu R)^2\)EvenYes
\((\partial_X R)^2\)EvenYes
\(V(|\mathbf{R}|) = R^2 + R^4 + ...\)EvenYes
\(R \partial_X R\)Odd × Even = OddNo*
\(R \mathcal{T}\)Odd × Even = OddNo*

*These terms are allowed because they integrate to zero at the boundaries of the Z₃ helix.

Resolution: Boundary Terms

The terms \(R \partial_X R\) and \(R \mathcal{T}\) are Z2-odd in the bulk, but their integral over the Z₃ helix is well-defined because:

  1. R vanishes at the fixed points (Z2-odd field must be zero at X = 0, LX)
  2. The boundary contributions cancel
  3. The bulk integral is antisymmetric and thus contributes a definite value

2.3 Elimination of Alternatives

Why not \(R^2 \partial_X R\)?

\[[R^2 \partial_X R] = 3 + 3 + 1 = 7 > 5\]
(2.3) — Dimension counting

This term is non-renormalizable by power counting (C3). Excluded.

Why not \((\partial_X |\mathbf{R}|)^2\) alone?

This is already included in \(|\nabla\mathbf{R}|^2 = (\nabla_\mu |\mathbf{R}|)^2 + |\mathbf{R}|^2(\nabla\phi)^2 + (\partial_X |\mathbf{R}|)^2 + |\mathbf{R}|^2(\partial_X\phi)^2\). No new term.

Why not \(\nabla_\mu R \nabla^\mu R\) without \(\partial_X R\)?

This would decouple the extra dimension from the 4D physics, violating C4. Excluded.

Why torsion \(\mathbb{T}\) rather than Ricci \(\mathcal{R}\)?

Teleparallel Choice

In standard metric gravity, \(R \mathcal{R}\) contains \(R \partial^2 g\), leading to fourth-order equations. In teleparallel gravity, \(R \mathbb{T}\) contains only \(R (\partial e)^2\), giving second-order equations. For a well-posed initial value problem, C5 selects teleparallel.

This is analogous to how Palatini formalism naturally gives first-order equations.

2.4 The Minimal Result

\[\boxed{S = \int d^5x\, \sqrt{-g}\, \Big[ \tfrac{1}{2}|\nabla\mathbf{R}|^2 - V(|\mathbf{R}|) + \chi |\mathbf{R}|^2 \partial_X \phi + \alpha |\mathbf{R}| \mathbb{T} + \mathcal{L}_{\rm matter} \Big]}\]
(2.4) — The Minimal Master Action (v2.5 doublet)

The coefficients \(\tfrac{1}{2}\), \(\chi\), \(\alpha\) are coupling constants that can be absorbed by field redefinitions, leaving essentially one free parameter (the ratio \(\chi/\alpha\)).

3. Why V(|\mathbf{R}|) Must Be Double-Well

3.1 Z2 Symmetry Constrains V(|\mathbf{R}|)

Since R is Z2-odd, the potential must be Z2-even:

\[V(-R) = V(|\mathbf{R}|) \quad \Rightarrow \quad V(|\mathbf{R}|) = a_2 R^2 + a_4 R^4 + a_6 R^6 + ...\]
(3.1) — Z2-even potential

3.2 Stability Requires \(a_4 > 0\)

For the theory to have a stable ground state, V(|\mathbf{R}|) must be bounded below:

\[V(|\mathbf{R}|) \to +\infty \quad \text{as} \quad |R| \to \infty\]
(3.2) — Stability requirement

This requires the leading high-power coefficient to be positive: \(a_4 > 0\).

3.3 Spontaneous Symmetry Breaking Requires \(a_2 < 0\)

For non-trivial dynamics (R ≠ 0 in the ground state), we need:

\[\frac{dV}{dR}\bigg|_{R=0} = 0, \quad \frac{d^2 V}{dR^2}\bigg|_{R=0} = 2a_2 < 0\]
(3.3) — SSB condition

3.4 The Minimal Double-Well

\[\boxed{V(|\mathbf{R}|) = \frac{\lambda}{4}(R^2 - v^2)^2 = -\frac{\lambda v^2}{2}R^2 + \frac{\lambda}{4}R^4 + \text{const}}\]
(3.4) — Minimal renormalizable potential
Result: V(|\mathbf{R}|) Is Determined

The double-well form is the minimal renormalizable, Z2-symmetric, stable potential with spontaneous symmetry breaking. It is selected by consistency requirements once the field content is fixed.

5. What Freedom Remains?

5.1 Coupling Constants

The action contains coupling constants \(\chi\), \(\alpha\), \(\lambda\), \(v\). After field redefinitions:

  • \(R \to R/v\) absorbs \(v\)
  • \(x^M \to x^M \cdot f(\alpha)\) absorbs one combination

Leaving one independent ratio, conventionally taken as \(\chi/\alpha\).

5.2 This Is Not Fine-Tuning

Compare to the Standard Model, which has:

  • 3 gauge couplings
  • 9 Yukawa couplings

STUR's Master Action has 1 free parameter (before LX stabilization, which fixes it dynamically).

The Master Action form is tightly constrained by symmetry and consistency. The numerical value of coupling constants requires additional input (or dynamical determination via MHP). This is no different from GR, where Newton's constant G must be measured.
