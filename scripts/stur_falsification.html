12.3 Recommended Experimental Protocol

Step 1: Baseline Measurement

Measure visibility \(\Matt{V}\) at multiple arm separations \(\Matt{\Delta L_1, \Delta L_2, \ldots, \Delta L_n}\) spanning at least one decade.

Step 2: Functional Form Test

Plot \(\ln(\Matt{V}/\Matt{V_0})\) versus \(\Matt{\Delta L}^2\). STUR predicts a straight line with slope \(-1/\Matt{\ell_{\rm coh}}^2\).

\[\ln\!\left(\frac{\Matt{V}}{\Matt{V_0}}\right) = -\frac{\Matt{\Delta L}^2}{\Matt{\ell_{\rm coh}}^2} \quad\Rightarrow\quad \text{linear in } \Matt{\Delta L}^2\]
(12.1) — Linearized Test

Step 3: Time-Series Check

At fixed \(\Matt{\Delta L}\), measure visibility over time. STUR predicts no systematic temporal variation (only statistical noise).

Step 4: Mass Independence Check

Repeat measurements with different particle masses (e.g., neutrons vs atoms). STUR predicts identical visibility curves when normalized to the same \(\Matt{\Delta L}\).

Step 5: Loop Geometry Test

For loop interferometers, verify that visibility scales with enclosed area squared, not linear path length.
