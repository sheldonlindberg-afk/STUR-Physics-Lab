12. Falsifiability Statement

Core Falsifiability Claim

STUR makes exactly one non-negotiable prediction: the Gaussian form of visibility decay.

The theory predicts:

\[\boxed{V(\Delta L) = V_0 \exp\!\left(-\frac{\Delta L^2}{\ell_{\rm coh}^2}\right)}\]

This functional form is not adjustable. Only the parameter \(\ell_{\rm coh}\) can vary. The Gaussian dependence on \(\Delta L^2\) follows necessarily from XCRM holonomy averaging under the central limit theorem.

12.1 Decision Criteria

STUR Consistent
  • Visibility decays as Gaussian in \(\Delta L^2\)
  • No temporal oscillation at fixed \(\Delta L\)
  • Mass-independent at fixed shot time
  • Area scaling for loop geometries
  • Single coherence length fits all data
STUR Falsified
  • Visibility linear in \(\Delta L\) (not quadratic)
  • Sinusoidal temporal modulation
  • Mass-dependent visibility at fixed \(\Delta L\)
  • Exponential (not Gaussian) decay
  • Multiple coherence lengths required

12.3 Recommended Experimental Protocol

Step 1: Baseline Measurement

Measure visibility \(V\) at multiple arm separations \(\Delta L_1, \Delta L_2, \ldots, \Delta L_n\) spanning at least one decade.

Step 2: Functional Form Test

Plot \(\ln(V/V_0)\) versus \(\Delta L^2\). STUR predicts a straight line with slope \(-1/\ell_{\rm coh}^2\).

\[\ln\!\left(\frac{V}{V_0}\right) = -\frac{\Delta L^2}{\ell_{\rm coh}^2} \quad\Rightarrow\quad \text{linear in } \Delta L^2\]
(12.1) — Linearized Test

Step 3: Time-Series Check

At fixed \(\Delta L\), measure visibility over time. STUR predicts no systematic temporal variation (only statistical noise).

Step 4: Mass Independence Check

Repeat measurements with different particle masses (e.g., neutrons vs atoms). STUR predicts identical visibility curves when normalized to the same \(\Delta L\).

Step 5: Loop Geometry Test

For loop interferometers, verify that visibility scales with enclosed area squared, not linear path length.

12.4 What STUR Cannot Predict

Phenomenological Inputs

STUR does not predict:

  • The numerical value of \(\ell_{\rm coh}\) (must be measured)
  • The specific substrate (UV completion) producing the fluctuations
  • Standard Model parameters (gauge group, masses, mixings)
  • Whether the coherence scale is accessible to current experiments

These limitations do not compromise falsifiability. The form of the visibility law is fixed by the axioms; only the scale is a free parameter. A single measurement of \(\ell_{\rm coh}\) at one \(\Delta L\) predicts visibility at all other separations.

11. Current Experimental Constraints

11.1 Existing Data

Current matter-wave interferometry experiments (neutron, atom, molecule) place lower bounds on the coherence length:

Experiment Particle \(\Delta L_{\rm max}\) Constraint
Neutron interferometry Neutron ∼ 10 cm \(\ell_{\rm coh} > 10\) cm
Atom interferometry Rb, Cs ∼ 1 m \(\ell_{\rm coh} > 1\) m
Molecule interferometry C₆₀, larger ∼ 1 mm \(\ell_{\rm coh} > 1\) mm
Interpretation

Current null results are consistent with STUR if \(\ell_{\rm coh} \gtrsim 1\) m. Smaller coherence lengths would have produced observable visibility decay in existing experiments. This sets a lower bound but does not falsify the theory.

The Path Forward

STUR provides a falsifiable target for experimental physics. The theory stands or falls on the Gaussian visibility prediction. Either experiments will confirm this form (determining \(\ell_{\rm coh}\)), or they will falsify STUR by revealing a different functional dependence.
