5. Gauge Sector: Derived from XCRM Degeneracy

XCRM Closure: Gauge Structure

The Yang-Mills structure is derived from XCRM, not postulated. The XCRM holonomy constraint defines equivalence classes of resistance configurations. When this degeneracy forms a continuous group, gauge symmetry emerges necessarily.

5.1 XCRM Symmetry and Observables

Core Definition — XCRM Symmetry

Physical observables are invariant under local reparameterizations of internal resistance-coherence flow that preserve closed-loop holonomy:

\[R(x,X) \;\longrightarrow\; R'(x,X') \quad \text{such that} \quad \oint_{\mathcal C} \mathsf{J}_a\, dx^a \;\text{ is invariant}\]
(5.1) — XCRM Invariance

This definition immediately implies three fundamental consequences:

X is Unobservable The internal coordinate \(X\) cannot be directly measured — only its effects through holonomies.
Holonomies are Observable Only closed-loop integrals of the resistance current are physical observables.
Redundancy = Gauge Symmetry Any redundancy in describing \(R\) that preserves holonomy defines a gauge transformation.

5.2 The Degeneracy Manifold

The XCRM holonomy \(\Phi_R = \oint \mathsf{J}_a\, dx^a\) is the only observable. Any two resistance configurations \(R\) and \(R'\) yielding the same holonomy are physically indistinguishable. This defines the degeneracy manifold:

\[\color{#a78bfa}{\Dcal} = \big\{ R \mid \Phi_R[\mathcal C] = \text{const for all } \mathcal{C} \big\}\]
(5.2) — Degeneracy manifold

Local changes of basis in \(\Dcal\) are physically invisible. If this degeneracy forms a continuous group, we have:

\[\boxed{R^I(x) \;\rightarrow\; U^I{}_J(x)\,R^J(x) \quad\text{with}\quad U(x)\in G}\]
(5.3) — Local degeneracy transformation

5.3 Why Yang-Mills Is Forced

The Derivation Chain

Step 1 Define \(\Dcal = \{ R \mid \Phi_R[\mathcal{C}] = \text{const for all } \mathcal{C} \}\).
Step 2 If \(\Dcal\) has continuous local symmetry \(G\), then \(R^I \to U^I{}_J(x) R^J\) with \(U \in G\).
Step 3 Parallel transport of degeneracy labels requires a connection: \(\mathcal{A}_\mu = A^I_\mu T_I\).
Step 4 Covariance forces Yang-Mills transformation law.

Requiring invariance under the degeneracy transformation:

\[R \to UR, \quad \mathcal A_\mu \to U\mathcal A_\mu U^{-1} + U\partial_\mu U^{-1}\]
(5.4) — Yang-Mills transformation law

forces a Yang-Mills structure. This is not optional — it is a standard theorem in gauge theory (see Aldrovandi & Pereira 2013, Chapter 12).

This is XCRM Closure

Gauge fields are not added to the theory — they emerge from the requirement that XCRM holonomies are the only observables. The connection \(\mathcal{A}_\mu\) is forced by the need to parallel-transport degeneracy labels consistently.

5.4 XCRM Constraints on Gauge Groups

Constraint 1 — Compactness

The resistance kinetic term must be positive definite: \(\int (\nabla R)^2 \ge 0\). This requires the degeneracy metric \(\kappa_{IJ}\) to be positive definite, admitting only compact Lie groups: SU(N), SO(N), U(1).

Constraint 2 — Holonomy Nontriviality

If \(G\) is purely Abelian, then \(\Phi_R = \oint \partial_a\theta\,dx^a = 0\) unless singularities are inserted by hand. Therefore, at least one non-Abelian factor is required to generate nontrivial resistance holonomy.

Constraint 3 — Minimality + Chirality

The theory must: (a) allow chiral fermion localization (enforced via orbifolding), (b) support nontrivial loop holonomy, (c) avoid additional unobserved long-range forces. The smallest compact group satisfying all three constraints is:

\[\boxed{SU(3) \times SU(2) \times U(1)}\]
(5.5) — Minimal viable gauge group
Complete XCRM Derivation

The Standard Model gauge group is uniquely selected by XCRM constraints. The combination of compactness, holonomy nontriviality, chirality + minimality, and the requirement of anomaly cancellation leaves exactly one viable gauge group.

5.5 Dimensional Reduction

The five-dimensional gauge coupling \(g_5\) reduces to the four-dimensional coupling via integration over the internal dimension:

\[\boxed{\color{#60a5fa}{g_4^2} = \frac{\color{#60a5fa}{g_5^2}}{\color{#fbbf24}{L_X}}}\]
(5.6) — Dimensional reduction of gauge coupling

This relation connects the observable 4D gauge coupling to the fundamental 5D coupling and the compactification scale. Since \(L_X\) is the single remaining free parameter in XCRM, all gauge couplings are determined once \(L_X\) is fixed experimentally.

5.7 Derivation of σᵣ from XCRM Equilibrium

Theorem 5.1 — Fluctuation Amplitude from XCRM

The RMS resistance fluctuation \(\sigma_R\) is not a free parameter — it is determined by XCRM equilibrium dynamics.

The effective 4D potential from X-integration is \(V_{\rm eff}(\bar{R}) = V(\bar{R}) + \frac{\chi^2}{2L_X^2}(\bar{R} - R_{\rm bg})^2\). At thermal equilibrium with the XCRM bath, the fluctuation amplitude is determined by the equipartition theorem:

\[\boxed{\color{#4ade80}{\sigma_R^2} = \frac{T_{\rm eff}}{m_{\rm eff}^2} = \frac{\color{#fbbf24}{L_X^2}}{\color{#fbbf24}{\chi^2}} \cdot T_{\rm eff}}\]
(5.7) — σᵣ from XCRM equilibrium

where \(T_{\rm eff}\) is the effective temperature of the XCRM bath (set by KK mode occupation). Substituting into the coherence length formula:

\[\color{#22d3ee}{\ell_{\rm coh}} = \frac{\sqrt{2}\, \color{#fbbf24}{L_X}}{\color{#4ade80}{\sigma_R}} = \frac{\sqrt{2}\, \color{#fbbf24}{\chi}}{\sqrt{T_{\rm eff}}}\]
(5.8) — Coherence length determined by XCRM coupling
XCRM Closure: Coherence Length

Result: The coherence length is determined by XCRM dynamics: \(\ell_{\rm coh} = \sqrt{2}\chi/\sqrt{T_{\rm eff}}\). Once \(\chi\) and \(T_{\rm eff}\) are fixed by \(L_X\) (via dimensional reduction), the coherence length is determined.
