Neurons are the most energy-demanding cells in the body. An action potential requires the Na⁺/K⁺ ATPase to restore ionic gradients — consuming ~4.5 μmol ATP/g/min at rest (10× muscle). Synaptic transmission accounts for 30–70% of total neuronal energy budget. Because neurons are post-mitotic (cannot divide), mitochondrial dysfunction accumulates irreversibly — making éR the existential parameter.

Neurodegenerative diseases progressively impair mitochondrial function. In Alzheimer's, Aβ oligomers disrupt membrane potential (f ↓), while tau tangles randomize holonomy (Φ_R ↑). In Parkinson's, α-synuclein aggregates block mitochondrial import in dopaminergic neurons — which fire tonically at 3–5 Hz and are uniquely vulnerable. In ALS, motor neurons with ℓ_axon up to 1 m are most exposed to holonomy drift.

STUR holonomy Φ_R = J · ℓ_axon encodes why long-axon neurons fail first. Motor neurons extend up to 1 m from spinal cord to foot — their holonomy is 1000× that of a 1 mm interneuron at the same J. V(Φ_R) = exp(−Φ_R²/2) decays rapidly: even modest J fluctuations cause catastrophic éR elevation in motor neurons, explaining the selective vulnerability seen in ALS.
