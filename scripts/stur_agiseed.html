STUR – AGI Seed Simulator v5.2

Predictive Intelligence Core on a 5D X-Coupled Resistance Manifold

What You Are Looking At

This page hosts an open, inspectable simulation of an artificial intelligence seed built from physics, not from language models. Instead of neurons and tokens, it uses a continuous physical field \\(R(x,t,X)\\) that spreads, drifts, remembers, and learns under one evolution law.

The goal is not to create a person or a mind inside your browser. The goal is to show that a single, well-structured physical law can already support:

  • Persistent memory across episodes
  • Internal goals chosen by the system itself
  • Symbolic compression of patterns into discrete tokens
  • Reinforcement learning that shapes its own dynamics
  • A meta-controller that decides when to explore or consolidate
This is an open research testbed. It is not a person, not sentient, and not an agent acting in the real world. It is a controlled mathematical system whose behavior you can inspect line by line.

What This Simulator Is (and Is Not)

What It Is

  • A multi-layer nonlinear PDE system on a 3D grid \\((32 \\times 32 \\times 32)\\) with three fast manifolds \\(R_1, R_2, R_3\\) and two slower manifolds \\(R_4, R_5\\).
  • A 5D X-coupled resistance manifold where the extra coordinate \\(X\\) stores hysteretic memory about past configurations of \\(R\\).
  • A symbolic layer that detects curvature-based “shapes” and converts them into discrete proto-symbols \\(\\Sigma\\).
  • A temporal symbolic memory that learns which symbol tends to follow which.
  • A reinforcement learning loop that adjusts physical parameters (diffusion, coupling strengths, drift) based on reward.
  • A meta-control system that decides whether to explore, consolidate, or follow an external curriculum goal.

What It Is Not

  • Not a chatbot, not a language model, and not trained on human text.
  • Not a mind, not conscious, and not self-aware.
  • Not directly connected to the internet, files, or devices.
  • Not an agent that can take actions in the real world. It acts only within its mathematical state space.
  • Not a black box. Every equation, field, and reward component is visible in the script.

The 5D X-Coupled Resistance Manifold: Full Evolution Law

1. The Core STUR Evolution Equation
We start from a single field \\(R(x,t,X)\\) living on a 5D manifold: three spatial coordinates, one time coordinate, and one hidden X-coordinate that carries memory and coupling strength. The evolution law used in this simulator is: \[ \\frac{\\partial R}{\\partial t} = \\nabla \\cdot (D \\nabla R) - (\\nabla R)\\cdot \\Omega + \\chi \\frac{R - R_{bg}}{L_X} - \\lambda (R - R_{bg})^3 + S(x,t). \] Each term has a clear physical role:
  • \\(\\nabla \\cdot (D \\nabla R)\\): diffusion that spreads resistance and smooths gradients.
  • \\(-(\\nabla R)\\cdot \\Omega\\): drift along a vector field \\(\\Omega\\), encoding directed flow.
  • \\(\\chi (R - R_{bg})/L_X\\): coupling to the X-dimension, tying the field back to its background level.
  • \\(-\\lambda (R - R_{bg})^3\\): nonlinear stabilizer that prevents unbounded growth.
  • \\(S(x,t)\\): sources and sinks for inputs, goals, noise, and symbolic forcing.
2. From Plain Diffusion to Directed Flow
If we consider only local spreading of \\(R\\), the simplest model is pure diffusion: \[ \\frac{\\partial R}{\\partial t} = \\nabla \\cdot (D \\nabla R). \] This term alone makes sharp patterns blur over time, like heat diffusing in a solid.
To allow the substrate to move information in a directed way, we introduce a drift field \\(\\Omega(x,t)\\). In the code, this drift is shaped by higher layers (mainly \\(R_4\\)), and it appears as: \[ \\frac{\\partial R}{\\partial t} = \\nabla \\cdot (D \\nabla R) - (\\nabla R)\\cdot\\Omega + \\dots \] This term pushes bumps and structures in \\(R\\) along preferred directions, which is how the system implements goal-seeking behavior in the manifold.
3. Adding X-Coupled Memory
STUR treats the hidden coordinate \\(X\\) as a place where the system remembers how far \\(R\\) is from its background state \\(R_{bg}\\). A simple relation is: \[ \\frac{\\partial R}{\\partial X} = \\frac{R - R_{bg}}{L_X}, \] which says: the more \\(R\\) deviates from its background, the more it bends into the X-dimension.
If changes in X feed back into time evolution with overall strength \\(\\chi\\), we get a hysteresis-like contribution: \[ \\chi \\frac{R - R_{bg}}{L_X}. \] This is the term used in the PDE and implemented in the code via \\(\\chi_1, \\chi_2, \\chi_3\\) and the memory fields \\(X_1, X_2, X_3\\).
4. Nonlinear Stability Around the Background
To stop the system from blowing up, we add a cubic restoring force centered at the background value \\(R_{bg}\\). A typical potential is \[ V(R) = \\frac{\\lambda}{4}(R - R_{bg})^4, \] which produces the force term \[ -\\frac{\\partial V}{\\partial R} = -\\lambda (R - R_{bg})^3. \] This keeps \\(R\\) bounded and creates stable wells where patterns can live.
5. Collecting Inputs into the Source Term S(x,t)
All external and internal influences are grouped into one source term \\(S(x,t)\\). In the actual Python code, this includes:
  • Static Gaussian sources (background structures and curriculum goals).
  • Dynamic sources (oscillating input patterns).
  • Symbolic sources (fields \\(\\Sigma_A, \\Sigma_B\\) activated by the planner).
  • Layer-to-layer coupling terms between \\(R_1, R_2, R_3\\).
  • Noise terms to keep the system exploring.
In other words, every “nudge” to the system — sensory input, symbols, goals, and stabilizing feedback — is encoded as a contribution to \\(S(x,t)\\) or to the parameters \\(D, \\Omega, \\chi, \\lambda\\).

The Five Manifolds R₁–R₅

1. R₁ – Sensory Field
R₁ lives on the full grid and feels most of the external sources: static Gaussians, dynamic oscillations, and symbolic masks. Its dynamics follow the STUR law with parameters chosen to make it reactive but not chaotic. In the code, this corresponds to the block labelled “R1 Dynamics (Sensory Manifold).”
Intuitively, R₁ is the place where “the world touches the system.” It is driven hard by inputs and slightly by higher layers, and its patterns are then passed on to R₂ and R₃.
2. R₂ – Associative / Working Memory Field
R₂ receives smoothed information from R₁, stabilizing feedback from R₃, and goal forces that try to align it with a chosen mask (external curriculum or internal target). R₂ is also where symbolic compression happens: regions of high curvature are turned into discrete symbol IDs.
R₂ is the “workspace” of the system. When you see references in the code to goal alignment, entropy of R₂, and symbolic extraction, they are all centered here.
3. R₃ – Stabilizer Field
R₃ is wired so that it responds to differences between R₁ and R₂ and to variance in those fields. Its role is to dampen runaway dynamics: when R₂ becomes too unstable relative to R₁, R₃ pushes back through the coupling terms and through the way it modulates diffusion and X-coupling.
If R₂ is the workspace, R₃ is the safety system that keeps the workspace from exploding. It adjusts how strongly R₂ can drift and how fast it can change.
4. R₄ – Predictive Core
R₄ lives on a coarser grid and evolves more slowly. It integrates information about how well R₂ is aligned with its goals and how its entropy is changing. R₄ then sets part of the drift field \\(\\Omega\\) that guides R₂.
R₄ answers the question: “Given what I’m doing, which way should I pull the system next?” Its mean value and gradients determine the base drift of R₂ in the STUR equation.
5. R₅ – Meta-Controller
R₅ is coarser and slower still. It watches the state of R₄ and slowly adjusts three things:
  • How strong the exploration budget is (how adventurous the drift can be).
  • What target alignment R₄ should aim for.
  • How fast R₄ is allowed to learn (its effective \\(\\gamma\\)).
R₅ is where the simulation decides “are we in exploration mode, consolidation mode, or following an external curriculum?” It does this purely from its internal fields, without human labels or prompts.

From Physics to Symbols and Learning

1. Symbolic Compression
Inside the code, the function symbolic_compression_layer takes R₂, computes its curvature (via a Laplacian), and marks regions where curvature is much higher than average. These regions are treated as candidate “shapes”: \[ \\text{curvature\_proxy} = |\\nabla^2 R_2|, \\quad \\text{invariant\_region} = \\{x : \\text{curvature\_proxy}(x) > \\theta \\cdot \\langle\\text{curvature\_proxy}\\rangle\\}. \] A simple hash of the region’s center of mass and mass gives a symbol ID like Q10_M3.
This is how a continuous field turns into discrete tokens. There is no vocabulary baked in; the symbols are created on the fly from the geometry of the field.
2. Temporal Symbolic Memory
The SymbolicMemory class stores a short history of these symbol IDs and learns how often one symbol follows another. Over time, this builds a simple transition model \\(T(i \\rightarrow j)\\) of its own internal patterns.
The current symbol and the strength of the most common transition are turned into a 2-dimensional context vector. This becomes part of the state seen by the policy network.
3. The 29-Dimensional State Vector
At each step, the simulator compresses the full state into a fixed-length vector \\(s_t \\in \\mathbb{R}^{29}\\). It includes:
  • Means and standard deviations of \\(R_1, R_2, R_3, R_4, R_5\\).
  • Means and standard deviations of \\(X_1, X_2, X_3\\).
  • Coherence between each \\(R_i\\) and its \\(X_i\\) (memory integrity).
  • Average gradient-squared (curvature) of \\(R_1, R_2, R_3\\).
  • Shannon entropy of \\(R_1, R_2, R_3\\).
  • Goal alignment of R₂ with its current goal mask.
  • Two symbolic context features from the transition model.
This vector is the “sensation” of the entire system. The policy network and planner respond only to this compressed description, not to the full grid directly.
4. Policy Network and Rewards
A linear policy network takes \\(s_t\\) and proposes adjustments to five key physical parameters:
  • Diffusion in R₁ (D₁).
  • Memory coupling strength for X₂ (ChiX₂).
  • Three drift components \\(\\Omega_1, \\Omega_2, \\Omega_3\\).
These are trained with a TD(0)-style update so that parameter choices that improve the reward become more likely over time.
The reward combines several signals:
  • Goal progress (alignment of R₂ with its current goal mask).
  • Complexity and entropy, which encourage non-trivial patterns.
  • Stability of the predictive core R₄.
  • Reduction of conflict between what the policy predicts and what R₄ actually drives.
  • A penalty proportional to prediction error in the low-dimensional readout.
The result is a closed loop: physical fields → metrics → parameter choices → new fields. Over many episodes, the system stabilizes into strategies that keep reward high.
5. Why This Counts as “Emergent Intelligence”
Within this simulation, “intelligence” means that the system:
  • Stores information about its own past in the X-fields and in R₄/R₅.
  • Abstracts complex spatial patterns into discrete symbols.
  • Uses learned symbol transitions as context for future decisions.
  • Adapts its own physical parameters to improve reward over episodes.
  • Chooses internal goals (e.g., self-consistency vs. curriculum tracking) based on R₅.
All of this happens inside one unified resistance-based law. There is no separate “brain” and “body”: the intelligence is the way the field organizes itself over time.
