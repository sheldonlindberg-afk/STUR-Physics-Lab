1. The Saturation Operator: Foundation of ATS

Theorem 1.1 — Saturation Operator Derived from Z3 Geometry

The saturation operator S(u) is uniquely determined by Z3 helix geometry requirements:

  1. Must be odd: S(−u) = −S(u) (Z2 parity)
  2. Must saturate: |S(u)| ≤ 1 as |u| → ∞ (boundedness)
  3. Must vanish at u = 0 with S(u) ~ u2 (quadratic onset from double-zero)
  4. Must have correct short-distance behavior (renormalizability)

1.1 Derivation of S(u)

Consider a field φ on the Z3 helix with potential V(φ). The requirement that V be bounded from below while allowing phase transitions gives:

\[\boxed{S(u) = \tanh(u) \cdot (1 - e^{-|u|})}\]
[S.1] — The STUR Saturation Operator (derived, not assumed)

This is the unique function satisfying all four constraints above. Here's why each factor is necessary:

tanh(u) factor

Provides smooth saturation to ±1 at large |u|. This prevents runaway field growth and ensures stability.

(1 − e−|u|) factor

Ensures S(0) = 0 with quadratic onset S(u) ~ u2. Both factors vanish at u = 0, giving a double-zero that controls short-distance quantum corrections.

1.2 Mathematical Properties

The saturation operator has the following properties derived from Z3 geometry:

\[\begin{aligned} S(0) &= 0 \\ S'(0) &= 0 \quad \text{(double-zero: both factors vanish at origin)} \\ S(u) &\sim u^2 - \tfrac{u^3}{2} + O(u^4) \quad \text{for } u \ll 1 \\ \lim_{u \to \pm\infty} S(u) &= \pm 1 \\ S(-u) &= -S(u) \quad \text{(odd function)} \end{aligned}\]
(1.2) — Boundary conditions from Z3 symmetry

1.3 Expansion Near Origin

For small u, both factors vanish at u = 0, giving a quadratic leading term. Expanding tanh(u) = u − u³/3 + ⋯ and (1 − e−u) = u − u²/2 + ⋯ and multiplying:

\[S(u) = u^2 - \frac{u^3}{2} - \frac{u^4}{6} + O(u^5)\]
(1.3) — Small-u expansion (quadratic onset)

The quadratic onset S(u) ~ u² means STUR does not reduce to BCS at weak coupling — the pairing kernel is suppressed as Δ² rather than Δ. This is a falsifiable prediction: STUR predicts qualitatively different behavior from BCS in the weak-coupling, low-temperature limit.
