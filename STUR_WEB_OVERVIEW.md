## The Big Picture

### The Flavor Problem

One of the great mysteries of physics is the "flavor problem." The Standard Model has 19 free parameters---numbers we measure but cannot explain. Most of these describe the masses and mixings of fermions (quarks and leptons). Why are there three copies of everything? Why does the top quark weigh 85,000 times more than the up quark? The Standard Model offers no answers.

### A Geometric Solution

STUR proposes that our universe has a tiny extra dimension---too small to see directly, but large enough to matter for particle physics. This dimension isn't just a line or a circle; it's twisted into a structure with three-fold symmetry, like a triple helix.

In this picture:
- **Each generation of fermions lives at a different point** along the helix
- **Mass differences arise from overlap** between fermion locations and the Higgs field
- **Mixing between generations** comes from the geometric misalignment between different types of fermions

This is similar to how different notes on a guitar string come from where you place your finger. The geometry creates the physics.

### Q: Is this a "Theory of Everything"?

**A:** In the sense of attempting to derive Standard Model and cosmological parameters from

observables are fully derived (D, <20% from PDG), 3 have an identified mechanism with

At the EFT level, the derivation chain is closed for Standard Model parameters and cosmological quantities. UV embedding beyond the EFT remains a longer-term direction, but does not affect the closed low-energy predictions. It should be understood as an "effective field theory"---a framework valid below a certain energy scale.

### Q: How does STUR differ from string theory?

**A:** String theory is a framework for quantum gravity that typically requires 10 or 11 dimensions. STUR works in 5 dimensions and focuses specifically on explaining Standard Model structure (generations, masses, mixing). It could potentially be embedded in string theory, but this has not been proven. STUR is more concrete and makes more specific predictions, but string theory is more ambitious in scope.

### Q: Has STUR been experimentally tested?

**A:** STUR's predictions for CKM parameters, the Higgs mass, and three generations all agree with current measurements. However, these could be coincidences. The key tests will be:
- **Fifth-force experiments** at the micrometer scale (ARIADNE experiment)
