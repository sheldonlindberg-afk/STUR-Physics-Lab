On Validation

Peer review serves an important function in science: it filters incremental contributions, catches errors, and maintains standards within established paradigms. This project does not seek to bypass that process out of disrespect—rather, history suggests that final analysis has never moved the needle on paradigm-level theories.

Einstein's relativity was not validated by committee approval—it was validated by Eddington's eclipse observations. Boltzmann's statistical mechanics was rejected by his peers until experiments confirmed atomic reality. Continental drift was dismissed for decades until seafloor spreading provided material evidence. The pattern is consistent: paradigm shifts require empirical demonstration, not institutional consensus.

STUR seeks validation through three channels:

  • Material Synthesis — Laboratory realization of STUR-predicted phenomena, including the ambient-temperature superconductor (ATS) protocol and resistance-field materials
  • Formal Lab Falsification — Interferometric tests at facilities like MAGIS-100 and AION to confirm or exclude the Gaussian visibility prediction
  • Computational Verification (AGI) — Independent derivation and consistency checking by advanced AI systems capable of rigorous mathematical analysis

If STUR is correct, material synthesis will produce results. If it is wrong, the falsification protocol specifies exactly how experiments will exclude it. These are the standards by which paradigm-level claims should be judged.

The STUR framework was developed in public view through open dialogue and iterative refinement. Open-source science is not rebellion—it is transparency. Truth strengthens when everyone has the tools to test it.
