So the round-1 result **survives on the orbifold**: k_eff = k₀ + ½ sgn M ∈ ℤ, β = k_eff α/2, and k = 1 is minimal. It now carries two extra stated assumptions: the Z₂ structure of the matter fields, and the global form of the gauge group.

## 1. Set-up and Z₂ assignments

The fundamental domain is y ∈ [0, πR], and the covering circle has length 2πR.

- **U(1)_B:** B_μ is odd and B₅ is even. There is no 4D B photon, and θ = ∮B₅ dy over the covering circle survives.
- **Photon:** it is in the bulk (R1.4), with A_μ even (giving the 4D photon) and A₅ odd (no EM Wilson line).
- **Matter:** a Z₂-odd U(1) acts on matter like charge conjugation. The Z₂ maps q_B → −q_B while keeping Q_EM fixed, because A_μ is even. So **a single Dirac field cannot carry q_B ≠ 0 and Q ≠ 0 at once.** The minimal content is a pair: ψ₁ with (q_B, Q) = (+1, 1) and ψ₂ with (−1, 1), related by

  ψ₂(x, y) = γ₅ ψ₁(x, −y).

**Masses (check O1).** Since ψ̄₂ψ₂(y) = −ψ̄₁ψ₁(−y), Z₂ invariance requires M₂ = −M₁ for constant masses. This is allowed because ψ₁ and ψ₂ are different fields, so no odd kink mass M ε(y) is needed. The kinetic term with D₅, and the couplings to B_μ and A_μ, map consistently (checked numerically with explicit Dirac matrices). The independent degrees of freedom are therefore ψ₁ on the whole covering circle, with a constant mass M, charges (1, 1), Wilson line θ, and **no chiral zero mode**. Its KK masses are M + i(n + θ/2π)/R, exactly the spectrum used in `stur_final.py` and R1.1 (with r = R).

A kink mass M ε(y) would arise for a *single* Z₂-projected field. Such a field must be B-neutral, so it cannot contribute to the θ-term. The round-1 concern is therefore resolved: the covering-circle picture is the correct one.

**Scalar.** The neutral scalar φ (q_B = 1, Q = 0) has two options:
- a Z₂ pair, equivalent to one complex scalar on the covering circle (2 real d.o.f. per KK level, as assumed in `stur_final.py`); or
- a single field with φ(−y) = φ*(y), which is allowed since Q = 0 (1 real d.o.f. per level).

The second option moves the trigger from H_c = 15.06/L, T_c = 9.78/L to **H_c = 16.71/L, T_c = 10.75/L** (check O5). The trigger mechanism survives either way.

## 2. θ-dependence of the induced θFF̃ coefficient

Since the pair is one covering-circle field, R1.1 applies unchanged:

c_g(θ) = ½ sinh(2πMR) / (cosh(2πMR) − cos θ),  and the integral of c_g over one period is π sgn M.

**New exact result (check O4).** The spectrum is symmetric under (n, θ) → (−n, −θ), so Φ(θ) − Φ(0) is odd in θ. Combined with Φ(θ + 2π) = Φ(θ) + π sgn M, this gives

**Φ(π) − Φ(0) = (π/2) sgn M exactly, for every MR.**

The roll θ: 0 → π therefore gives ΔΘ_eff = κπ + (π/2) sgn M = k_eff π, with **no e^(−2πMR) correction**. The only deviation of β from k_eff α/2 is that θ has not fully settled at π (the "lands/π" column of `stur_final.py`).

Caveat †: a brane mass mixing of ψ with an SM lepton at y = 0 (section E) could in principle break the reflection symmetry. It cannot change the per-period winding π sgn M, which is topological as long as no mode becomes massless.

## 3. Anomaly inflow, fixed points and the bare level

**Fixed-point anomalies.** On orbifolds, a bulk fermion's anomaly is localised at the fixed points and split evenly between them [11]. A bulk Chern–Simons term generated by massive fermions moves anomalies between the fixed points [11, N3]. Localised anomalies can survive even when no zero modes do [N4, N5].

For our pair, the Z₂ acts by exchanging ψ₁ and ψ₂, an off-diagonal matrix P = σ₁ in flavour space. The fixed-point anomaly coefficients involve traces with P inserted:
- tr(P q_B Q²) = 0 and tr(P Q³) = 0, because P is off-diagonal and the charge matrices are diagonal;
- the U(1)_B gauge parameter vanishes at y = 0 and is ±1-valued at y = πR (section 4), so no localised B anomaly can arise at all.

There are no chiral zero modes, so **no fixed-point anomaly needs cancelling**. This is our own application of the [11] formalism; the explicit inflow computation in [N4, N5] for this charge assignment has not been checked line by line †.

**Parity of the Chern–Simons terms (check O2).** With the parities above:

| 5D term | Z₂ parity of the density | consequence |
|---|---|---|
| B∧F_A∧F_A (gives θFF̃ for the photon) | **even** | constant coefficient allowed |
| A∧F_A∧F_A | odd | needs a jumping coefficient ε(y) [N3]; integrates to zero here |
| B∧F_B∧F_A | odd | same |

So the term that matters is consistent with a constant bulk level, and no fixed-point counterterm is required.

**The bare level.** The 4D condition from round 1 is that Θ_eff(θ + 2π) − Θ_eff(θ) ∈ 2πℤ in every allowed EM background. With the fermion giving π sgn M, this requires κ ∈ ℤ + ½ and hence k_eff = κ + ½ sgn M ∈ ℤ. This is the 5D parity-anomaly structure [10, 12, N6 †]. All 4D A_μ(x) backgrounds are allowed on the orbifold (A_μ is even), so the orbifold adds no new restriction, and the bare level is in ℤ + ½ as on the circle.

**New caveat: the global structure of the gauge group.** "Allowed EM backgrounds" depends on the minimal electric charge and the global form of the gauge group. The bulk photon is hypercharge, and brane quarks carry fractional charges.
- Reece [N1] shows that for G_SM = [SU(3)×SU(2)×U(1)]/Z₆, the photon coupling "can have a fractional part which is a multiple of 1/3, but which is determined by the gluon coupling". Choi, Forslund, Lam & Shao [N2] derive the general quantisation conditions for the quotients q = 1, 2, 3, 6.
- θ couples to no gluons, because ψ is colourless. Applying [N1] with zero gluon coupling (our application, and the normalisation map E ↔ k is to be checked †): **k_eff ∈ ℤ requires the Z₆ (minimal) global structure.**
- Without the quotient, flux quanta are larger and the 4D condition alone permits fractional k_eff. Our rough estimate is multiples of 1/9 if the minimal EM charge is 1/3 †. In that case k = 1 would no longer be the unique discrete value near the data; k = 7/9 … 16/9 would all lie within 2σ of the joint measurement.

This is the "charge-quantisation" sensitivity of axion birefringence emphasised in [17, N7]. For STUR-ROLL it is an input that must be stated: **the integer ladder assumes the Z₆ global structure.**

## 4. Large gauge transformations and θ's period (check O3)

ξ(y) = ny/R is odd, winds 2πn around the covering circle, vanishes at y = 0, and equals nπ at y = πR. It is therefore an allowed orbifold gauge transformation, and **θ → θ + 2πn: the period of θ is still 2π.** Its value at y = πR is exp(iξ) = (−1)ⁿ, an element of the C-invariant subgroup {±1}.

A useful by-product: a brane mass mixing of a q_B = 1 field with an SM lepton is gauge-invariant at y = 0, where ξ = 0, but **forbidden at y = πR**, where it would pick up (−1)ⁿ. Section E's "brane mixing is allowed because the B gauge parameter vanishes at the fixed points" is true only for y = 0.

## 5. Conclusions

1. **k_eff on S¹/Z₂:** k_eff = κ + ½ sgn M, with κ ∈ ℤ + ½ and k_eff ∈ ℤ. This assumes the Z₆ global structure; otherwise the lattice can be finer. With κ = +½: k_eff = 1 for M &gt; 0 and 0 for M &lt; 0, as in R1.1.
2. **β = k α/2 with k = 1 survives**, and the relation β = k_eff α/2 is exact. No exponentially small correction is needed.
3. **Δθ = π survives.** The covering-circle fermion, periodic and with the same twist as the scalar, gives V ∝ +cos θ; the scalar gives −cos θ [18, 20]. Their minima differ by exactly π. A different B charge or twist for the scalar would change Δθ. That is an assumption to state, not a derived fact.
4. **The matter content needs pairs.** Each B-charged bulk field must come as a Z₂ pair, or as a C-projected field if it is EM-neutral. For ψ the pair is mandatory, and its bulk masses are forced to be (M, −M).
