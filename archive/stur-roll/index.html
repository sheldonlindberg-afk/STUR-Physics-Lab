A falsifiable prediction for the LiteBIRD era

Light from the Big Bang, turned by half of α

|β| = kα/2 = 0.2091°k = 1 · no fitted parameter · computed 0.2080°

The oldest light in the universe arrives slightly polarized, and recent measurements suggest its polarization has been turned by about a fifth of a degree. STUR-ROLL says that turn is not an accident: a hidden field rolled exactly halfway around a circle, and half a turn rotates light by exactly half of nature's fine-structure constant.

STUR-ROLL adds one sector to ΛCDM: the holonomy θ of a Z₂-odd U(1) on an S¹/Z₂ orbifold, coupled to photons by a 5D Chern–Simons term at integer level k. A smooth birth fixes θ = 0; a parity-fixed brane twist makes it a hilltop; the roll θ: 0 → π after recombination gives β = kα/2 exactly.

The idea

Four steps from the birth of the universe to a number

Each step is either an assumption, stated openly, or a result that follows from the one before. Switch to Technical at the top for the equations.

Axiom A1 plus the derived chain of HYPOTHESIS.md. Each step cites the refinement round that established it.

assumption

A smooth beginning

The universe starts out smoothly, with a tiny fifth dimension that closes off neatly. Nothing can be twisted at that moment, so the hidden field starts at exactly zero, at the bottom of a valley.

Axiom A1: nucleation through a geometry in which S¹/Z₂ caps off. Holonomies around the contractible cycle are trivial, θ = ϑ = 0. With bosons periodic and fermions antiperiodic, Z(θ) = Tr e−LHeiθQ has positive weights, so E(θ) ≥ E(0) to all orders (R5).

derived

The coupling comes in whole numbers

How strongly the field turns light can only come in whole-number steps, like electric charge. The smallest step means: half a turn of the field rotates light by exactly half the fine-structure constant, α/2.

The 5D Chern–Simons term gives L = (kα/4π)θFF̃ with k = κ + ½ sgn M ∈ ℤ (parity anomaly, Z₆ structure). The fermion tower gives Φ(π) − Φ(0) = (π/2) sgn M exactly, so Δθ = π yields β = kα/2 (R1.1, R2.2, R3.2).

derived

A switch flips, and the valley becomes a hilltop

A switch on the edge of the extra dimension flips. Symmetry forces it to land exactly fully flipped. That changes the landscape: the field's valley floor becomes a hilltop, and the field is still sitting on top.

An orbifold-odd brane angle ϑ (coupling ϑJy) has parity-fixed extrema at exactly 0 and π; it rolls to π for mϑ ≳ 2H. With qψ odd it is a fermion-parity defect: ψ becomes periodic and V = m²f²(1 + cos θ) (R6).

derived

The roll, after the light left

Between about 3 million and 1.4 billion years after the Big Bang, the field rolls off the hilltop and settles half a turn away. The CMB light had already left, so it arrives with the full turn: 0.2091°.

For 2×10−31 ≲ m ≲ 10−28 eV the roll occurs at z ≈ 4–300, after recombination: βrec = 0.2081–0.2094° for s₀ = 10−4–10−3 (R2.1, R6).

Inputs, conditions, and what STUR-ROLL does not claim

Discrete choices (no dials): level k = 1; the brane potential's minimum at π; brane charges qψ odd, qφ even; the Z₆ global structure.

Conditions: 2×10−31 ≲ m ≲ 10−28 eV; 0.9 TeV ≲ 1/L < f/197; Hnuc ≲ 0.076 f; mϑ ≳ 2Hnuc.

Not claimed: STUR-ROLL is not a theory of everything. Gravity is general relativity; dark matter and dark energy remain those of ΛCDM. The new sector holds less than 2×10−5 of the matter energy.

Prior art: the same observable, β ≈ 0.21cγ°, comes from the hilltop axion of Lee, Murai, Takahashi & Yin (PRD 112, 043538, 2025). STUR-ROLL derives both of their assumptions (cγ = 1 and the hilltop start) from a 5D theory.

The fingerprint

Big patches of sky are rotated less

The CMB's polarization was imprinted twice: when the CMB was released (fine patterns on the sky) and again when the first stars lit up the universe (sky-wide patterns). By the second time, the field had already rolled, so the sky-wide patterns are rotated less. That is why the angle depends on the size of the pattern.

β(ℓ) from the CLASS E-mode source (seed 10−4, zre = 7.69). For ℓ ≲ 10 the reionization source dominates and β is reduced; recombination modes take over from ℓ ~ 6 and β reaches the α/2 plateau by ℓ ~ 20. A generic ALP with free mass and amplitude can mimic each curve to rms 0.0003–0.007°, so the shape alone does not discriminate; the amplitude and the tomographic difference do.

Calibration-free checkAn instrument that is slightly mis-rotated turns every pattern by the same amount, so the difference between big and small patterns ignores that error. STUR-ROLL says the difference can never exceed 0.209°.A miscalibration adds a constant to every ℓ, so β(2) − β(30) is immune; STUR-ROLL bounds |β(low ℓ) − β(high ℓ)| ≤ α/2.

The sharpest test

Early light versus late light

LiteBIRD can measure the rotation of "late" light (from when the first stars formed) separately from "early" light (from the CMB release). STUR-ROLL says the late light is turned much less, so the difference is large and negative. An instrument error, or a field that rolled only recently, would make the difference zero.

Δβ = βreio − βrec. A fraction f of the reionization shell may lie in an opposite-sign domain, where photons are rotated by α − βown; then βreio = (1 − f)βown + f(α − βown) (R7 A2). LiteBIRD: σ(Δβ) ≈ 0.05°.

Usually small (below 0.085 after a long inflation). Near 0.5 the difference vanishes, but the sky would then look patchy, which can be checked.Popp ≤ 0.085 for Nextra ≥ 60 (R2.4). f ≈ 0.5 implies a large-scale anisotropic rotation, rms ≈ 0.2°.

The caveat that matters most Measuring this angle requires knowing exactly which way the telescope's detectors point, and that calibration leans on our own galaxy's dust. The teams call the signal a hint. If it turns out to be a calibration error, the test disappears. β measurements calibrate the polarization angle on Galactic foregrounds; the ACT and ACT+Planck authors describe the signal as a hint with systematics to be understood. Tomography (Δβ) is the calibration-independent check.

If it is right

What it would mean for physics

Confirmation would not overturn Einstein or the standard model of cosmology. It would add something new and strange on top of them, and it would tell us something about how the universe began.

Separating what the data alone would establish from what is specific to STUR-ROLL. Full discussion: IMPLICATIONS.md.

Something invisible fills the universe and affects only light's polarization.

Key terms and common questions

Fine-structure constant α
The strength of the interaction between light and charge, about 1/137. Half of it, in degrees, is 0.2091°.
Birefringence β
A rotation of light's polarization on its way to us.
CMB
The afterglow of the Big Bang, released about 380,000 years after it.
Multipole ℓ
Angular size on the sky: small ℓ is a big patch, large ℓ a fine pattern.
Redshift z
How much the universe has expanded since light set out. Larger z means earlier.
Axion
A hypothetical, extremely light particle that can rotate polarization.
Orbifold, brane
A tiny extra dimension folded in half; its edges, where ordinary particles live.
LiteBIRD
A Japanese-led satellite that will map the CMB's polarization across the whole sky.
Is this a "theory of everything"?
No. It adds one small sector to the standard model of cosmology to explain one measurement. Gravity, dark matter and dark energy are left as they are.
Is the 0.2091° really not fitted?
It is α/2, fixed by a whole number (k = 1) and by the field rolling exactly half a turn. The field's mass only affects whether the roll finishes in time, which shifts the result by under 3% across the allowed range.
Has anyone predicted this number before?
Yes: Lee, Murai, Takahashi & Yin (2025) obtained β ≈ 0.21° from a hilltop axion. STUR-ROLL's contribution is to derive their two assumptions from a five-dimensional theory.
What if the measurement is a calibration error?
Then the test disappears: STUR-ROLL would be unmotivated but not refuted. The tomographic test (early versus late light) is immune to that error.
Does it explain dark matter or dark energy?
No, and this was tested rather than assumed. Before calculating, the two most natural extensions were written down with pass/fail rules. A heavy particle made at the end of inflation either leaves a forbidden pattern in the CMB or is made in amounts at least a hundred times too small. A second hidden field can mimic dark energy but breaks a widely trusted rule of quantum gravity. Both are documented in round 8.
Does it predict particle masses or mixing angles?
No. The old "theory of everything" version claimed to, but every claim failed a check fixed in advance (archive triage). Round 9 tested whether STUR-ROLL itself can predict the Cabibbo angle: its symmetries cannot tell the quark generations apart, and letting quarks spread into the extra dimension gives an angle anywhere from about 0.15 to 0.6. Consistent with the measured 0.225, but not a prediction (round 9).
How was the math checked?
Nine refinement rounds, each with its own script. Round 7 recomputed every number, including an independent re-integration, and corrected seven statements. Round 8 tested dark-matter and dark-energy extensions against preregistered criteria. This page's simulator is tested against the Python code in CI.
