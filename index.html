What is STUR?

STUR is a physics framework that tries to answer a big question: Where do all the numbers in physics come from?

Right now, physicists have to measure things like the mass of an electron or how strong gravity is. These numbers are just plugged into equations — nobody knows why they have those exact values.

STUR proposes that if you start with just one number (the Planck mass — nature's fundamental scale), everything else can be calculated, not measured.

The Big Idea

Imagine the universe is like a spiral staircase with exactly 3 steps that repeat. This "helix" shape isn't random — it's the simplest structure that works mathematically.

Why are there exactly 3 types of electrons (electron, muon, tau)? Why does the Higgs boson weigh what it does? STUR calculates these from geometry instead of measuring them.

How is this different?

Standard Physics

Why that number? Unknown — we measured it at the LHC.

"The Higgs mass is ~125 GeV."
Calculated from how the Higgs connects to gravity in the spiral.

Can we test it?

Yes! A good theory makes predictions that could prove it wrong. STUR makes 21 specific predictions that experiments can check.

For example, STUR predicts a specific pattern in how atoms lose quantum behavior over distance. Experiments like MAGIS-100 and AION (giant atom interferometers) are being built right now that could test this.

If the experiments don't match the predictions, STUR is wrong. That's how science works — and that's what makes STUR a real scientific theory, not just an idea.
