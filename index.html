1 · Teleparallel gravity

The same gravity as Einstein's, told a different way

In one line: space stays flat, every point carries its own rulers and clock, and gravity is how those rulers are twisted relative to their neighbours.

The famous picture. In 1915 Einstein described gravity as the shape of spacetime. A mass bends it, and everything moving nearby follows the bends. That's the rubber-sheet picture most of us grew up with.

The other picture. In 1928 Einstein explored a related idea he called "distant parallelism". Physicists later developed it into a theory that gives exactly the same predictions as his 1915 theory. It's called teleparallel gravity, or TEGR for short. In TEGR, space is never bent. Instead, every point in space carries its own little set of measuring sticks and a clock. Far from any mass, all those sets line up perfectly with one another. Near a mass, neighbouring sets stop lining up. That mismatch is called torsion, and it's what we feel as gravity.

Torsion behaves like a force field on a fixed, flat stage, much like the electric field around a charged object. That resemblance to the other forces is why some physicists find this picture a useful starting point for thinking about gravity and quantum physics together.

Same answers as Einstein, to every decimal we can measure

As a check, we solved gravity's equations in the twist form for the Sun and the Earth, then traced planets, light rays and clocks through the result. Curved space is used nowhere in that calculation. These are textbook results; recomputing them shows the twist picture really is the same theory.

Light bending, leg by leg: Newton's gravity predicts only half the bending of starlight. In the twist picture you can see where the other half comes from. The "time" rulers give Newton's half, and the "space" rulers give the rest, which Newton's theory has no place for.

Why learn gravity this way?

It looks like the other forces

Electricity, magnetism and the nuclear forces are all fields on a fixed stage. In TEGR, gravity is written the same way.

A new view, not a fix

Writing gravity this way doesn't by itself make it easier to combine with quantum physics. The deep problems of quantum gravity are still there.

Nothing is given up

Black holes, orbits, light bending and GPS all come out exactly as in Einstein's curved picture. It's a different view of the same physics, not a rival theory.

Go deeper: the TEGR equations tetrad, Weitzenböck torsion, Schwarzschild

established TEGR (Aldrovandi & Pereira, Teleparallel Gravity, 2013). The dynamical field is the tetrad eaμ, with gμν = ηab eaμ ebν. The connection is the curvature-free Weitzenböck connection, so gravity is carried entirely by torsion:

Γρμν = eaρ ∂μeaν,   Tρμν = Γρνμ − Γρμν
Sgrav = (c⁴/16πG) ∫ e T d⁴x,   T = −R + (divergence)  // same field equations as GR

worked example The identity T = −R + divergence, verified symbolically, then spot-checked at random points in 200-digit arithmetic, where it vanishes to working precision. For a static spherical tetrad e⁰ = eAdt, e¹ = eBdr, the TEGR field equations give 2rA′ = e2B − 1 and 2rB′ = 1 − e2B, hence Schwarzschild, uniquely (Birkhoff). The weak field gives Ψ = Φ and ∇²Φ = 4πGρ. The classic tests in the table are numerical geodesic integrals of that tetrad, not textbook formulas.

2 · An open question: is gravity quantum?

Where is the gravity of something in two places at once?

In one line: nobody knows yet. Standard quantum theory suggests each version of the object carries its own gravity field; some physicists propose that gravity stays classical. Experiments are being designed to decide.

Quantum objects can be in a superposition: not here or there, but a combination of both, which physicists call two "branches". Atoms and large molecules have been put in superpositions in the lab.

So what does their gravity do? There are two broad possibilities:

A. Gravity follows each branch

Each version of the object has its own gravity field, just as it has its own electric field. Two such objects can then become entangled through gravity alone, linked in a way no classical force could produce.

What you expect if gravity is quantum, as in standard (perturbative) quantum gravity.

B. Gravity stays classical

There is a single, non-quantum gravity field. Then gravity can never entangle anything.

The idea behind "classical gravity" models, such as Oppenheim's.

What it costs gravity to stay classical

A classical gravity still has to respond to quantum matter. Work by Diósi, Tilloy and Oppenheim's group shows that this is possible only if gravity also adds a little random jitter. How much jitter is allowed is an active research question, and lab experiments are already putting limits on it.

worked example These notes work through one case. If the jitter is smeared over a very small distance and has no memory, it would heat every kilogram of matter by about 0.5 W, 6×10¹⁰ times the heat flowing out of the Earth's interior. Nothing like that is observed. Change either assumption and the conclusion changes, so treat this as an illustration of how such models are tested, not as a proof that gravity is quantum.

Gravity barely keeps records. On standard physics, splitting a tiny diamond into two places would send out about 10−59 gravitons. So gravity by itself keeps essentially no record of which branch happened, and wouldn't destroy the superposition on its own.

3 · A research idea: gravity and entanglement

Could gravity's strength come from the entanglement of empty space?

In one line: empty space is entangled across every surface. A serious but unproven idea says Einstein's equations, and gravity's strength, follow from that entanglement.

established Quantum fields fill all of space, even a perfect vacuum. Draw any imaginary surface: the field on one side is entangled with the field on the other, by an amount that grows with the surface's area. This "area law" is well known (Srednicki, 1993), and we recomputed it.

open idea In 1995 physicist Ted Jacobson showed something remarkable. Assume that ordinary heat-flow rules (thermodynamics) hold at every tiny horizon in spacetime, and Einstein's equations come out. Gravity's strength, Newton's constant G, is then tied to how much entanglement there is per unit area. We re-checked each step of his argument in code.

It's an influential idea, and it suggests gravity might not be a separate ingredient but something that emerges from quantum physics. But it rests on an assumption that hasn't been established, and physicists still debate it. It doesn't predict the value of G either: run backwards, the measured G only fixes a combination of unknowns.

Go deeper: area law, Unruh, Jacobson cs = 0.3046, G = πa²c³/(N csħ)

worked example Area law by Srednicki's method: S = 0.3046 (R/a)² per scalar field (literature 0.30). Modular Hamiltonian on a lattice: K = 2π × boost to 0.99997 at L = 60 (Bisognano–Wichmann), so an accelerated observer sees T = ħg/2πckB (the Unruh effect).

δQ = T δS on every local horizon  ⇒  Gμν + Λgμν = (8πG/c⁴) Tμν
1/(4Għ) = entanglement per area  ⇒  G = π a² c³ / (N cs ħ),   cs = 0.3046

Jacobson's algebra is re-derived symbolically: Raychaudhuri, the null-vector lemma and the contracted Bianchi identity. Its premise, local equilibrium on every local horizon, is assumed. The measured G fixes only a²/N, the cutoff a against the number of fields N. Caveats: cs depends on the regulator, and for gauge fields the area-law coefficient includes contact terms (Kabat 1995), so the identification is cleanest for scalars and fermions.

4 · What measurement really does

Records, not observers

In one line: interference disappears when the surroundings keep a record of which path was taken. Nobody has to look.

Send particles through two slits and you get stripes, an interference pattern. Put a detector at the slits and the stripes fade. The popular story says "observing changes reality". The physics is more down to earth: the stripes fade by exactly as much as the detector recorded which slit was used. The detector doesn't need to be read, switched on to a screen or noticed by anyone. A stray air molecule or photon carrying the information works just as well. Physicists call this decoherence.

Try it. Slide the detector strength and watch the fringe contrast and the path information trade off. Their squares always add up to exactly 1.

1.00fringe contrast (V)
0.00which-path record (D)

V² + D² = 1.000 at every setting.

still debated Decoherence explains why interference vanishes and why big objects look classical. It doesn't, by itself, say why we see one particular outcome rather than another. That is the "measurement problem", and the different interpretations of quantum mechanics answer it differently.

Why probabilities are what they are

Quantum chance follows one exact rule: probability = amplitude squared (the Born rule). Well-known arguments by Gleason (1957) and Zurek (2005) show it's the only consistent rule, given some reasonable assumptions, such as no faster-than-light messages. Whether those assumptions are fully innocent is still debated.

Branches, not split sources

An electron's Coulomb field is entangled with its position branch by branch; it is not sourced by the mean charge density. The semiclassical alternative, the electromagnetic analogue of the Schrödinger–Newton equation, lets the electron repel itself and binds hydrogen at 6.64 eV, against the measured 13.60 eV. The gravitational version of this question is what the λ experiments test.

A worked lab example

Fringe loss counts the spread in exchanged energy quanta, so heat destroys more of it. For a single trapped ion, contrast falls from 0.787 to 0.111 as its motion warms from 0.1 to 5 quanta.

Go deeper: visibility, the Born rule and the ion example V = |⟨E₁|E₂⟩|, −ln V = 2 Var(ΔN)

established V = |⟨E₁|E₂⟩| and the complementarity D² + V² = 1 (Englert 1996). In the demo, ⟨E₁|E₂⟩ = cos θ, with θ running from 0° to 90° across the slider. src/stur_measurement.py T3 gives D² + V² = 1.000000 at every angle.

worked example The Born rule by four published routes, each reproduced numerically: envariance with fine-graining (Zurek), Gleason-type additivity (only F(x) = x is additive, to 10−15), no-signalling (every other power signals), and branch frequencies. Caveat: envariance assumes probabilities exist and depend only on the state (Schlosshauer & Fine 2005). The additivity and no-signalling routes don't, but they assume non-contextuality and locality.

For researchers

Three colours and three generations from perturbativity in 3-3-1 models

Result (conditional): keep Nc general, take no generation or neutrino number from experiment, and require every gauge coupling to stay perturbative to the Planck scale. Only Nc = Ng = 3 survives, with quark charges ⅔ and −⅓.

The SU(3)L anomaly plus asymptotic freedom of colour force Nc to be odd and Ng = Nc, which makes Witten's global anomaly automatic. SU(3)L running then excludes Nc ≥ 7, and U(1)X running excludes Nc = 5. The selection holds at two loops with Yukawa terms, at three loops in the gauge couplings, with one-loop threshold matching across the heavy spectrum, for 3-3-1 scales of 2–30 TeV, and in both variants β = ±1/√3. It takes the measured electroweak couplings at the TeV scale as input; in the β = +1/√3 variant Nc = 3 itself reaches MPl (α = 1, three loops) only if the heavy charged leptons are heavier than about 0.56 TeV with the bileptons at their LHC bound, so there the selection is conditional on that mass. Requiring the scalar sector to stay perturbative to MPl bounds the exotic-quark masses below 1.4–1.8 times the bilepton mass.

conditional on the 3-3-1 framework and on Planck-scale perturbativity. The closest prior work is Doff & Pisano (2000), who find Ng = k·nc without fixing k or nc. An INSPIRE search found no earlier use of running to fix Nc; comments from 3-3-1 experts are welcome.

For students

The symmetry-breaking ladder: from 3-3-1 to the diamond experiment

What it shows: the forces don't turn into one another. At each threshold a symmetry breaks and the forces separate, down to the two that act between the diamonds: gravity, the signal, and electromagnetism, the background. Every step is derived and checked in code.

3-3-1 → Standard Model → electromagnetism. From the scalar vacuum values, the gauge-boson mass matrix gives one massless photon with charge Q = T3 + βT8 + X, the hypercharge matching condition, and the W, Z, Z′ and bilepton masses. The strong force. Colour is never broken; chiral symmetry is, in the linear sigma model (a model of QCD's low-energy structure): three pions, the Gell-Mann–Oakes–Renner relation (mπ = 127.6 MeV against 135.0 at leading order; 132.6 ± 3.8 MeV at O(p4) in chiral perturbation theory, QCD's own effective theory), and the nuclear force as one-pion exchange with range 1.41 fm. Electromagnetism stays unbroken, so between neutral diamonds what remains is the Casimir–Polder force, derived here from electrostatics and the dipole field.

Gravity is not part of the chain; it sits beside it, with Newton's G as its own input. One-graviton exchange gives Newton's law, twice the coupling for light, and the experiment's 2.19 rad entangling phase. MacDowell–Mansouri (1977) writes general relativity with a cosmological constant as a de Sitter gauge theory broken to Lorentz: an equivalent reformulation, not a unification. Inputs are measured values; why they take those values, and how gravity unifies with the other forces, is not derived.

Black holes are not another step down: nothing breaks at a horizon. They are gravity's strong-field end, and the first formula here with ħ, G, c and kB together. The Hawking temperature is fixed by the geometry (Euclidean time is smooth only with period 2π/κ), the first law holds in exact fractions, and the measured cosmological constant caps a black hole at 2×1022 solar masses. Against data: the predicted shadows of M87* and Sgr A* (37.8 and 52.3 μas) match the EHT rings (42 ± 3 and 51.8 ± 2.3 μas), and GW250114's horizon area grew from 237,000 to about 407,000 km², as Hawking's area law requires. Hawking radiation itself has never been measured.

Links across the rungs. Neutral matter: qp + qe is exactly the mixed SU(3)L (or SU(2)L) anomaly, for any number of colours. The diamond experiment needs it below 2.8×10−19 e per nucleon, which no discharging can arrange; measurement gives 10−21 e, and anomaly cancellation gives zero. π0 → γγ: rung 1's charges and rung 2's fπ give 7.79 eV, against PrimEx's 7.80 ± 0.12 eV. Neither selects Nc; the R-ratio does. The Planck mass: the cutoff the 3-3-1 selection runs to is √(ħc/G), with the same G as the diamonds' phase; the selection would survive G 8.4× weaker at the reference spectrum, 1.6× weaker at the worst point of the threshold scan.

For teaching

Common misconceptions, and the physics that answers them

Misconceptions students bring to the classroom, each answered by a result worked out on this page.

Consciousness, or "the observer", creates reality.
Interference is lost whenever the surroundings record which path was taken. A photon or an air molecule is enough, and no mind is involved (section 4). Mainstream physics gives consciousness no special role.
Entanglement lets you send messages instantly.
Entangled correlations appear at a distance, but they can't carry a message. This "no-signalling" rule is built into quantum theory and confirmed by experiment.
Your thoughts or intentions can change quantum outcomes.
The odds are fixed by amplitude squared, confirmed to many decimal places. No controlled experiment has found intention moving them.
"Quantum" healing, energy and wellness products.
Warm, large, wet objects like bodies are constantly recorded by their surroundings, so their quantum superpositions vanish almost instantly. "Quantum" on a label isn't physics.
Physicists have no idea how gravity and quantum physics fit together.
At everyday energies physicists routinely calculate small quantum effects of gravity. What's untested is whether gravity really is quantum, which is what the diamond experiments aim to check (section 2). The hard open problems are at tiny scales, like black-hole interiors and the Big Bang.
Einstein said space is curved, so a "twist" picture must be wrong.
They are two ways of writing the same theory, with identical predictions. Einstein explored a closely related idea himself in 1928 (section 1).
The quantum probability rule is arbitrary.
It's tightly constrained. Gleason's theorem and related arguments show that, given reasonable assumptions, amplitude squared is the only consistent rule (section 4).

How sure is each piece?

Every claim carries a label

Trust comes from saying exactly what is established, what is a worked example, and what is still open. This is independent work and has not been peer reviewed. Where it goes beyond textbook physics, it says so.

established

Published and tested: gravity as torsion (TEGR, equivalent to Einstein's theory), records erasing interference (decoherence), the entanglement area law, the Unruh effect.

worked example

Calculations done here to illustrate or check known physics. The numbers on this page come from 25 open scripts and are pinned by automated tests that run on every change.

under stated assumptions

The argument against one class of classical-gravity models (section 2). Hawking radiation: it follows from quantum fields on a classical background, but no black hole's radiation has been seen. Each holds only under the assumptions stated next to it.

open

Whether gravity is quantum: the diamond experiments haven't been done. Whether gravity emerges from entanglement (section 3). Why we see one outcome (section 4). Whether black holes destroy information.
