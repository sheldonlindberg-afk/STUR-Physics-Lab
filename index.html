Cosmic Birefringence from Integers

|β| = kα/2 = 0.2091°k = 1 (minimal level) · no continuous parameter fitted

A holonomy θ on a compact fifth dimension couples to light through a Chern–Simons term at integer level k. As the universe cools, θ = 0 becomes a hilltop, θ rolls to π, and the polarization of the CMB rotates by exactly kα/2.

The scope is narrow on purpose: Lambda-CDM plus one derived sector, not a replacement. Gravity is TEGR, which is GR. The new sector is invisible everywhere except in β, and LiteBIRD decides.

Falsification Criteria
|β| moves away from 0.209°
LiteBIRD
The amplitude is fixed at α/2 with no free parameter to absorb a different value.
|β(low ℓ) − β(high ℓ)| > 0.209°
LiteBIRD
This test is calibration-free: a miscalibration rotates every ℓ equally. STUR-ROLL bounds the difference by α/2; a generic ALP does not.
No single θ mass fits β(ℓ)
One mass sets the whole multipole pattern, from the ℓ = 2–10 reionization bump to the ℓ ≥ 30 plateau.
Anisotropic birefringence at high ℓ
θ = 0 at every emission point, so the recombination-era rotation is exactly uniform. An ALP with initial-angle fluctuations would rotate patchily.
→
Caveat: Angle Calibration
The β measurements rely on calibrating the polarization angle against Galactic foregrounds, and the ACT+Planck authors call the signal a hint whose systematics need to be understood. If it is a systematic, the test disappears. The shape of β(ℓ) on its own does not discriminate either: a generic ALP with a free mass and a free amplitude reproduces it to an rms of 0.0003–0.007°. What can discriminate is the amplitude, the calibration-free bound, and uniformity at high ℓ.
