Which of nature's numbers can be calculated from the others?

One Python simulation, built only from established physics, with nothing tuned and nothing fitted. It starts from the measured constants of nature, calculates everything it can, and states plainly which numbers it can derive and which it can't.

What the simulation is

production_v2_2.py is a single, self-contained program. It starts from about thirty measured or defined constants: particle masses, coupling strengths, the CMB temperature, Newton's constant and others, each with its uncertainty and published source. It then calculates forward, using only physics that is established and published:

  • the Friedmann equation for the expansion of the universe;
  • the renormalization group for how particle masses and forces change with energy, with every coefficient computed from group theory;
  • exact linear algebra for how quarks mix;
  • semiclassical gravity (Hawking, Bekenstein, Unruh, and gravity as a quantum effective field theory) where quantum theory and gravity meet.

Every scale it uses comes out of its own earlier steps: the top-quark mass scale, the unification scale, the Planck mass. Nothing is typed in to make an answer come out right.

What it is attempting

It is a theory-of-everything workshop. A final theory would calculate the constants of nature instead of measuring them. Nobody has one. This simulation builds as far toward it as established physics reaches, then reports where it stops. For each number it asks:

  1. Can it be calculated from the other numbers? If so, it does the calculation and compares the result with measurement.
  2. If not, what would it take? It tests the established ideas that would relate it to others: grand unification, texture zeros, flavour symmetries, fixed points. It shows exactly where each one fails or what it would require.
  3. Do patterns emerge? Famous large-number "coincidences" are computed and labelled. Each one is either an exact identity, a known mechanism, a measured input, or open, so patterns don't turn into numerology.

Negative results count. A clean "this cannot be calculated within these frameworks", backed by an exhaustive search, narrows down where a deeper theory has to do its work.

The rule: nothing tuned, nothing fitted

Inputs

Measured or defined constants, each with an uncertainty and a source (PDG 2024, Planck 2018, CODATA 2018, SI, IAU). They are the only physics numbers that enter a calculation.

Comparisons

Measured results (the quark-mixing matrix, the age of the universe), used only to judge the output, never inside a calculation.

Unknowns

Quantities nothing can fix (a phase, a superpartner mass, tan β) are never given a value. They are scanned over their whole range, and the prediction is the band that results.

Enforced

The repository's test fails if any non-integer number appears in the script outside the input, comparison and numerical-setting tables. The output ends with 37 self-checks of the calculation itself.

Patterns that emerged

  • identity Gravity's strength for a proton is exactly (mp/MPlanck)². "Why is gravity weak?" becomes "why is the proton light?"
  • identity The de Sitter entropy (10122) times the dark-energy-to-Planck density ratio (10−123) is exactly 3/8. The two famous huge numbers are one fact.
  • mechanism The proton is 1019 times lighter than the Planck mass because the strong force strengthens only logarithmically as energy falls: ln(MP/ΛQCD) = 45.
  • coincidence The smallest mass gravity can entangle within the age of the universe (89 MeV/c²) is 0.64 pion masses. It is the same combination of ħ, G, c and the Hubble time as Weinberg's 1972 large-number relation. They agree in order of magnitude, and no mechanism is known.
  • pointer In supersymmetry, two independent relations (b–τ unification and the top-quark fixed point) land on the same tan β ≈ 1.35. That pins one number, not the masses, and is not checked against the Higgs mass.
  • open Still not calculated by anything: α, the Yukawa couplings, the cosmological constant, and whether gravity is quantum. The last is a measurement waiting to be made: a classical gravitational field cannot entangle masses at all.
