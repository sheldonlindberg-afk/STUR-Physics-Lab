STUR Physics Lab

One simulation. Nothing tuned.

One Python file that uses only the standard library. Every number it prints is calculated from measured inputs. Measured results are used only to judge the output, never inside a calculation. Where the inputs cannot fix a quantity, the script scans its whole range instead of choosing a value.

Download .py View on GitHub

Paste it into any Python 3.8+ runner, notebook or AI assistant's code tool. It runs in about 3 minutes and ends with 32 self-checks. Expected output: results/production_v2_2.txt.

The rule

  • INPUTS: measured or defined constants, each with an uncertainty and a source (PDG 2024, Planck 2018, CODATA, SI).
  • COMPARISONS: measured results, used only to judge the output.
  • NUMERICS: step counts, grid sizes and precision. The self-checks vary them and show the results do not move.
  • Everything else is calculated. The repository's test fails if any non-integer number appears in the script outside those three tables.

Part B: quark mixing from quark masses

The six measured quark masses are run with the renormalization group up to m_t(m_t) and to the superpartner scale (2.54 TeV) that Part I derives. Every coefficient is computed from group theory. The masses fix every entry of the Fritzsch six-zero texture, and diagonalizing both sectors exactly gives the CKM matrix. The texture's two phases are fixed by nothing, so each prediction is the band swept out over all of their values.

Part C: every texture pattern that makes a prediction

The Fritzsch pattern is only one guess about where the zeros in the quark mass matrices sit. The script tests every guess that makes a prediction: all pairs of Hermitian mass matrices with five or six zeros, 1,000 pairs in 188 classes. The masses fix each sector's entries exactly. Every unknown (the phases, and one free entry in five-zero sectors) is scanned or tested, never set.

Part D: what 3-3-1 says about the mass matrices

From the 3-3-1 charges (β = ±1/√3), the script finds every gauge-invariant Yukawa coupling. Every entry of both mass matrices is allowed, so 3-3-1 by itself forces no zero: it can neither rescue nor select a texture. What it does fix is that one family (a triplet) differs from the other two. That turns the surviving pattern into three distinct 3-3-1 hypotheses, which differ in which family is the triplet. Their flavour-changing Z′ couplings would tell them apart.

Part E: can a flavour symmetry make the survivor inside 3-3-1?

  • Minimal scalars: no. An exhaustive scan of Z2 to Z6 (50,364 pattern pairs) finds no texture zero that keeps all three families mixing. The reason is structural: every row's charge shifts by the same amount between the up and down sectors.
  • Hermitian matrices: impossible. That would need a symmetry exchanging left-handed quarks (SU(3)L triplets) with right-handed ones (singlets).
  • Extra scalars: the zeros, but no prediction. Z6 with 6 scalar triplets gives exactly the survivor's zeros. As general matrices, though, they have 14 parameters for 10 observables: −4 predictions.

Result: no flavour symmetry inside 3-3-1 turns the survivor into a predictive model. Its prediction needs left-right symmetric gauge structure, which is outside 3-3-1.

Part F: does any zero pattern predict the CKM matrix?

Inside 3-3-1 a symmetry can only make zeros in general mass matrices, so the script checks every such pattern: 61,009 pairs in 497 classes. It counts each pattern's physical parameters exactly, from the rank of 10 invariants computed in integer arithmetic. Of the 472 classes that mix all three families and have a phase, 470 have exactly 10 parameters for 10 observables, so they predict nothing. The other two cannot violate CP. No pattern predicts anything.

Part G: other gauge frameworks

The same question for the Standard Model and for left-right symmetric models. In the Standard Model, symmetries make zeros in general matrices, and none predicts anything (Part F). In left-right models the matrices are Hermitian, but the zeros must sit in the same places for up and down quarks. All 3 such patterns that predict anything are excluded, at 31σ or more.

Closure: in the Standard Model, in left-right models and in 3-3-1, the quark sector closes with 10 measured inputs: the 6 masses and the 4 CKM parameters. Texture zeros calculate none of the CKM numbers. That would need relations between couplings (non-abelian flavour symmetries or grand unification).

Part H: where quantum theory and gravity meet

A workshop built only from established physics, with every pattern labelled. From G, ħ, c and kB plus measured masses, the script computes:

  • the Planck units;
  • the one-loop quantum correction to Newton's law;
  • Hawking temperatures, including the black hole that evaporates in exactly the age of the universe (1.7×1011 kg);
  • the de Sitter horizon (3.3×10122 kB) and the Unruh effect;
  • the entanglement that gravity would create between two masses in superposition. If gravity is classical, it is zero. Measuring it is the open test.

Patterns: two famous large-number coincidences are exact identities. One is αG = (mp/MP)². The other is SdS·ρΛ/ρPlanck = 3/8: the 10122 entropy and the 10−123 vacuum energy are one fact. The weakness of gravity for a proton comes from logarithmic running of the strong force. Still open: α, the Yukawa couplings, ρΛ, and whether gravity carries entanglement.

Part I: can α be calculated from the other numbers?

No accepted theory derives α. Grand unification relates it to the strong coupling and the weak mixing angle: if all three forces meet at one energy, α follows. With Standard Model particles alone the forces don't meet: the required 1/α is 81.8, against a measured 127.95. With supersymmetry the answer depends on the unknown superpartner mass, so α is not predicted. Read backwards, the measured α points to superpartners near 2.5 TeV. The closed formulas proposed for α (Eddington, Wyler and others) are all excluded by thousands of standard deviations or more.

Part J: can the Yukawa couplings be calculated from the other numbers?

Grand unification predicts quark-to-lepton mass ratios. Minimal SU(5)'s ms/md = mμ/me is off by 558σ. The Georgi–Jarlskog version, ms/md = mμ/(9me), is off by 10σ. Bottom–tau unification fails in the Standard Model. In supersymmetry, using the scales from Part I, it works only at tan β = 1.35. A large top coupling running down predicts a 217–220 GeV top in the Standard Model, against 163 GeV measured. In supersymmetry it needs tan β = 1.33–1.40, the same narrow region. No Yukawa coupling is calculated. Every scale used comes from the algorithm itself.
