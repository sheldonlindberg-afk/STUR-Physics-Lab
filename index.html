Quantum Gravity Algorithm

ckm_from_331_overlaps.py computes quark masses and the CKM matrix in a 3-3-1 model with no fitted inputs. Every field's profile on an internal coordinate comes from its quantum numbers. Those profiles are solved together with the U(1)X background that all the fermions source, and the loop repeats until the background stops changing. Yukawa couplings are then overlap integrals of the converged profiles.

The only external numbers are measured, and none is adjusted toward quark data: α and sin²θW at MZ (which fix the gauge couplings), the Higgs vev v = 246 GeV, and the 3-3-1 scale, which only sets the exotic-quark masses. It runs in about two seconds and ends with seven PASS/FAIL checks. Independent work, not peer reviewed.

How it works ↓

1 · The idea

Masses and mixing as overlaps

In one line: if each quark is a wave along a hidden coordinate y, its coupling to the Higgs-like fields is how much the waves overlap. Small overlaps would give small masses and small mixings.

The Standard Model leaves the quark masses and the four CKM parameters as free numbers. Overlap models try to explain them: fermions get profiles along an internal coordinate y, and a Yukawa coupling is the integral of a left-handed profile, a scalar profile and a right-handed profile.

Usually those profile positions are chosen to fit the data. This script doesn't choose them. In the 3-3-1 model, SU(3)C × SU(3)L × U(1)X, every field has fixed quantum numbers, and the script derives each profile from them alone. Fields with identical quantum numbers are not separate bumps placed by hand. They are the successive orthogonal modes (0, 1, 2, …) of a single operator. That is how generations arise here.

2 · The algorithm

Iterate until it locks in

  1. Couplings from measurement. gL = √(4πα)/sin θW, and for β = −1/√3, gX²/gL² = s²/(1 − 4s²/3). This gives gL = 0.652 and gX = 0.377.
  2. Modes. For each field species f, solve Hf = −d²/dy² + y² + gX Xf A(y) and keep as many of its lowest modes as the model has copies of f. Qa (two antitriplets) keeps 2; the right-handed up type (u, c, t and the exotic U) keeps 4; the right-handed down type (d, s, b, D1, D2) keeps 5. The y² term is the localizing background and sets the unit of length.
  3. Background. Every fermion field in the model, leptons and exotic quarks included, sources the U(1)X background: −A″ = gX Σf nf Xf |ψf|², counting colour and SU(3)L components.
  4. Repeat. Mix the new A half-and-half with the old one and go back to step 2. Stop when A changes by less than 10⁻¹².
  5. Masses and CKM. Mass-matrix entries are v × ∫ ψL Φ ψR dy, using the converged profiles and scalar modes. Diagonalize M M† for the 4 × 4 up and 5 × 5 down systems, keep the three light states, and form V from the rows that the W boson connects.
Go deeper: numerics solvers, checks

The modes come from a finite-difference operator on 401 points, y ∈ [−8, 8]. Eigenvalues come from Sturm-sequence bisection and modes from inverse iteration, with explicit orthogonalization against lower modes. The background equation is a tridiagonal (Thomas) solve. The small mass matrices are diagonalized with a complex Jacobi solver. The repository's tests check every solver against numpy.

3 · The 3-3-1 structure

Which scalar each quark couples through

The model is the β = −1/√3 version. Each row of the mass matrix pairs a left-handed component with the scalar whose vev sits in that component, and the script asserts that U(1)X is conserved (−XL + XΦ + XR = 0) in every term.

Q1, Q2 = (d, −u, D)3̄, 0ρ* (light u)η* (light d), χ* (exotic D)

vη = vρ = v/√2, because nothing in the model prefers one over the other. Raising the exotic scale w fourfold changes |V| by only 2×10⁻³, which the script checks.

Why it fails, and why that's informative

Mirror symmetry survives. The fermion content has zero total X charge, and the localizing background is even in y, so the self-consistent background is even too. The script started from a lopsided background and watched it decay back to the even one. With an even background, every mode is either even or odd, and any overlap of odd total parity is exactly zero. That kills the off-diagonal entries that mixing needs and leaves some quarks massless. Mixing among massless states isn't even defined, so those |V| entries are basis artefacts.

No source of CP violation. Every profile is real, so every Yukawa is real and J = 0 exactly. The minimal 3-3-1 field content has no complex number of its own to supply.

So the calculation shows what is missing: something that breaks y → −y (for example, chirality-dependent coupling to a kink, as in domain-wall fermions) and a complex phase. Each would have to be derived, not chosen, to keep the rule that nothing is fitted.

6 · What's missing

Ingredients that would still have to be derived

Breaking y → −y

In domain-wall models, left- and right-handed zero modes come from fields with opposite-sign couplings to the wall and can sit at different places. A derived chirality-dependent shift would turn on the overlaps that parity now forces to zero.

A CP phase

With real profiles, J = 0. A nonzero J needs a complex phase that the model itself produces, such as a spontaneous phase between scalar vevs.

vη/vρ and running

The vev ratio is set equal because nothing fixes it yet. Yukawas are compared at one scale, without renormalization-group running.

Changelog from the uploaded file to this version

Uploaded as production_v2_2.py. Its complex Jacobi eigensolver had inconsistent signs and phases. That is now fixed and agrees with numpy to 10⁻¹⁵. An earlier demo chose nine profile centres to match the measured |V| values. It was removed because nothing may be fitted, and replaced with the self-consistent loop. The original overlap and selection functions are still in the file as a library.
