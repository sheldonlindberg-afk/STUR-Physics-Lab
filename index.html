Quark mixing from where quarks sit.

ckm_from_331_overlaps.py builds the quark Yukawa matrices of a 3-3-1 model from overlap integrals of localized wavefunctions on an extra internal coordinate, lets the model's U(1)X charges decide which couplings exist, then diagonalizes the result to get the CKM matrix and the Jarlskog invariant J. No mixing angle is typed in.

The file runs as pasted in any Python 3.8+ runner, notebook or AI assistant's code tool, in well under a second, and ends with seven PASS/FAIL checks on its own numerics. Independent work, not peer reviewed.

How it works ↓

1 · The idea

Masses and mixing as overlaps

In one line: if each quark is a bump at its own place along a hidden coordinate y, its coupling to the Higgs-like field is how much the bumps overlap, and small overlaps give small masses and small mixings.

The Standard Model leaves the quark masses and the four CKM parameters as free numbers, and those numbers are strongly hierarchical. A common way to make hierarchies natural is to give the fermions profiles along an internal coordinate y (as in split-fermion and extra-dimension models). A Yukawa coupling is then the integral of a left-handed profile, a scalar profile and a right-handed profile, and Gaussians that sit apart overlap exponentially little.

This script puts that mechanism inside a 3-3-1 model, SU(3)C × SU(3)L × U(1)X, where the three quark generations are not copies of each other: two left-handed quark families sit in the antitriplet and the third in the triplet, with different X charges. So the charge rule alone decides which scalar each generation can couple to.

2 · What the script computes

Five steps, each a function you can call

  1. Profiles. solve_localized_wavefunction integrates d(ln ψ)/dy = −(y − y0)/w² to a Gaussian, normalizes it on the grid, and reports its measured centre and RMS width.
  2. Scalar backgrounds. build_scalar_profile gives each scalar channel a Gaussian amplitude and a y-dependent phase (a linear slope plus a small oscillation). These phases are the only source of CP violation.
  3. Selection rule. is_331_coupling_allowed keeps ψ̄L Φ ψR only if −XL + XΦ + XR = 0 and the SU(3)L representations can contract.
  4. Yukawa matrices. build_derived_yukawa_331 sums, over the allowed scalars, the overlap ∫ ψL* Φ ψR dy (trapezoid rule) times a Clebsch–Gordan weight. The demo sets every weight to 1.
  5. CKM. diagonalize_ckm_from_yukawas diagonalizes H = Y Y† for each sector with a complex Jacobi solver, forms V = Uu† Ud, and returns |Vij| and J = Im(Vus Vcb Vub* Vcs*).
Go deeper: the overlap formula Yukawa entry, Jarlskog invariant
Yij = ΣΦ allowed cijΦ ∫ dy ψL,i(y)* Φ(y) ψR,j(y)
Φ(y) = v e−(y−yΦ)²/2σ² ei(k y + a sin ω y)
H = Y Y† = U diag(m²) U†,   V = Uu† Ud

Rows of V are ordered by ascending mass in each sector, so Vus is V[0][1]. J is invariant under rephasing the quark fields, so it is the physical measure of CP violation; it flips sign if every scalar phase is conjugated.

3 · The 3-3-1 selection rule

The charges choose the scalar for each generation

The demo uses the β = −1/√3 assignment. Right-handed quarks are SU(3)L singlets with uR: X = +⅔ and dR: X = −⅓. Imposing −XL + XΦ + XR = 0 gives:

Left-handed quarksSU(3)L, XUp sector couples viaDown sector couples via
Q1, Q23̄, 0ρ* (X = −⅔)η* (X = +⅓)
Q33, +⅓η (X = −⅓)ρ (X = +⅔)

With two scalar channels offered to each sector, every one of the 9 entries is allowed by exactly one of them and forbidden by the other, so the script reports 9 allowed and 9 forbidden couplings per sector. The third generation feels a different scalar profile from the first two, which is where the 3-3-1 structure enters the mixing.

4 · The demo result

What comes out, and what was put in

The nine localization centres were chosen (by a Nelder–Mead search) so that |Vus|, |Vcb| and |Vub| match their measured values. Widths, scalar profiles, phases and Clebsch–Gordan weights were fixed by hand beforehand. Nine knobs for three targets is underdetermined, so the matched magnitudes show that the mechanism can carry the hierarchy, not that it predicts it. The rest is output.

6 · Limits

What this is not

Not a prediction

The |V| magnitudes are matched by choice of inputs. A prediction would need the centres to come from somewhere else, such as a potential that the 3-3-1 fields themselves generate.

A loose group check

The SU(3)L test only asks whether the representations can contract; it doesn't use the scalar's representation or compute real Clebsch–Gordan coefficients, which the demo sets to 1.

No running

Yukawas are computed at one scale and compared with low-energy data. The first-generation masses come out below what double precision can resolve.

Changelog a fix to the uploaded eigensolver

As first uploaded (production_v2_2.py), the Jacobi rotation used inconsistent signs and phases: on random Hermitian matrices its eigenvalues disagreed with numpy, and U diag U† did not rebuild H. The rotation now removes the phase of apq first and then applies the standard real Jacobi step; eigenvalues and eigenvectors agree with numpy to 10⁻¹⁵. The rest of the physics code is unchanged; the demo and self-checks were added.
